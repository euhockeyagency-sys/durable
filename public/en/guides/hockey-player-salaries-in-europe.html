Hockey player salaries in Europe: what's actually published

Almost no league publishes individual salaries. This page collects what genuinely is public — official union figures, government-mandated minimums, and dated third-party estimates clearly marked as such — and says plainly where nothing reliable could be found.

Reviewed by Viachaslau Marshchonak, hockey agent working with European markets since 2016. Every figure below is sourced and dated; nothing here is an EHA estimate unless stated. Confirm current pay directly with a club before treating any number as an offer benchmark.

Why there is no single reliable salary table

Individual hockey contracts are private almost everywhere. Even where a players' union has negotiated a collective agreement, that agreement typically sets a floor, not the actual pay of any specific player — Eurofound, the EU's own research agency, documents that Sweden's first professional-hockey collective agreement (signed 28 October 1999, covering roughly 250 elite players) established negotiated, per-player pay above a floor, not disclosed individual salaries. That pattern has held ever since across most of Europe.

What follows is organised by how solid the source is: official union or government figures first, then dated third-party reporting clearly marked as an estimate, then an explicit list of leagues where nothing credible could be found at all.

Sweden: SHL and HockeyAllsvenskan

Sweden has the most union-published detail of any European league. SICO (Sveriges Ishockeyspelares Centralorganisation) publishes an SHL collective agreement with an age-based youth minimum — 22,000 SEK/month at age 18 rising to 25,000 SEK/month at age 21, gross — though that specific figure dates to a 2019 revision and a newer agreement has since been signed without a public number found for its updated floor. SICO's own site separately states an SHL average around 75,000–90,000 SEK/month, undated. The most rigorously dated figure comes from a third-party tracker, hockeyarena.se (updated 15 April 2026), which used 2024 Swedish tax records to calculate an SHL-wide average of 811,263 SEK/year, with the season's top earner at 2,752,700 SEK.

For HockeyAllsvenskan, SICO describes a typical player around 15,000 SEK/month, with the strongest clubs paying 40,000–60,000 SEK/month for top players; Division 1 below it is largely unpaid.

Finland: Liiga

The players' union SJRY's 2020–24 agreement referenced a 24,000 EUR/season threshold used to protect lower earners during a labour dispute, with roughly 100 top-division players below it at the time — that agreement window may since have expired and should be reconfirmed. More current and concrete: Finland's national broadcaster Yle (21 January 2026) reported sharp salary inflation tied to a 2026/27 league restructuring (only 14 of 17–18 clubs retaining top-flight status) and Jokerit's return with a 3M+ EUR roster budget, citing named examples — a player previously on €75,000–80,000 now commanding €130,000–140,000, with several named players over €200,000/year.

Czechia, Germany and Switzerland: estimated, not disclosed

None of these leagues publish salaries. The most consistent cross-league estimate found is playhockeyineurope.com (24 September 2025), which gives base-salary-only ranges explicitly marked as unofficial: Czechia's Extraliga 50,000–250,000+ EUR/year (second-tier 15,000–35,000 EUR; third-tier up to roughly 12,500 EUR for imports); Germany's DEL 90,000–350,000 EUR/year (DEL2 roughly 20,000–60,000 EUR per player-agency sources); Switzerland's National League 215,000–300,000+ EUR/year.

Swiss media (Blick, 20 Minuten) confirm the National League does not disclose salaries at all and offer their own tiered estimates: superstars 600,000–900,000 CHF/year, established players 350,000–500,000 CHF, supporting players 200,000–350,000 CHF, with a league average around 215,000 CHF reported for 2020. Germany's Oberliga, by contrast, is explicitly semi-pro — many players receive only expense compensation or in-kind benefits rather than a salary.

North America: NHL, AHL, ECHL — official and exact

Unlike anywhere in Europe on this page, North American leagues under a collective bargaining agreement publish an exact minimum. The NHL's 2025/26 league minimum is $775,000, rising on a published schedule to $850,000 (2026/27), $900,000 (2027/28), $950,000 (2028/29) and $1,000,000 (2029/30) under the current NHL–NHLPA agreement. The AHL's official CBA minimum was $52,725/season ($41,625 for players loaned from a lower league) through 2024/25 — reconfirm whether a newer agreement has since taken effect. The ECHL publishes an official weekly salary band for 2024/25: a $15,130 cap for the first 30 days ($14,600 afterward), an $11,100 floor, a $530/week rookie minimum and a $575/week returning-player minimum; 2025/26 figures were not yet published at the time of research.

Thinner data: Austria, Poland, Norway, Denmark

Austria's ICEHL: a third-party estimate of 40,000–150,000 EUR/year (playhockeyineurope, 24 September 2025), no official source found. Poland's Tauron Hokej Liga: Polish-language sources describe 20,000–35,000 EUR/year for solid domestic or import players and 40,000–75,000 EUR for top stars, undated. Norway's top league: a third-party estimate of 25,000–55,000 EUR/year, with the league itself described as aspiring to full professionalism rather than uniformly there. Denmark's Metal Ligaen: a Danish player-agency source describes 25,000–35,000 EUR/season for imports, a possible league salary cap ("lønloft") referenced in Danish sports media but whose exact figure could not be confirmed, and many domestic players earning close to nothing.

Where no credible data exists at all

This research found no usable salary information — official or third-party — for Slovakia's Tipos Extraliga or 1. Liga. That is a real gap, not an oversight: several searches turned up only league standings and structure pages, nothing on pay. Treat any specific number quoted for Slovak hockey with real scepticism until you see its source.

How to use this page

Every figure above is a floor, an average, or a third-party range — never a specific offer's benchmark. Before treating any number here as a reference point for a real contract, confirm the current season's figure with the club directly, and ask whether a quoted number is gross or net, and whether it includes housing, bonuses or other in-kind benefits, since those vary enormously and are rarely included in the ranges above.

Why isn't there one reliable salary table for European hockey?

Individual player contracts are private in almost every league. Even where a players' union has a collective agreement setting a minimum, actual pay above that minimum is negotiated per player and not published.

Which league publishes the most reliable salary information?

North American leagues under a collective bargaining agreement — the NHL, AHL and ECHL — publish exact, official minimum salaries. In Europe, Sweden's SHL and HockeyAllsvenskan have the most union-published detail of any league on this page.

Is the NHL minimum salary official?

Yes. Under the current NHL-NHLPA agreement the league minimum is $775,000 for 2025/26, rising on a published schedule to $1,000,000 by 2029/30.
