Two hockey team offers: how to choose a player's next step

Compare role, development, education and the full budget. A practical table and fictional example help prepare questions before the family decides.

By Viachaslau Marshchonak, European Hockey Agency. Updated 31 October 2026.

Compare the options · Check the budget

Discuss the two offers

First establish what you are comparing

A camp invitation and a season offer are different opportunities. Before comparing prices and league names, confirm the team, season, current player status and remaining selection conditions. If one offer concerns only a tryout, keep it as a separate scenario.

Use the club-offer checklist for credibility and documents and the camp and tender guide for status. This article supports the choice after those checks.

Define the objective and non-negotiable conditions

EHA editorial method: before discussing team names, choose the season's main objective: regular competition, specific skill development, preparation for the next level or combining hockey and education. Discuss it with the player.

  • Set a total family budget ceiling and contingency.
  • Define essential school, housing and responsible-adult arrangements for a minor.
  • Confirm eligibility, registration and season-specific documents with the relevant organizations.
  • Mark an unconfirmed essential condition as unknown and request an answer. Missing information is not a positive assessment.

A table for two written offers

For each row, record team A's answer, team B's answer and the supporting document or message. Labels such as proposed, confirmed and needs clarification are more useful than a combined score.

Discuss a role rather than promised minutes

Ask specifically what task the coach sees for the player, the positional competition, how lineup decisions are made and how progress will be discussed. Those answers help evaluate the plan without guaranteeing future ice time.

A stronger league can be useful with a suitable role and support. A more accessible league may provide needed game experience. Neither the league label nor projected minutes decides the question alone; full-game evidence and team context are needed.

Check an early-departure scenario

For each team, calculate non-refundable spending, the return journey and remaining housing or school obligations. Agree where a minor lives and studies after injury or a team change. Review the agreement before paying.

What to send EHA for a discussion

  • Birth year, position, current team and the player's objective.
  • Available links to verifiable statistics and a recent full game.
  • Both offer summaries: status, projected role, school, housing and costs.
  • The family's budget ceiling, decision timeline and unknown conditions.

Remove passport numbers, bank details and unnecessary personal data before sending. A free initial review helps identify sporting questions and missing information. Detailed video analysis and other paid work are agreed separately; disputed legal terms should be reviewed by a qualified lawyer.

Frequently asked questions

Should we always choose the stronger league?

No. Compare readiness, role, coaching, education and budget. The league name does not determine the best option.

Can we choose by promised ice time?

Discuss the specific plan and competition. A projected role alone does not guarantee future minutes.

How do we compare different monthly charges?

Multiply the charge by actual months and add other mandatory expenses. Account for confirmed coverage and a contingency.

Are the example prices actual tariffs?

No. All teams, terms and amounts are fictional and demonstrate the comparison method only.
