# Finland junior hockey fact-check

Checked 9 August 2026 against official sources. This is an editorial record, not legal advice.

## Verified points for 2025/26 (2026/27 not yet published at check time)

- Suomen Jääkiekkoliitto's (SJL) Kilpailusäännöt 2025–2026, §5.2, define age categories by single birth year, not a two-year band: U20 = born 2006, U19 = 2007, U18 = 2008, U17 = 2009, U16 = 2010, and so on down to U9. A 2026/27 rules update was adopted by the SJL board in late January 2026, but the document itself could not be fetched directly (site blocks automated access) — the site should state 2025/26 figures and flag that the 2026/27 birth years should shift by one year, unconfirmed.
- SJL's transfer rule for players under 18 (§6.7) is Finland's own, on top of the IIHF baseline: a **permanent** transfer to Finland for a player under 18 requires the player's guardian to relocate to Finland permanently together with the player, with documents proving the permanent move attached to the transfer application. This is a materially different condition from Sweden's rule (which asks for proof of top-level play in the home country plus a guardian/representative appointment, not guardian relocation).
- For players U15 and younger, the domestic transfer window closes 30 November, except when the player moves to Finland permanently with a guardian.
- The IIHF's own baseline (International Transfer Regulations, effective Oct 2025) applies underneath the Finnish rule: no transfer fee before age 18, and the general 1 July–15 February transfer-deadline window does not apply to under-18 transfers.
- Finland's structural equivalent to Sweden's NIU/LIP school system is the nationally designated **urheilulukio** network (~15 upper-secondary schools with a Ministry-granted special educational mandate) plus regional **urheiluakatemia** sport-academy partnerships, under the Olympic Committee's "kaksoisura" (dual-career) framework. There is no single nationally branded hockey-only school category comparable to NIU; some schools (e.g. Sotkamon lukio) publish a hockey-specific line via their urheilulukio status, but this is school-by-school, not a uniform national programme.
- No official SJL housing/billeting model was found for junior players moving clubs. As with Sweden, this must be verified with the club directly as a separate practical and safeguarding matter.
- Migri's "residence permit for athletes, coaches and referees" route appears aimed at adults earning income from sport; no minor/junior-athlete-specific immigration pathway was found. A non-EU minor's route is more likely family-reunification- or study-based; this needs direct confirmation with Migri or a consulate, not assumption.
- The Finnish Olympic Committee runs a national safe-sport framework ("Et ole yksin" / "You Are Not Alone," with Väestöliitto) and a 2025–2027 "Turvallinen toimintaympäristö" working group with a children's-rights subgroup. SJL's own club-level implementation of this could not be independently confirmed from a primary source.

## Publication note

The public page must state the guardian-relocation condition for a permanent transfer clearly and cautiously, since it is a materially different (and in practice more demanding) requirement than Sweden's. Retain the disclaimer to verify current rules with SJL, IIHF, the club, Migri and the consulate. Do not present housing, school admission or immigration approval as guaranteed.
