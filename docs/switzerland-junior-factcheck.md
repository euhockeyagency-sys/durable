# Switzerland junior hockey fact-check

Checked 9 August 2026 against official sources. This is an editorial record, not legal advice.

## Verified points for 2025/26 (2026/27 age table not yet published at check time)

- Swiss Ice Hockey Federation (SIHF) "Reglement für den Spielbetrieb im Nachwuchs-, Amateur- und Frauensport 2026/2027" (R054, dated 27 May 2026), Art. 101, defines age by a formula (second season-year minus birth year), with named tiers U21-Elit, U18-Elit (tied to National League/Swiss League club organisations), plus U21-Top/A, U18-Top/A, U16, U14, U12 and U9 below. The "Reglement zum Ausbildungsvertrag 2025/26" confirms "U18- (U17 und U18) und U21-Jahrgänge (U19 bis U21)."
- The most recently published exact birth-year table (dated 11 August 2025, for season 2025/2026 — no 2026/27 table found yet) states: U21 = born 2005 and younger; U18 = born 2008–2009 and younger; U16 = 2010–2011 and younger; U14 = 2012–2013 and younger; U12 = 2014–2016 and younger; U9 = 2017 and younger. Applying SIHF's own stated formula, the 2026/27 table would shift every cut-off by one year — but this is a calculation, not a published table, and must be flagged as such on the public page.
- **Important, counter-intuitive finding on minor transfers**: per SIHF's "Rahmenbedingungen für Registrierungen und Transfers" (dated 24 June 2025), Art. 11 Ziff. 3, a player under 18 who is not yet playing on a Swiss senior team does **not need an international transfer card at all** — only a copy of the passport/ID, a written release from the last foreign club, and the last foreign residence address, filed with the registration. This is a *simpler* process than for an adult, not a stricter one. For a foreign junior never before registered anywhere, Form T11 (written parental confirmation) is additionally required.
- General transfer rule ("Weisungen zu den internationalen Transfers," 18 July 2023): for minors specifically, the parent/guardian's signature is required on the standard transfer form (used once a player is registered for a senior team or the T11 route doesn't apply). No hard "transfer must happen before age 18" rule was found.
- Transfer windows: 15 April–15 September (main period); 1 November–31 December ("Jokertransfer," also the deadline for international transfers into youth/amateur hockey).
- No single nationwide SIHF-run school system; instead named club/canton partnerships under Swiss Olympic's school-partnership model: EVZ Hockey Academy (Zug) with OYM College (a 4-year short-track upper-secondary or 2+2-year apprenticeship track, open from roughly U14/U15 once compulsory schooling is complete); Kunst und Sportschule Zürich, officially partnered with GCK/ZSC Lions and EHC Kloten; Swiss Women's Hockey Academy, partnered with OYM College and Sportmittelschule Engelberg.
- **Housing is one area where Switzerland differs from Sweden's precedent: it does have some official documentation.** The U18-Elit/U21-Elit training-contract template (R067) lists "Wohnlösungen, Gastfamilie" (housing solutions, host family) as an example of an individually negotiated compensation item (Art. 4.3), and the Swiss Women's Hockey Academy page states players stay at home or with a host family depending on where they live. This is still a club-level, individually negotiated arrangement — not a federation-run, vetted billeting programme with published safeguarding standards — and that distinction should be made clearly on the public page.
- Switzerland is outside the EU but bound by the bilateral Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons (in force since 1 June 2002) with the EU/EFTA, giving EU/EFTA citizens materially fewer restrictions than third-country nationals. **A serious, though dated (2016), finding**: an SIHF directive on work/residence-permit control states that third-country (non-EU/EFTA) nationals cannot be admitted as members of junior or senior teams, or as coaches, under Switzerland's foreign-worker permit system for professional athletes — this is stated as inadmissible outright, not merely restricted. This is an important caution to include, but the source predates a 2019 renaming of the underlying immigration law (AuG→AIG) and must be re-verified against current SEM guidance before being stated as current fact.
- Swiss Sport Integrity (established 2022) is the independent national ethics/reporting body under Swiss Olympic's Ethics Statute (in force 3 January 2022), which explicitly covers minor athletes and their guardians; SIHF's own site confirms adherence and a mandatory reporting duty for coaches and supervisory staff.

## Publication note

The public page should present the "no transfer card needed under 18" finding accurately without implying transfer is unregulated — parental consent and club-release documentation are still required. The non-EU work-permit restriction must be flagged as a serious, structural caution while noting the source is dated and needs re-verification with SEM before a firm claim. Retain the disclaimer to verify current rules with SIHF, IIHF, SEM and the club, and do not present a host-family arrangement as a federation-vetted guarantee.
