European Hockey Signing Windows: When Clubs Sign Imports & When It's Too Late

A month-by-month guide that separates club recruiting habits from official registration, transfer and visa deadlines.

Short answer

There is no single European transfer window. Clubs in most leagues sign imports mainly from April to August, but signings continue during the season as replacements, and official deadlines are set separately by each national federation and competition. The IIHF's international transfer window runs from 1 July to 15 February (it does not apply to players under 18), while national deadlines confirmed for 2026/27 range from 30 December in the Alps Hockey League to 31 January in Sweden and Czechia.

It is rarely too late to look, but it does get narrower: after September you compete for replacement spots, and after the target competition's registration deadline that league is closed for the season. Below is a month-by-month guide. Club behaviour is described as a typical pattern from EHA's experience, not a rule.

1. Four separate clocks

  1. Roster building: when clubs usually recruit. A market pattern, not a deadline.
  2. Registration and international transfer: official deadlines of the federation or competition, and the IIHF transfer process for a move between countries.
  3. Visa and residence: a non-EU player needs the right permit, which has its own processing time.
  4. Junior or adult route: junior moves follow the school year and different rules; players under 18 are outside the IIHF window.

A signed contract does not make a player eligible. All four clocks have to line up.

3. Month by month

April–May: plan and start early

Rosters for next season are shaped as soon as playoffs end. Players with strong recent numbers and a clear role should start in April or May. Prepare a hockey resume, a highlight video and at least one full game.

June–July: the main signing period

Middle and lower leagues sign most of their imports now. Check import limits and the visa route of each target country before you agree terms.

August–September: late spots

Camps, injuries and failed tryouts open places. Consider tryout terms, lower levels and countries with later start dates.

October–December: replacements

Clubs replace imports who do not fit. Current-season video and fitness matter most.

January: the last additions

Many national deadlines fall at the end of January. Confirm the exact date of the target competition in writing.

4. Juniors and adults

Junior moves follow the school year: applications to sport schools and junior programmes often run in winter and spring, and players under 18 are not bound by the IIHF 1 July–15 February window, although national rules may still apply. See the junior hockey calendar and documents and IIHF transfer for a minor.

Adult imports follow club roster cycles and competition deadlines described above.

5. Visa and permit timing

For a player who needs a permit, the application usually starts after a signed contract and can take weeks. Check the processing information of the country's migration authority, whether the player may train before the permit is granted, and who pays the fees. A late signing with a long permit process can miss a registration deadline. See work visa for a hockey player and visas and transfers.

7. If you are searching late

  • Confirm which competitions still accept registrations and until when.
  • Widen the range: a lower league, another country or a club that needs a replacement.
  • Consider a tryout with clear written terms: length, who pays travel and housing.
  • Keep training and keep your video current; a call can come at short notice.
  • If this season closes, use the time to prepare for April and May.

Missing the main recruitment period does not make a search impossible; it changes which options are realistic.

FAQ

Is there one transfer deadline for all of Europe?

No. Each federation and competition sets its own deadlines. The IIHF window of 1 July–15 February governs international transfers of players aged 18 and over, and national deadlines sit inside it.

When do European clubs sign most imports?

In EHA's experience, mostly from April to August, with replacements during the season. This is a pattern, not a rule.

Is it too late to find a club in October?

Not necessarily. Clubs replace players during the season, and lower leagues may still have places. Check the registration deadline of the target competition.

Does the IIHF window apply to junior players?

Not to players under 18: the IIHF regulations exempt their transfers from the 1 July–15 February window. National rules may still apply.

Can a contract be signed after the deadline?

Signing and eligibility are separate. After the competition's deadline a player may be under contract but not allowed to play in it this season.
