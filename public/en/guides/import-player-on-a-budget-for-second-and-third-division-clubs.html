How a second- or third-division club can find an import on a limited budget

Role first, then eligibility and total cost, then players — with five concrete reasons to re-check Elite Prospects statistics before you sign.

Updated 18 November 2026.

Order of work · Five checks · Budget · Club request

Short answer

A second- or third-division club with a limited budget gets the best import by fixing the role first, checking eligibility and total cost second, and only then comparing players — using Elite Prospects as a starting list, not a verdict. Before you trust a points line, re-check five things: sample size, a change of league level, mixed competitions, missing role context and whether the numbers match full-game video.

The cheapest mistake to avoid is signing a player who cannot be registered or does not fit the role you actually need.

Work in this order

  1. Role: position, line, special teams, minutes you can give, character in the room.
  2. Eligibility: your league's import rule, the player's status, the IIHF transfer and the work-permit salary condition for non-EU players (league rules, permits).
  3. Total cost: salary, housing, car, flights, insurance, ITC and registration fees (ITC costs).
  4. Shortlist: three to five players who fit 1–3.
  5. Evidence: the five checks below, full-game video, a call with a previous coach.

Five reasons to re-check Elite Prospects statistics

Reason to re-checkWhyWhat exactly to check
1. Small sampleFewer than about 25–30 games, or one hot month, can make a player look better or worse than he is.Games per season over the last two seasons; scoring by month; whether the season ended early through injury or a move.
2. League level changedPoints in a weaker league do not carry over one-to-one; a step up usually lowers production.Which league each season was in, its level relative to yours, and how other players moved between the two leagues.
3. Competitions mixed togetherRegular season, playoffs, cups and tournaments are sometimes added together, or junior and senior lines are confused.Read each row: competition, team and games. Compare regular-season senior games only.
4. No role contextA first-line power-play forward and a fourth-line penalty killer are not comparable on points.Line, special teams, ice time if published, and what the coach used him for. Ask the previous coach.
5. Statistics do not match videoA good line can come from a strong team; a poor one can hide a useful defensive player.A full game and full shifts, not highlights. Check pace, decisions under pressure, and the habits behind the numbers.

Making a limited budget go further

  • Pay for a defined role; avoid paying a premium for points earned in a different role.
  • Look at players whose status saves an import place in your league, where the rule allows.
  • A trial with written terms costs less than a wrong season-long contract (trial checklist).
  • Plan arrival so the first two weeks work (adaptation checklist).

Frequently asked questions

Can a club rely on Elite Prospects statistics?

As a starting list, yes; as a verdict, no. Re-check sample size, league level, mixed competitions, role and video.

What should a club check first?

The role, then eligibility under your league's import rule and the total cost, then compare players.

How many games are enough to trust statistics?

Fewer than about 25–30 games is a weak basis; look at two seasons.
