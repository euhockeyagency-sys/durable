Alps Hockey League vs Oberliga: which to choose in 2026/27

Two leagues often offered to the same player: the cross-border Alps Hockey League and Germany's third-level Oberliga, compared point by point.

Updated 18 November 2026.

Short answer

Both are realistic first senior steps in Central Europe, but they reward different players. The Alps Hockey League's 2026/27 point system makes young non-domestic players cheaper in the quota (1 point if born 2007 or later, 2 if born 2005–2006, 4 for a senior import, 16 points per game) and the league spans four countries. The Oberliga has 26 German teams in two groups, is reported at three imports per game, and for non-EU players the German athlete visa requires €50,700 gross a year — which many Oberliga offers do not reach.

Which one for which player

  • U20/U22 import without EU passport: Alps Hockey League — the age points help and the German salary filter is avoided
  • EU senior player with a defined role: Both; compare ice time and the package
  • Non-EU senior player: Alps Hockey League unless the Oberliga offer reaches €50,700 gross a year
  • Player with German status: Oberliga — you do not use an import place

Frequently asked questions

Which league has more room for young imports?

The Alps Hockey League: under its 2026/27 rules a non-domestic player born in 2007 or later counts 1 point of 16.

Can a non-EU player join an Oberliga club?

Only if the offer meets the German athlete route, €50,700 gross a year in 2026, among other conditions.

Which pays more?

There is no reliable sample for the Oberliga; EHA estimates AlpsHL pay at about €15,000–45,000 per season.
