News · 10 August 2026

NCAA now lets CHL players keep Division I eligibility

A rule change that took effect in August 2025 removes one of the biggest trade-offs junior players used to weigh when choosing between Canada's CHL and an NCAA-track league.

The NCAA Division I Council approved a rules change on 7 November 2024, effective 1 August 2025: a player who competes in the CHL (the umbrella for the WHL, OHL and QMJHL) can now retain NCAA Division I eligibility, provided he receives no more than "actual and necessary expenses" — meals, lodging, transport, equipment, coaching, ice time and medical or health insurance — with no salary. Before this change, CHL players were generally treated as professionals for NCAA purposes and lost Division I eligibility outright.

The change does not extend to NCAA Division III, where CHL players remain ineligible, and it does not help a player who has signed an NHL contract: returning to the CHL after an NHL deal still forfeits NCAA eligibility. Per the OHL's own published FAQ, a player who leaves the league before completing his Standard Player Agreement also forfeits the league's education package; completing the agreed term first preserves it for later use at an NCAA school.

Why this matters for players: until this rule, choosing the CHL over an NCAA-track junior league (such as the USA's NCDC) was effectively a one-way decision — it closed the NCAA Division I door. That is no longer automatically true. It does not make the two routes equivalent: NCAA's own age-based eligibility model and each league's roster, education and immigration rules still differ in ways covered on EHA's Canada junior hockey and USA NCDC guides. But it is now a real, current factor to weigh rather than an outdated assumption to correct.
