How to choose a hockey academy: a checklist for parents

Four criteria to compare and the questions about ice time, exit and refunds that families most often skip.

Criteria · Ice time · Exit and refund · Before you pay · Next step

Short answer

Compare academies on four things — the level the player will actually play at, the school, the housing and supervision, and the full cost — and get each in writing. Then ask the three questions families most often skip: how ice time is decided, how the player can leave the programme, and what part of the fee is refunded if he does. A famous name, a glossy brochure or a promise of minutes is not a criterion.

Four criteria to compare

CriterionWhat to compareWhat to ask for
Playing levelWhich league and division each team plays in; how many players per birth year; who coaches and with what licenceA federation status where one exists — e.g. the Czech “Akademie ČH” licence
SchoolNamed school, language, accreditation, timetable around ice, recognition at homeSchool enrolment in writing; in Sweden hockey profiles run through upper-secondary schools (NIU/LIP)
HousingBillet family, dormitory or flat; who supervises evenings and weekends; mealsWritten house rules and an emergency contact; Hockey Canada's billeting standard as a benchmark
Full costFee plus everything outside it: insurance, books, uniform, travel, flights, pocket moneyAn itemised offer; published examples show what is often extra (CIHA: insurance, textbooks, uniform package; SSM: books, uniforms, a $3,000 incidental deposit)

Questions about ice time

  • Which team will the player be registered with at the start of the season, and in which league?
  • How many players of his position and birth year are in the programme?
  • How is ice time decided, and how often is the player's role reviewed?
  • If he does not play regularly, is there a partner team where he can?
  • What feedback will we receive, from whom and how often?

No academy can honestly promise minutes. Treat a written promise of ice time as a reason to ask more questions, not as a guarantee.

Exit, refund and deposit

  • Deposit: amount, due date, and whether it is refundable — and in which cases.
  • Leaving early: what notice is needed, and what happens to fees already paid for housing, school and hockey.
  • Injury or illness: is any part refunded, and does insurance cover medical costs abroad?
  • Being cut or moved: what happens if the academy moves the player to a lower team or ends the place.
  • Not registered: what is refunded if the federation transfer or residence permit is not approved in time.

Ask for these terms in the contract itself, in a language you understand. For a minor, check who has to sign — the rules depend on the country and the programme (see moving under 18).

Before you pay

  1. Verify the legal entity that receives the money.
  2. Speak to a family whose child spent last season there.
  3. Get the school enrolment confirmed by the school.
  4. Compare the full cost of at least two options.
  5. Run through the readiness checklist with your child.

Frequently asked questions

What matters most when choosing a hockey academy?

The level the player will actually play at, a confirmed school place, supervised housing and the full cost in writing — plus clear exit and refund terms.

Can an academy guarantee ice time?

No. Ask how ice time is decided and reviewed, and whether there is a partner team if the player does not dress regularly.

What should a refund policy cover?

The deposit, leaving early, injury or illness, being cut or moved, and a failed transfer or residence permit.
