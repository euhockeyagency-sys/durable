Hockey in Belgium: leagues, work permits and transfers

A cautious guide for players and families: a top league currently being restructured, domestic divisions, regionalised work permits, IIHF transfer and tryouts.

01

How hockey in Belgium is structured

The Royal Belgian Ice Hockey Federation (RBIHF), founded 1912, runs Belgium's domestic pyramid directly: Division 1 down to the recreational Divisions 3–4, plus youth categories from U8 to U18 and a new U23 competition being added for 2026/27. Belgium's stronger clubs also compete in an international league jointly branded with the Netherlands and, since 2023/24, Germany.

Important, current as of this review: that international league — long known as the BeNe League — was renamed the Central European Hockey League (CEHL) for 2024/25, and by mid-2026 it was visibly unstable: several clubs (including Dutch and German sides) left during 2026, and Belgian clubs' top-flight home for 2026/27 was still being finalised at the time of research. Confirm the current name, format and participating Belgian clubs directly with RBIHF before treating any older description of "the BeNe League" as current.

02

Division 1Belgium's own top domestic tier
Divisions 2–4lower and recreational levels

2025/26 Division-1-level clubs included Bulldogs Liège, Chiefs Leuven, Cold Play Mechelen and HYC Herentals. Division 2 is being reclassified as a contact division from 2026/27. Because the top-flight picture was unsettled at the time of research, verify with RBIHF which Belgian clubs are playing in which competition for the season in question.

03

Youth hockey

RBIHF runs U8, U10, U12, U14, U16 and U18 categories, with 2025/26 team counts of roughly 9 (U18), 13 (U16) and 13 (U14), and is adding a U23 bridge competition for 2026/27 to connect youth and senior hockey. For an international junior, ask the club to confirm the team, role, education, housing, guardian arrangements where required, insurance and international transfer in writing.

04

Choosing a realistic level

Clubs assess previous league strength, skating, decision speed, physical readiness, play away from the puck, role, special teams, recent video and verifiable statistics. Given the current restructuring, also ask which specific competition — CEHL or a domestic division — the role is for, since a "Belgian club" offer can mean very different levels right now.

05

Import limits

No official RBIHF or CEHL regulations document could be located publicly. Secondary reporting from mid-2026 describes the CEHL import cap being cut sharply — from four per squad to one — with some Dutch players who were previously exempt now reclassified as imports on Belgian rosters. This is not officially confirmed.

Before accepting: given both the league restructuring and the reported change to the import rule, ask the club for the written, current roster rule rather than relying on any historical figure, including the ones in this guide.
06

Contract, salary and conditions

No official salary information is published for Belgian hockey. Put the following in writing before signing:

  • gross or net amount, currency, payment dates, bonuses and taxes;
  • housing, deposit, utilities, meals and transport;
  • equipment, sticks, medical care and insurance;
  • tryout, injury, termination and national-team release terms;
  • who pays for the work permit, IIHF transfer and federation fees.
07

EU/EEA and non-EU players: a regionalised system

EU/EEA/Swiss citizens have standard free-movement rights and do not need a work permit, though residence registration still applies. Belgium's non-EU route is unusual in Europe because it is regionalised: work-permit applications go through whichever region the employer is based in, not a single federal office.

  • Flanders: a dedicated professional-athlete route; the 2026 minimum salary threshold is €52,083.50/year.
  • Brussels: separate rules by contract length — competition of 3 months or less per year can be exempt from a permit; longer contracts need a Single Permit or a cross-border "long-term authorisation to work"; short stays up to 90 days use a "short-term authorisation to work." A specific minimum-remuneration figure for Brussels could not be confirmed.
  • Wallonia: requires a contract compliant with the 1978 law on professional-athlete contracts, plus a "Permis B" for stays under 90 days.

Since 3 January 2019, longer non-EU work stays generally use the federal Single Permit, which combines residence and work authorisation, but the application itself is filed through the relevant region. Confirm the correct region and current thresholds directly — none of the official pages consulted showed a page-level update date.

08

IIHF transfer, federation registration and insurance

  1. International transfer: a move from another national federation requires an IIHF international transfer.
  2. Registration: RBIHF's 2026/27 registration deadline was 1 August 2026 — confirm the current deadline for the season in question.
  3. Work authorisation: sporting eligibility does not replace the regional work-permit process for a non-EU player.
  4. Insurance: confirm cover for tryouts, training, games, injury and treatment.
09

Player documents

  • valid passport;
  • signed agreement or official club letter;
  • EU residence registration, or Single Permit / regional work-permit documents for a non-EU player;
  • last hockey registration and information for the IIHF transfer;
  • medical records and insurance;
  • for a minor: consent, guardian, school and housing arrangements.
10

Recruitment timing and tryouts

RBIHF's registration deadline has fallen around 1 August in recent seasons, but 2026's CEHL restructuring compressed and disrupted the usual planning calendar, so this year's timeline may not be typical. Before a tryout, confirm dates, format, costs, insurance, housing, contact person, decision criteria and response deadline. Use the EHA tryout checklist.

Editorial review: 9 August 2026. This is a new market for EHA, captured mid-restructuring — re-check the current league name, format, import rule and work-permit region directly with RBIHF and CEHL before acting; several points on this page are explicitly flagged as unconfirmed or in flux.

Is the BeNe League still called that?

No. It was renamed the Central European Hockey League (CEHL) for the 2024/25 season after German clubs joined as full members. As of mid-2026 several clubs have since left CEHL, and Belgium's top-flight structure for 2026/27 was still being finalised — verify the current name and format directly with the Belgian federation before acting.

How many import players can a Belgian club use?

Not officially confirmed. Secondary reporting describes the cap being cut from four imports per squad to one for 2026/27, with some previously exempt Dutch players reclassified as imports — but this must be verified directly with the league before relying on it.

Do EU citizens need a work permit in Belgium?

No, EU/EEA/Swiss citizens have standard free-movement rights. Non-EU players need a Single Permit, applied for through the region where the employer is based — Flanders, Brussels or Wallonia each run their own process.

Is an IIHF international transfer required?

Yes, when a player moves from a club or federation in another country.
