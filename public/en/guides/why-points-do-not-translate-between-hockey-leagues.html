Why 1.0 points per game in one league does not guarantee the same scoring in another

How league-equivalency models such as NHLe work, why one number is not enough, and why EHA publishes the method but no coefficients yet.

Updated 18 November 2026.

Method · Limitations · EHA data · Next step

Short answer

1.0 point per game in one league does not mean 1.0 in another, because leagues differ in level, pace, ice time and how scoring is shared. Public “league equivalency” models such as NHLe estimate a factor for each league from players who moved between leagues and played enough games in both. EHA does not publish its own coefficients yet: our verified sample of moves with season statistics is too small to give stable numbers, so this page explains the method and its limits instead.

How league-equivalency models work

  1. Collect pairs of seasons: players who played league A one season and league B the next (or the same season), each with a minimum number of games.
  2. Compare scoring rates: points per game in B divided by points per game in A, across all such players.
  3. Chain leagues: leagues with few direct moves are linked through intermediate leagues.
  4. Adjust: better models correct for age (players improve), position, and the selection effect — only players who were good enough get to move up.

The result is a factor such as “points in league A are worth about X% of points in league B”. It describes average players, not you.

Why one number is not enough

LimitationWhat it means for a player
Small samplesBetween smaller European leagues there may be only a handful of moves a season; one outlier changes the factor.
Role and ice timeA first-line power-play forward and a depth player move with the same factor but very different outcomes.
AgeA 19-year-old usually improves; a 29-year-old may not.
PositionDefencemen and goaltenders are judged on much more than points.
Seasons changeLeagues change level, import rules and format; old factors age.

What EHA's data allows today

EHA has 37 published transfer cases, but they do not yet record the previous league, the season and games and points before and after each move, and many come from different leagues. That is not enough to estimate a factor for any pair of leagues with useful precision. Before an “EHA Index” is published or built into the calculator, we will need, for each league pair: a recorded season, at least 20 games before and after for each player, enough players to report a range rather than a single number, and a check of the model on moves it did not see.

Until then the Level Calculator uses broad level bands, the player's current level, age and scoring as a rough signal — not a league-by-league conversion.

Want a level estimate that uses your league, age and points together? Take the EHA Level Calculator: it gives an indicative league range in two minutes. It does not check citizenship, age rules or registration, so treat the result as preliminary.

How to use points when you choose a league

  • Compare like with like: regular-season senior games, the same role.
  • Expect scoring to fall when you step up a level; plan for a role, not for your old points.
  • Let video and a coach's view decide close cases (why good stats do not bring offers).

Frequently asked questions

What is NHLe?

A public league-equivalency model that estimates how scoring in one league translates to the NHL, based on players who moved between leagues.

Does EHA publish league coefficients?

Not yet. Our verified sample of moves with season statistics is too small for stable numbers.

Will my points drop if I move to a stronger league?

Usually yes; plan for a role rather than for your old production.

Does the Level Calculator convert points between leagues?

No. It uses broad level bands with your current level, age and scoring as a rough signal.
