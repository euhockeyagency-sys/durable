After U20: How to Move into Senior Hockey in Europe

Start with a team where you can train at the senior pace and compete for a clearly defined role. Before accepting an offer, ask where you fit in the lineup, what you need to show to play and who will confirm your registration.

Assess readiness · Compare offers · Plan the move

1. Turn your last U20 season into evidence

EHA assessment. Start with the role you can show on video. List your league and team, position, regular assignments at even strength and on special teams, and any senior practices or games. Separate junior and senior statistics. If a club sees only your junior highlights, it still has to judge how you react under pressure and what you do without the puck.

  • Forwards: show puck retrievals, decisions under pressure, support away from the puck and tracking back.
  • Defencemen: show retrievals under a forecheck, first passes, gap control and recovery after a turnover.
  • Goalies: show traffic, rebound control and how you reset after a goal. Ask prospective teams how starts and practice reps are allocated.

Prepare a one-page hockey résumé and one or two recent full games with jersey number, date and key timestamps. Include a competitive game where the opponent forces you to make difficult decisions.

2. Compare two offers by the role you can earn

Illustrative EHA example. Two hypothetical senior teams are considering the same player after U20.

QuestionA · Higher divisionB · Lower division
Where do I start?Training squad, match role to be decided.Competing for a specific lineup role.
What decides selection?Ask who is ahead of you and when the coach reviews the lineup.Ask what the coach expects in that role and who else is competing for it.
What happens if I sit?Ask about eligible game opportunities and who confirms them.Ask how feedback, practice reps and selection are handled.
What will I pay?Request written housing, travel and other costs.Request the same items so both offers can be compared.

If your priority is regular senior games, look closely at B and verify the competition for that role. If A can explain how you will progress from training to match selection, it deserves a closer look. Compare the full package using the written-offer checklist.

3. Plan the transition before and after signing

  1. Before the junior season ends: gather full-game video and ask your current coach which senior role your play supports.
  2. When comparing teams: shortlist a few options using the senior league directory. Ask each team about lineup competition, training, housing and what happens if you are not selected.
  3. Before travelling: confirm the written terms, the season’s competition rules, domestic registration and who handles any IIHF transfer. For a move abroad, check residence and work requirements for the actual arrangement separately.
  4. During your first month: ask the coach for two concrete priorities and a date to review them. Track games dressed, assignments and feedback so you can discuss progress with examples.

A qualitative study of seven Swedish semi-professional players described the transition in phases from preparation to establishing a role. The steps above are EHA’s practical application of that idea. For the governing transfer documents, see IIHF Rules and Regulations.

Questions players ask after U20

Can I move into senior hockey without senior games on my résumé?

Present the strongest evidence you have from U20 and any senior training. Ask what the prospective team needs to see before offering a trial or considering you for its lineup. Eligibility is checked separately.

How do I know whether an offer includes a real playing opportunity?

Ask where you would train, which players compete for your role and when the coach reviews selection. Request written terms for the offer and clarify what happens if you are left out of the lineup.

Which senior role suits you now?

Share your birth year, position, last league, playing role and a link to a full game. If you have an offer, add a short summary of its terms.
