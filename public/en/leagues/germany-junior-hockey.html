Germany junior hockey: player route and checks

A practical, cautious overview of Germany's U20 and U17 Division tiers for a player and family. Note that the old "DNL1/DNL2" naming was retired in 2018/19 — current official terms are U20/U17 Division I–III.

01

League position and 2026/27 status

U20 Division I/II/III and U17 Division I/II are Germany's top national junior tiers, run by the German Ice Hockey Federation (DEB). For 2026/27, DEB published the Division I schedules: U20 started on 29 August 2026 and U17 Division I North/South started on 5 September 2026. The old "DNL1/DNL2" branding is no longer the official naming.

02

Age and registration

DEB's Spielordnung (Art. 50) fixes age by the calendar year the season begins, spanning three birth years per tier — wider than some other markets. For 2026/27 (season begins 2026): U20 = born 2007–2009; U17 = born 2010–2011. Recheck this for the exact season you are assessing, since the reference year rolls forward annually.

03

What a club evaluates

Clubs assess skating, pace, skills, role, physical readiness, decision-making, full-game shifts and development potential, not highlights alone.

04

Foreign players under 18 — a real simplification

DEB's Spielordnung (Art. 52a) states that for a player who has not yet turned 18, the formal international transfer card is not required — a simple written or electronic confirmation from the player's former national federation suffices, which is a simplification relative to an adult's transfer. A general rule for all minors still requires the permit application to be countersigned by the player's legal representative. Underneath this, the IIHF baseline applies: no transfer fee before age 18, and the general transfer-deadline window does not apply to under-18 transfers. One point that needs direct confirmation with DEB before booking any move: the domestic transfer-window rule (1 June–15 September and 1 December–31 January) does not carry an explicit under-18 exception in the text found, even though the IIHF layer above it waives its own deadline for minors — ask the club how these two rules interact for your specific timing.

05

Education alongside hockey

Germany's nationwide "Eliteschule des Sports" brand (43 sites across all sports) has a confirmed ice-hockey site: the Schul- und Leistungssportzentrum Berlin, taking players from grade 7. Separately, Bavaria runs its own distinct state programme — "Partnerzentren des Wintersports" / Stützpunktschule, not the Eliteschule brand — with Realschule Füssen recognised for ice hockey alongside EV Füssen, and a boarding house at neighbouring Gymnasium Hohenschwangau. These are two different systems; confirm which one, if either, applies to a specific club before assuming a school place.

06

Housing and care

No DEB-level housing or billeting policy was found. Practice is club-level only: Krefeld has run a boarding house via a youth-welfare partner since 2006; Adler Mannheim's academy has its own residence; Kölner Haie and EHC Nürnberg publicly recruit host families for their Division players. Confirm the responsible adult, safeguarding contact, room, meals, transport, insurance and emergency contact directly with the specific club.

07

Recruitment timing

There is no single universal tryout window beyond the domestic transfer periods noted above. Request a real assessment schedule, evaluation criteria and decision date directly from the club.

For players with Russian and Belarusian passports

Russian and Belarusian citizens must meet visa or other residence requirements; sporting eligibility is established separately. A passport and club invitation do not confirm U20/U17 registration or residence for a full season.

Russian passport: a biometric passport is required for new visa applications and German residence-permit extensions from 1 January 2026. Official instructions preserve use of non-biometric passports with certain valid German visas or permits issued before that date. Check the source for the exception’s conditions; do not automatically apply the Russian document rule to Belarusian passports.

The school route is subject to §16f AufenthG. Confirm the qualifying school and admission, funding, insurance and support for the minor separately. If the player already lives in Germany on another basis, the conditions of their own status matter.

08

Immigration and travel

The school residence basis in §16f(2) AufenthG normally applies from grade 9 and sets conditions for the international composition of the class and the type of school. The school must be public or state-recognised with an international orientation, or a qualifying school financed outside or mainly outside public funds. A club-linked school does not automatically qualify.

For a minor, separately confirm legal-representative consent, support, housing and funding against consular and immigration requirements. A §16f(2) school permit does not authorise employment; any paid sporting duties require separate clarification.

09

Costs and written terms

Do not rely on verbal promises about fees, equipment, housing or travel. Ask for a written breakdown, payment recipient, refund terms and what is conditional on selection.

FAQ

Does a player under 18 need a full international transfer card in Germany?

No. DEB's Spielordnung (Art. 52a) states the formal transfer card is not required before 18 — a written or electronic confirmation from the player's former federation suffices, alongside a legal representative's countersignature.

Is there one German "hockey school" brand?

No. The Eliteschule des Sports brand has one confirmed ice-hockey site (Berlin); Bavaria runs a separate state programme (Partnerzentren des Wintersports) recognising Realschule Füssen — these are two different systems.

Is there a DEB-level housing policy for junior players?

No DEB-level housing or billeting policy was found — arrangements are club-level only (for example Krefeld, Adler Mannheim, Kölner Haie, EHC Nürnberg), so confirm directly with the specific club.

What was the old "DNL" naming?

DNL1/DNL2 was the competition's former branding, retired for the 2018/19 season — current official terms are U20/U17 Division I–III.

Can a Russian or Belarusian passport holder play in Germany?

The passport does not confirm sporting eligibility. Agree registration with the club and DEB and establish an appropriate residence basis. A team invitation does not replace a visa or permit.

Does a Russian player need a biometric passport?

A biometric Russian passport is required for new visa applications and German permit extensions from 1 January 2026. Certain valid German visas and permits issued earlier have exceptions; see the official instructions for their conditions.
