Tipsport Extraliga vs National League: which fits you?

Czechia's Tipsport Extraliga and Switzerland's National League are each country's top professional men's competition. Neither publishes a foreign-player quota or a universal salary table — here is what the two pages already confirmed actually say, side by side.

01

Quick comparison

🇨🇿 Czechia

Tipsport Extraliga

  • Czechia's top professional men's league
  • 14 clubs for 2026/27, schedule opens 16 September 2026
  • Seven games per round
  • No published foreign-player number
  • EU member — EU/EEA citizens use the standard EU route

🇨🇭 Switzerland

National League

  • Switzerland's highest professional men's competition
  • No public 2026/27 team or game count found — verify with SIHF
  • Season format set by the current official calendar
  • No published foreign-player number
  • Outside the EU, bound by the EU/EFTA free-movement agreement
02

League position and level

Both are their country's top flight, but "top flight" is not a shared scale. Tipsport Extraliga is Czechia's top professional men's league; the National League is Switzerland's highest professional men's ice hockey competition, run by SIHF with National League AG organising the competition. Comparing a specific club's budget, roster and recent results tells you more than the league label alone.

03

2026/27 format and schedule

Tipsport Extraliga's published draw confirms 14 clubs and a schedule opening 16 September 2026, seven games per round; playoff, licensing and eligibility detail must be checked in the current APK LH documents. The National League's page does not state a specific 2026/27 team or game count — use the current official SIHF game calendar and directives rather than an earlier season's numbers.

04

Foreign-player and roster rules

Neither page states a specific foreign-player quota. For Tipsport Extraliga, the public schedule alone does not prove a vacancy or a player's foreign-status treatment — the club must confirm current competition rules, registration deadline and roster place. For the National League, do not assume a universal import place; the club must verify current National League and SIHF directives, player classification, licence history and any game-sheet restrictions.

05

Recruitment timing

Neither league publishes a single recruiting window for imports. For both, many decisions happen before the season while in-season openings are targeted individually — approach a club for a defined position, and do not travel or pay costs without a written invitation and terms.

06

Pay, housing and written terms

Neither league publishes one official salary table covering every club. In both cases, get gross/net pay, payment dates, bonuses, housing, deposit, utilities, food, travel, equipment, insurance, injury terms, trial period, termination and return travel in writing before relying on a verbal offer.

07

Residence and right to work

Czechia is an EU member, so EU/EEA citizens use the standard EU route, while third-country nationals face a separate immigration and employment procedure confirmed with Czech authorities. Switzerland sits outside the EU but is bound by the bilateral Agreement on Free Movement of Persons with the EU/EFTA, giving EU/EFTA citizens an easier route than third-country nationals — in both countries, a sporting offer alone is not a visa or work permission, and the club and player must verify the applicable official route before travel.

08

IIHF transfer — the same underlying process

Both moves follow the current IIHF International Transfer Regulations when the player moves between national associations. In both countries, an ITC does not replace domestic registration, a valid contract, insurance or immigration permission — confirm who starts the transfer and who pays each fee before travel.

09

Which one fits your situation

This is not a "which league is better" question — it is a fit question. An EU passport removes one layer of friction for Czechia specifically; a non-EU/EFTA player faces a genuinely separate check for Switzerland. Neither country publishes a foreign-player cap, so the real constraint in both cases is whether a specific club has a role, budget and roster spot for your position — not the league name. Send your profile for both if the level realistically fits, and let the club-level conversation decide it.
