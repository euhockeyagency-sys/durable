How to prepare hockey highlights, full shifts and game video for European clubs

A coach decides in seconds whether to keep watching. Here is how to structure a highlight video and a full-game link so the first look turns into a real evaluation.

Short answer

Send a highlight video of about three to four minutes that tells the coach who you are in the first five seconds and shows your strongest shifts in your main role within the first thirty. Add a link to at least one recent full game, host both on an open link, and send them with your hockey resume. The structure below is EHA's recommendation based on how clubs screen profiles, not a rule set by any league.

1. What a coach checks first

Before judging the hockey, a coach needs four answers: who the player is, which position and role, which league and season the footage comes from, and how to find the player in the frame. If any of these takes effort, the video is often closed before the good shifts start.

2. Length and order: an example opening

About three to four minutes is enough; a longer compilation is less likely to be watched to the end. The example below is a recommended structure, not a requirement:

3. Make yourself easy to find

  • Mark yourself for about a second before each clip: a circle, an arrow or a short freeze frame.
  • Put your jersey number and colour on the title card and keep them consistent.
  • Start each clip a few seconds before your action, so the coach sees how the play develops.

4. What to show by position

Forwards:

  • forecheck and puck recovery;
  • puck protection and play along the boards;
  • passes that create chances, not only finishes;
  • defensive-zone coverage and backchecking;
  • faceoffs, if you take them.

Defencemen:

  • first pass out of the zone under pressure;
  • gap control and one-on-one defending;
  • retrievals and decisions behind the net;
  • net-front play and blocked shots;
  • offensive-zone activation and shots that get through.

Goalies:

  • saves through traffic and on screens;
  • rebound control;
  • lateral movement and recovery;
  • puck handling;
  • different types of saves, not only the spectacular ones.

5. Footage quality

  • A stable wide broadcast or arena camera that shows the whole play is more useful than shaky phone footage from the stands.
  • Aim for at least 720p.
  • No music and no effects. Use slow motion sparingly: coaches judge real game speed.
  • Use footage from the latest completed or current season. Older video needs an explanation.
  • Choose games against credible opposition, especially if you target a stronger adult league.

6. The full-game link

Highlights show peaks; a full game shows shift-to-shift habits, play away from the puck, recovery after mistakes and late-game decisions. When a club is seriously interested, it will usually ask for unedited footage.

  • Prepare one or two recent full games, unedited, with ordinary broadcast sound.
  • Pick games where you had substantial ice time in a representative role.
  • Write your jersey number and colour in the description; listing the start time of each period helps.

Full shifts: the format clubs ask for after the highlights

A full-shift video is every shift of one game, cut together without the time on the bench — usually 12–20 minutes for a skater. It shows what a highlight reel hides: how you get into position, how you skate when you do not have the puck and what you do after a mistake. Many coaches watch it before they ask for a full game. Make one for your best recent game and one for a game against strong opposition.

  • Keep every shift in order, including the quiet ones; do not cut the shift when you lose the puck.
  • Add a small marker on yourself at the start of each shift if the camera is wide.
  • Write the game, date, score and your jersey number in the first frame.
  • Goaltenders: send the full game instead — workload and recovery between shots are the point.

Ten-point error check before sending

  1. The first five seconds do not say who you are.
  2. Goals only — no defensive or off-puck clips.
  3. Clips older than the last two seasons at the front.
  4. Music or effects over game sound.
  5. Slow-motion replays of the same play.
  6. Footage that cannot show which player you are.
  7. Only weak opponents while you aim at a stronger league.
  8. A private link, an expired link or a download that needs an account.
  9. No full game or full-shift link at the end.
  10. Statistics in the video that do not match Elite Prospects.

Not sure which level your video should be aimed at? Take the EHA Level Calculator: it gives an indicative league range in two minutes. It does not check citizenship, age rules or registration, so treat the result as preliminary.

7. Hosting, file naming and link check

  • YouTube (unlisted), Veo or Hudl can work. The link must open without a password, login or access request.
  • Name files and videos in one pattern, for example Surname_Firstname_2007_D_2025-26_Highlights and …_FullGame_vs_Opponent.
  • Open each link in a private browser window and on a phone before sending.
  • Do not delete, replace or make private a video after you have sent the link: clubs may open it weeks later.
  • Place the same links in your hockey resume and check that the name, birth year and season match your Elite Prospects profile.

8. What to send with the video

Video works together with a profile. Send a short resume with position, birth year, height and weight, the leagues and statistics by season, your Elite Prospects link and a contact.

For players under 18, the contact is a parent, guardian or agent. Do not put a minor's personal phone number, email or social accounts in the video, its title or a public description.

9. Common mistakes

  • Only goals, with no defensive or off-puck work.
  • No title card, so the coach cannot tell who to watch.
  • Footage only against weak opposition while targeting a stronger league.
  • A private link or one that asks for access.
  • A long compilation with music, effects and repeated replays.
  • A profile sent with a promise that video will come later.

10. Checklist before sending

  • Title card with name, birth year, position, shooting side, size, team, league, season, number and colour.
  • Strongest relevant shifts in the first 30 seconds.
  • About three to four minutes, no music.
  • Player marked before each clip.
  • Clips that match your position, from the current or last season.
  • At least one full-game link.
  • Links open without login, checked on a phone.
  • File names follow one pattern.
  • Resume and Elite Prospects link attached; no minor's personal contacts.

11. Next step

Video is only useful when it reaches a club that needs your position and level. Check your realistic level with the level calculator and read about assessing league level and the club search process. FAQ

How long should a hockey highlight video be?

About three to four minutes. Put your title card in the first five seconds and your strongest relevant shifts within the first thirty.

Do I still need a full game if I have highlights?

Yes. Highlights earn attention; a full game lets a coach check habits, play away from the puck and consistency. Clubs that are seriously interested usually ask for one.

Can I add music or slow motion?

Leave out music and effects. Use slow motion sparingly, if at all: coaches want to judge real game speed.

Is phone footage good enough?

Only if it is stable, wide enough to show the play and at least 720p. A league broadcast or arena camera is usually better.

Should my contact details be in the video?

Put a contact on the last frame or in the resume: the player's own for adults, a parent, guardian or agent for players under 18. Never publish a minor's personal phone, email or social accounts.
