Clubs · adaptation

An import player's first two weeks at the club: adaptation checklist

Housing, equipment, schedule, language, role and coach feedback, stage by stage — with a printable checklist.

Checklist · Printable version · Club request

Short answer

An import's first two weeks decide much of his season. Prepare housing, equipment, schedule and paperwork before he lands; in the first 48 hours cover the basics of daily life and a first talk about his role; in week one put the schedule, team rules and language in writing; in week two review the role and give video-based feedback. The printable checklist below covers all four stages.

The checklist by stage

Before arrival

  • Flat ready: keys, bedding, kitchen basics, Wi-Fi, address sent in advance
  • Airport pick-up arranged with a named person and phone
  • Registration, ITC and permit status confirmed; insurance active from the first practice
  • Equipment list agreed: what the club provides, sizes, sticks
  • First-week schedule sent: practices, gym, games, team events
  • Contacts: team manager, coach, doctor or physio, an English- or Russian-speaking teammate

First 48 hours

  • Welcome and tour: rink, locker room, gym, medical room
  • Local SIM or eSIM, bank or payment card, transport or car handover
  • Town registration or residence steps that must happen early
  • Grocery shop and the route to the rink
  • Short meeting with the coach: expected role, line, special teams

Week 1

  • Daily schedule in writing in a language the player understands
  • Equipment fitted; skates and sticks checked
  • Team rules: fines, travel, dress code, off-ice obligations
  • Language: key hockey terms and staff names
  • Medical baseline with the team physio
  • End-of-week check-in: what is going well, what is missing

Week 2

  • Role review with the coach after the first games: minutes, line, special teams
  • Video session on 3–5 clips from the player's games
  • Living check: flat, transport, food, loneliness
  • Paperwork follow-up: residence, tax number, bank, insurance
  • Agree the next feedback date

Feedback that works

Players adapt faster when they hear early and concretely what the coach wants: two or three points, shown on video, with a date for the next talk. For the player's side of adaptation see homesickness and adaptation abroad; for a trial period see the trial checklist.

Frequently asked questions

What should a club prepare before an import arrives?

Housing, airport pick-up, registration and permit status, insurance from the first practice, equipment and the first-week schedule.

When should the coach talk about the player's role?

In the first 48 hours, and again after the first games in week two.
