# School and education for a foreign junior hockey player: fact-check

Checked 15 October 2026. This is an editorial record, not legal advice. It extends the per-country junior fact-checks, which already record how each country's hockey-school structures work (Sweden NIU and LIP, Finland urheilulukio, Czechia ad hoc partnerships and Sportovní gymnázium Pardubice, Slovakia CTM and ÚTM with dormitories, Germany Eliteschule and Bavaria's PZW, Switzerland EVZ Academy and Kunst und Sportschule Zürich). Those structures are not repeated here. This record covers what they leave open: whether a foreign minor may legally attend, what school documents are needed, and what online study can and cannot do.

Confidence key: **DIRECT** = the primary page was fetched and read. **SEARCH** = only a search summary of the page was available (direct fetch blocked or not attempted); re-check before publishing. **CONFLICT** = sources disagree.

## 1. Sweden: residence permit for upper secondary school (DIRECT)

Migrationsverket, "Other studies and exchange studies at upper secondary school" (no last-updated date shown; most recent news item on the site 1 October 2026):

- "You can only get a residence permit for upper secondary school studies if it is an IB (International Baccalaureate) programme," or "an international exchange arranged through an organization that promotes international student exchange." You cannot get a permit for studies at compulsory-school level or independent language studies.
- "You cannot be granted a residence permit for distance learning as a form of study." Studies must be full time (normally at least 20 teaching hours per week) and the applicant must be finally admitted.
- Under 18: written consent of parents or legal guardians (the agency provides a form), a birth record naming the parents, an ID copy for each guardian, and an accommodation letter. Exchange students under 18 enclose a host-family letter; otherwise the letter is written by the guardians and describes the person the student will live with.
- Money: at least SEK 10,656 per month for applications in 2026 (SEK 10,584 in 2025); an under-18 applicant may rely on the parents' or guardians' accounts; private individuals cannot sponsor. Application fee for under 18: SEK 750.
- First application must be made from outside Sweden. Maximum permit length one year. The permit does not give the right to work. Documents in Swedish or English, with certified translations.

**What this does and does not establish.** The page lists IB and exchange as the only upper secondary routes. It does not mention hockey programmes such as NIU or LIP, so whether a non-EU minor can be admitted to one of those under another route could not be confirmed. A page may say that admission to a school and immigration permission are separate checks (as `sweden-junior-factcheck.md` already does) and that the permit categories listed by Migrationsverket for upper secondary school are IB and exchange. It must not say that non-EU players can or cannot attend NIU. EU/EEA citizens are outside this page and their position was not researched.

## 2. Finland: tuition fees for non-EU upper secondary students from 1 August 2026

Verified 15 October 2026 against the adopted law texts. The Ministry of Education and Culture (okm.fi) and Migri (migri.fi) sit behind a bot check that blocks automated access, and no attempt was made to bypass it; the primary texts below were read from Parliament and from the government bill database instead.

**DIRECT, Parliament's reply EV 158/2025 vp (HE 143/2025 vp, committee report SiVM 11/2025 vp), dated 10 December 2025.** Parliament adopted the bill. The adopted texts, quoted in Finnish with translation:

- **Lukiolaki (714/2018) new 34 a §.** "Koulutuksen järjestäjän on perittävä kolmannen maan kansalaiselta lukuvuosimaksu ... opiskelijalta, joka hakee oleskelulupaa opiskelun perusteella tai oleskelee maassa opiskelun perusteella myönnetyllä oleskeluluvalla." The education provider must charge an annual tuition fee to a third-country national admitted to the studies who applies for a study-based residence permit or lives in Finland on one. "Lukuvuosimaksu peritään myös kolmannen maan kansalaiselta, joka suorittaa tässä laissa tarkoitettuja opintoja etäopetuksessa": the fee is also charged to a third-country national who studies at a distance. "Lukuvuosimaksun suuruus on vähintään lukiokoulutuksen keskimääräinen yksikköhinta" (at least the average unit price of upper secondary education; if the provider's own unit price is lower, at least that). "Lukuvuosimaksun suorittaminen on edellytys opetukseen osallistumiselle": paying is a condition of attending teaching. The fee is set on the unit price of the first study year for the whole programme. Exemptions in this section: students in commissioned training, and students on a temporary permit of up to one year for an exchange programme. The section contains no exemption for persons of compulsory-education age.
- **Oppivelvollisuuslaki (1214/2020) 2 §.** A child is subject to compulsory education if permanently resident in Finland, which means having a municipality of residence (kotikunta) there. "Kolmansien maiden kansalaiset, jotka oleskelevat Suomessa opiskelutarkoituksessa, eivät kuitenkaan ole Suomessa oppivelvollisia": third-country nationals staying in Finland for study are not subject to compulsory education.
- **Act 719/2018 (third-country nationals' entry and residence for study) 7 a §.** A national study residence permit for an institution other than a higher-education institution is granted if the study "johtaa tutkintoon tai ammattiin" (leads to a degree or a profession) and the applicant "on suorittanut koulutuksen järjestäjän vaatimat maksut" (has paid the fees the education provider requires). So paying the fee is a condition of the permit.
- **Entry into force.** The adopted texts leave the date blank ("päivänä kuuta 20 "). The fee provisions apply to students who start studies after entry into force. The government bill proposes 1 August 2026, and the Espoo page and ministry summaries state 1 August 2026.

**DIRECT, government bill HE 143/2025 vp, read in the Edilex database (explanatory section 4.1.2).** The bill's own explanation says persons of compulsory-education age (under 18) would not be liable, but "alle 18-vuotiaat, jotka ovat tulleet Suomeen opiskelijan oleskeluluvalla" (under-18s who came to Finland on a student residence permit) would still be liable. Read with the adopted oppivelvollisuuslaki 2 §, this means: a minor who comes to Finland specifically to study on a student permit is outside compulsory education and is fee-liable; a minor who lives in Finland with family on a family-based permit and has a municipality of residence is not on a study-based permit and is not the target of the fee. This is the bill's explanatory text, not a quoted section of the law, so a page should attribute it that way.

**DIRECT, Espoo city (a provider, not the rule-maker):** same scope; exempt are EU/EEA/Swiss citizens, permanent residence permit holders and exchange students; students who started before 1 August 2026 do not pay; persons coming from abroad to study "may only apply for a residence permit after the tuition fee has been paid"; learning materials, school meals and matriculation fees are charged separately. Its fee for Espoo is EUR 10,300 a year (30,900 over three years).

**Not confirmed:**
- The fee amount. The law sets a floor, not a figure. Search summaries of the ministry page give EUR 8,953.47 as the average unit price for 2026; Espoo's page gives about EUR 8,500 as its unit price and EUR 10,300 as its total fee. Fees vary by provider. A page must not publish a single figure.
- How Migri treats an application for a lukio place (whether lukio "leads to a degree or a profession" under 7 a §), the means requirement (reported as EUR 800 a month), the insurance requirement and the in-person submission rule for under-18s. These come only from search summaries of migri.fi and remain unconfirmed.
- Whether an urheilulukio is treated differently from any other lukio. Nothing in the adopted texts mentions sport schools, so the fee provisions as written apply to lukio education generally.
- How the vocational (ammatillinen) provision differs: its exemption list includes a person of compulsory-education age ("oppivelvollisuuslain 2 §:ssä tarkoitettu oppivelvollinen"), which the lukiolaki provision does not contain.

## 3. Czechia: visa for a minor attending a secondary school (SEARCH)

Czech embassy and consulate pages (several posts, no Ministry of Interior page returned; requirements vary by post and some pages are dated):

- A secondary-school student not on an exchange programme applies for a long-term visa for study, not a residence permit.
- Written consent of both parents (typically) for a minor, translated into Czech, with signatures notarised if not signed at the consulate. Applications for children under 15 can be filed by a guardian.
- Czech-language confirmation of enrolment from the school; proof of accommodation (a document from the school confirming accommodation, or a lease or similar agreement); travel medical insurance; documents generally in Czech and not older than 180 days.
- Funds: reported as 15 times the existential minimum, with half that amount for under 18; one post's figure of CZK 3,130 a month may be out of date.
- No Czech-specific hockey or sport-school visa found. The page should not state amounts, and should send families to the consulate for their country of residence.

## 4. Switzerland, Germany, Slovakia, Austria

- **Switzerland, SEARCH:** nothing found on how a non-EU minor obtains a permit for a sports school. General student-permit requirements reported by SEM sources: admission to a recognised institution, sufficient funds, accident and health insurance, a study plan. SEM recommends a parental consent declaration (no special form; German, French, Italian or English) for minors entering Switzerland. Cantonal migration offices issue permits. Not enough to publish minor-specific rules.
- **Germany:** already recorded in `germany-junior-factcheck.md`: BAMF cites §16f AufenthG (school attendance, including boarding school); notarised consent for a minor travelling without both parents; the §80 Abs. 4 AufenthG custodian designation needs a second verification pass.
- **Slovakia and Austria:** existing records cover school structures and Austria's pupil permit (fee EUR 218, means threshold EUR 1,308.39 a month, 2026); no further school-admission research here.

## 5. Online school and distance learning

- **DIRECT, Sweden:** no residence permit for distance learning (section 1).
- **DIRECT, Finland:** the adopted lukiolaki 34 a § charges the fee also to a third-country national who studies at a distance (section 2).
- **Existing record, USA:** `usa-junior-factcheck.md` notes that an F-1 student visa is tied to a SEVP-certified school and that homeschool or fully online study does not qualify (secondary summaries; USCIS and State Department pages could not be fetched).
- **NCAA:** the NCAA international page fetched here does not mention online or distance courses. Whether online courses count toward core-course credit for a given player was therefore not confirmed; the Eligibility Center decides.
- Net position for a page: online study is not a confirmed basis for a residence permit in any country researched, and the one explicit statement (Sweden) rules it out. Do not present an online school as a way around a school requirement.

## 6. Diplomas and academic eligibility for the NCAA

**DIRECT**, NCAA.org, International Student Initial Eligibility Requirements (no date; footer 2026):

- Division I: 16 NCAA-approved core-course credits before the expected date of secondary-school graduation and a minimum 2.3 core-course GPA. Division II: 16 credits and a 2.2 GPA. Division III schools set their own academic standards; the Eligibility Center certifies athletics eligibility only. Students with solely international credentials are not required to meet the 10/7 progression rule; students who completed any secondary education at a US school must.
- Documents: official transcripts from year nine up in the native language, proof of graduation as defined for the country, and "a complete, word-for-word certified English translation" for non-English records. Possible additional documents: year-eight transcript, diploma or graduation certificate, grading scale with the minimum passing grade, course completion dates. Students' own emailed records are not accepted; the school must send them.
- Which system applies: use the requirements for the educational system the student is completing, not the school's location (a French diploma earned in Venezuela uses France's requirements). GCE, GCSE and IGCSE generally use the England and Wales requirements.
- Country crediting is in country-specific PDF profiles, updated on a rolling basis. The 2025-26 international guide could not be fetched (connection reset), so the entries for Sweden, Finland, Germany, Czechia, Slovakia, Switzerland and Austria were not read. Do not state how any country's upper secondary or sport-school track is credited.
