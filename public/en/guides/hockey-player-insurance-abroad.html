Import player insurance: what the club covers and what the player may pay

A checklist to use with your contract and the policy in front of you: practices and games, sport exclusions, deductible, treatment, rehabilitation, pay during injury.

Checklist · Who pays · After an injury · Next step

Short answer

Before you sign, find out in writing which policy covers you for practices and games, from which date, and what it excludes. Many ordinary travel and health policies exclude professional or contact sport, so a player who relies on his own travel insurance can be left without cover after a hockey injury. The club's contract and the actual policy wording decide who pays for treatment, rehabilitation and your salary while injured — not a verbal promise.

Use the checklist below with the club's contract and the policy document in front of you. If the club cannot name the insurer and send the conditions, treat the cover as unconfirmed.

What the club usually covers and what the player may pay

There is no single European rule. In some countries an employed athlete is covered by statutory health and accident insurance through employment; at semi-professional levels many clubs use a group policy, and some expect the player to arrange cover. The answer for you is in two documents: your contract and the policy conditions.

  • EU/EEA citizens: a European Health Insurance Card covers necessary public healthcare during a temporary stay, but it does not replace employment-based insurance or cover private treatment.
  • Non-EU players: the residence permit application usually requires proof of health insurance; check that the policy you buy for the permit does not exclude hockey.
  • Equipment and dental: ask separately; these are common gaps.

What to do if you are injured

  1. Tell the team doctor or physiotherapist at once and get the injury written down with the date and the game or practice.
  2. Ask the club which insurer to notify and who files the claim; note the claim number.
  3. Keep every medical report, invoice and prescription.
  4. Follow the treating doctor's plan; return-to-play decisions are medical decisions.
  5. Check your contract for pay during injury and the review dates.

Frequently asked questions

Does a European hockey club insure its import players?

Often, but not always and not in the same way. Ask for the insurer's name and the policy conditions before signing.

Is my travel insurance enough for hockey?

Usually not: many travel policies exclude professional or contact sport. Check the exclusions.

Who pays for surgery after a hockey injury abroad?

The policy and your contract decide. Check treatment caps, deductible and rehabilitation before you sign.

Do I keep my salary while injured?

Only if your contract says so. Look for pay during injury, its duration and what happens if the season ends.
