# Czechia junior hockey fact-check

Checked 9 August 2026 against official sources. This is an editorial record, not legal advice.

## Verified points for 2026/27

- Český svaz ledního hokeje's (ČSLH) "Všeobecná ustanovení...sezóna 2026-2027" (consolidated text, published 30 July 2026), §2, citing čl. 217 odst. 1 of the Soutěžní a disciplinární řád, define age categories by exact birth-date range: junioři (≈U20) = born 1 January 2007 – 31 December 2008; starší dorostenci (≈U18) = born 1 January 2009 – 31 December 2010; mladší dorostenci (≈U16, new tier for 2026/27) = born 2011 only.
- Top junior competitions are the Komerční banka extraliga juniorů plus Liga juniorů (≈U20), and the CCM extraliga staršího dorostu plus Liga staršího dorostu (≈U18); a new mladší dorostenci (≈U16) extraliga/liga bridge tier starts in 2026/27.
- ČSLH's transfer rules for a club registering a player arriving from abroad (Art. 10 of the extraliga/I.liga transfer rules) simply require a transfer card completed per IIHF regulations, where IIHF regulations require one — **no additional, Czech-specific restriction on the international transfer of a minor was found**, beyond the domestic rule that players under 15 need a notarised legal-guardian signature on transfer forms.
- The underlying IIHF International Transfer Regulations (effective 28 September 2024, as hosted officially by ČSLH), Section I.5, state that a player under 18 may transfer at any time with no transfer fee, and that the general transfer-deadline window does not apply to under-18 transfers. In practice this makes an under-18 international transfer procedurally *less* restricted than an adult's — no fee, no deadline window, and (per what was found) no Czech-specific guardian-relocation or highest-level-play condition layered on top, unlike Finland or Sweden respectively.
- The domestic transfer window runs 1 May to 31 January of the season; the IIHF international window for Czechia (Northern Hemisphere) runs 1 July–15 February, explicitly waived for under-18 transfers per I.5.2.
- ČSLH's own parent-facing material states directly that combining school and elite hockey is **not systemically organised** in Czechia, unlike some other countries: primary level has club-linked "sportovní třídy," secondary level relies on ad hoc club-"academy" partnerships (example given: Bílí Tygři Liberec), and a "Univerzitní liga" exists at university level. One concrete named example: Sportovní gymnázium Pardubice runs an ice-hockey specialisation with its own talent exam.
- No official ČSLH housing/billeting policy for junior players was found. Sportovní gymnázium Pardubice has a general student dormitory ("Domov mládeže") open to any enrolled student — a standard school dorm, not a hockey- or federation-specific scheme.
- Czechia is EU/Schengen; standard EU/EEA-vs-non-EU immigration framework applies. The Interior Ministry's visa-purpose code table lists "Sportovní" (code 10) as an eligible purpose for a long-term visa; no distinct minor's-sport-visa procedure beyond universal minor rules (guardian applies on the child's behalf; under-12s exempt from fingerprinting) was found.
- The Národní sportovní agentura's national ethics code requires each sport federation to appoint a "Svazový safeguard" (federation-level safeguarding officer). Whether ČSLH has published its own child-safeguarding policy or named this officer could not be confirmed from a primary source — only general awareness content (a June 2026 conference item on "safe space" in hockey/figure skating) was found.

## Publication note

The genuinely reassuring finding here — that Czechia adds no extra national restriction to an under-18 international transfer beyond the (already lighter) IIHF baseline — should still be presented cautiously: absence of a found restriction is not the same as an official confirmation that none exists. Retain the disclaimer to verify current rules with ČSLH, IIHF, the club, the Interior Ministry and the consulate. Do not present school placement, housing or a safeguarding contact as guaranteed, since the education and safeguarding pictures are explicitly ad hoc.
