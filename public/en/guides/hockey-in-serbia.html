Hockey in Serbia: leagues, work permits and transfers

A cautious guide for players and families: the domestic championship and the regional IHL, realistic level, work-permit rules for a non-EU market, IIHF transfer and tryouts.

01

How hockey in Serbia is structured

The Serbian Ice Hockey Federation (Savez hokeja na ledu Srbije), an IIHF member since 2007, runs a domestic senior championship, a second domestic tier, youth categories from U12 to U20, and women's hockey. Serbia's strongest senior clubs — Crvena zvezda, Partizan and Vojvodina — currently compete in the International Hockey League (IHL), a regional competition alongside Slovenian and Croatian clubs, run by the Slovenian Ice Hockey Federation rather than by Serbia's own federation.

This is a small, developing market. Sources disagree on the exact current club count for the purely domestic championship, so the federation's own competitions page should be checked directly before assuming a specific number of teams.

For a foreign player, the practical target is more likely one of the three Serbian clubs playing in the IHL than the purely domestic championship, simply because it is the higher and more visible level. Ask any club to confirm which competition it is actually registered for this season — a "Serbian club" can mean either route.

03

Youth hockey

The federation publishes U12, U14, U15, U16, U18 and U20 categories, and Serbian clubs can also enter an IHL-linked U19 regional junior competition. Depth and current activity at each age group were not independently verified — confirm with the federation before treating a youth category as an active, regularly competing group.

For an international junior, ask the club to confirm the team, role, education, housing, guardian arrangements where required, insurance and international transfer in writing. A camp invitation is not a roster place.

04

Choosing a realistic level

Clubs assess previous league strength, skating, decision speed, physical readiness, play away from the puck, role, special teams, recent video and verifiable statistics. In a small market, ask specifically which of the two routes (IHL club or domestic championship club) is offering the place, since the two differ meaningfully in level and visibility.

05

Import limits

No specific published limit on foreign players could be confirmed for either the domestic championship or the regional IHL from the federation's available material.

Before accepting: ask the club for the current roster rule in writing and confirm your passport and registration history are eligible under it — do not assume a number that applies elsewhere in the Balkans also applies here.
06

Contract, salary and conditions

No official salary information is published for Serbian hockey, and the market's small club base points to a semi-professional character rather than confirmed full-time professional pay. Put the following in writing before signing:

  • gross or net amount, currency, payment dates, bonuses and taxes;
  • housing, deposit, utilities, meals and transport;
  • equipment, sticks, medical care and insurance;
  • tryout, injury, termination and national-team release terms;
  • who pays for the work permit, IIHF transfer and federation fees.

Verify the legal entity and signatory authority. Verbal promises do not replace a signed agreement.

07

Housing and costs

No published cost-of-living data specific to hockey clubs was found. Ask for the address, rent, deposit, utilities, furniture, arena transport and move-in dates directly, and calculate the amount left after taxes and compulsory expenses before treating any figure as reliable.

08

Work and residence: a non-EU market

Serbia is not an EU or EEA member. Unlike many guides on this site, there is no EU-citizenship shortcut here: an EU passport does not exempt a player from Serbia's normal work-authorisation process. Visa-free short-stay entry, available to many nationalities including EU citizens for up to 90 days in a 180-day period, covers travel only and does not include a right to work.

The standard route is a single permit combining temporary residence and work authorisation ("jedinstvena dozvola"), filed through Serbia's Foreigners Portal, involving the employer, a labour-market step through the National Employment Service, and a decision from the Ministry of Interior. The governing rule is the Rulebook on Single Permit Issuance for Foreign Nationals' Temporary Residence and Work (Official Gazette No. 6/2024). A permit can run up to 36 months; processing time should be confirmed directly rather than assumed.

Important: a club invitation, tryout or hockey registration does not itself authorise paid work in Serbia.
09

IIHF transfer, federation registration and insurance

  1. International transfer: a move from another national federation requires an IIHF international transfer.
  2. Registration: the club must confirm deadlines and eligibility for the exact competition — domestic championship or IHL.
  3. Work authorisation: sporting eligibility does not replace the single residence-and-work permit described above.
  4. Insurance: confirm cover for tryouts, training, games, injury and treatment.
10

Player documents

  • valid passport;
  • signed agreement or official club letter;
  • work-permit and residence documents once the route is confirmed;
  • last hockey registration and information for the IIHF transfer;
  • medical records and insurance;
  • for a minor: consent, guardian, school and housing arrangements.

The final list is set by the club, federation, IIHF and Serbian state authorities. Do not buy a non-refundable ticket before entry and documentation are confirmed.

11

Recruitment timing and tryouts

No official recruitment calendar was found for Serbian clubs. Given the market's small size, early contact and an exact role fit likely matter more than timing a message to a specific window — but this is orientation, not a confirmed rule.

Before a tryout, confirm dates, format, costs, insurance, housing, contact person, decision criteria and response deadline. Use the EHA tryout checklist.

What is the top level of hockey in Serbia?

Serbia's strongest clubs play in the regional International Hockey League (IHL) alongside Slovenian and Croatian clubs, administered by the Slovenian Ice Hockey Federation. A separate domestic championship also exists; its current club count varies by source and should be checked with the Serbian federation.

Is there a published import-player limit?

No specific numeric limit could be confirmed from the federation's published material. Ask the club for the current roster rule in writing before relying on it.

Does an EU passport simplify working in Serbia?

No. Serbia is not an EU member, and EU citizens must obtain the same single residence-and-work permit as any other foreign national. Visa-free short-stay entry is a separate matter and does not include a right to work.

Is an IIHF international transfer required?

Yes, when a player moves from a club or federation in another country.
