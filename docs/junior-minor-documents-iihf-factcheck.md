# Documents for a minor and the IIHF transfer steps with dates: fact-check

Checked 15 October 2026. This is an editorial record, not legal advice. It extends the existing record in `junior-pathway-compare-factcheck.md` and the per-country junior fact-checks, and it complements `junior-schools-education-factcheck.md` (school access and permits) and `junior-calendar-factcheck.md` (windows by month). It does not repeat their content.

Confidence key: **DIRECT** = a primary page or document was read in this pass. **RECORD** = taken from an existing fact-check in this folder, with its own check date. **SEARCH** = only a search summary was available; re-check before publishing.

## 1. The IIHF International Transfer Regulations (DIRECT)

Source: IIHF International Transfer Regulations, "These Regulations come into effect on 6th October 2025" (20 pages, the 2026 file on blob.iihf.com). Text extracted and read in full for Part I and Part II appeals. Article numbers below are from that document.

**Who does what.** The MNA (national association) of the club that wants to register the player submits an online Transfer request to the player's Current MNA through the IIHF Online Transfer System (art. I.3.1). Players do not file it themselves. Any player who wants to take part in a competition under another MNA's jurisdiction needs a Transfer (I.2.1).

**The clock, in order:**

1. **Day 0, request.** The new club's MNA submits the request in the System (I.3.1).
2. **Within 7 days, answer.** The "Deadline" is seven days after the System issues the request notice to the Current MNA, or any extension of it (I.1.1(c)). The Current MNA must approve or refuse before it lapses, and may ask the IIHF to extend if there are reasonable grounds (I.3.2).
3. **If no answer, automatic approval.** "If the Transfer is not approved or refused before the Deadline lapses, the Transfer will automatically be approved" (I.3.3).
4. **Approval date.** The transfer is approved on the date the new MNA receives the confirmation from the System. The player is under the new MNA's jurisdiction from that date, may only start to play for the new club when the transfer is approved, and must stop playing for the former club from that date (I.3.4).
5. **If refused.** The Current MNA may refuse only if the player has a professional contract with a club in that MNA, has not fulfilled contractual obligations to the former club, has not met financial commitments to it (debts, unreturned equipment), or there is a material reason between the clubs other than compensation such as a confirmed suspension or pending disciplinary proceeding (I.3.5). It must state its reasons and upload the evidence. The new MNA tells the club and player (I.3.6).
6. **Appeal of a refusal.** The new MNA may appeal on the player's behalf within 7 days of the refusal notice, with documents translated into English; the Current MNA then has 7 days to respond; the IIHF decides on written submissions only and approves if no ground in I.3.5 exists (II.1.2 to II.1.6). A decision of the IIHF can be appealed to the IIHF Disciplinary Board within 7 days, with a CHF 1,000 fee, and the decision stays in effect during the appeal (II.1.7 to II.1.9).
7. **Extraordinary case.** The IIHF can approve the transfer of a documented refugee or asylee without the old MNA's approval (I.3.8, I.3.9). Not relevant to an ordinary move.

**Players under 18 (art. I.5):**
- "A Player under 18 years of age is permitted to Transfer from one MNA to another utilizing the System. No Transfer fee shall apply for a Transfer completed before the Player's 18th birthday" (I.5.1).
- "The Transfer Deadline indicated in Article I.4.8 shall not apply to the Transfers of Players under 18 years of age" (I.5.2). The general Northern Hemisphere window runs from 00.01h CET on 1 July to 23.59h CET on 15 February, moves to the following Monday if the date falls on a weekend, and may be extended in a Winter Olympic year (I.4.8). This confirms the waiver already recorded in the country files.
- The regulations contain **no rule on parents, guardians, relocation or school** for a minor (a keyword check for guardian, parent, minor and school found none beyond the "Education Agreement" definition). Those requirements are national (section 2).

**Contract age (art. I.8.7 and I.8.8):** the IIHF views 18 as the legal age of majority for contract signing unless national law shows otherwise. Contracts for players under 18 are permitted only if consistent with national laws, and a player under 18 "may not sign a Contract for a term longer than three years"; a longer clause is not recognised. "Contract" includes an Education Agreement (an education, hockey-school or amateur contract, I.1.1(a) and (d)).

**Limited and unlimited transfers (I.4):** a limited transfer lasts for the duration of the new contract and a new transfer is needed to extend it (I.4.2, I.4.4). A permanent unlimited transfer requires an executed Unlimited Transfer Request Form and makes the player a full member of the new MNA (I.4.7). A change between the two within two months of approval needs no new request (I.4.6).

**Try-outs (I.10):** the current MNA may give written permission for exhibition games during a try-out of up to 15 days from the first game; beyond that a new written permission is needed (I.10.1, I.10.2). During the try-out the player is under the jurisdiction of the MNA where he plays.

**Fees (I.11):** the IIHF Transfer Fee is a fixed fee in the Guidelines paid by the new MNA; the current MNA may not charge more than CHF 300 for approval; the new MNA may charge a registration or licensing fee proportionate to its services; MNAs can agree to waive their transfer fees but not the IIHF's (I.11.1, I.11.2). For a transfer completed before the player turns 18, art. I.5.1 states that no transfer fee applies. Whether a national registration or licensing fee can still be charged to a minor was not confirmed.

**Not in the document:** the Online Transfer System Guidelines (which set the IIHF fee and the exact items a club must upload) are described as published on the IIHF intranet and were not read. A page must not say what documents the club's MNA uploads, or what the IIHF fee is.

## 2. National layer for a minor (RECORD)

Taken as recorded; see each file for sources and check dates.
- **Sweden** (`sweden-junior-factcheck.md`, 3 August 2026): SIF's under-18 rule requires documentation from the home federation that the player is at the highest level or nationally relevant, written documentation of a guardian or parent-appointed representative, and access to civil education or vocational training; an unlimited transfer is not allowed in the normal case and the transfer runs at most to the playing year in which the player turns 18; general window for a move from a foreign club 1 June to 31 January.
- **Finland** (`finland-junior-factcheck.md`, 9 August 2026): SJL §6.7 requires the guardian to relocate to Finland permanently with a player under 18 who transfers permanently, with proof attached to the application.
- **Germany** (9 August 2026): for a player not yet 18 the formal Transferkarte is not required; a written or electronic confirmation from the former federation suffices (DEB Spielordnung Art. 52a). The Art. 55 domestic window and the custodian-designation rule in §80 Abs. 4 AufenthG need a second check.
- **Czechia** (9 August 2026): no additional Czech restriction on an international transfer of a minor found, beyond the IIHF baseline; absence of a restriction found is not confirmation that none exists.
- **Slovakia** (14 August 2026): SZĽH requires the legal guardian's consent to a minor's transfer as co-signatory, not relocation.
- **Austria** (14 August 2026): domestic licensing 1 June to 15 February regardless of age; "minor" for registration means not yet 18 by 31 December of the federation year.
- **Switzerland** (9 August 2026): no Transferkarte for minors; a 2016 work-permit directive is flagged as dated.

## 3. Immigration and travel documents: what has dates

- **Sweden, DIRECT (Migrationsverket "Waiting times" page, no update date shown).** For "other studies and exchange studies at upper secondary school" the processing time is 3 months for a first application and 7 months for an extension. The figure is the time within which 75% of cases decided in the last 12 months were decided; if too few cases were decided, none is reported. Related requirements are in `junior-schools-education-factcheck.md`: first application from outside Sweden, SEK 10,656 a month for 2026 applications, permit for at most one year, parental consent form, accommodation letter.
- **Canada, DIRECT (IRCC Guide 5269, "Applying for a Study Permit outside Canada", dated 6 October 2026).** Children 17 years of age or less coming to study without an accompanying parent or legal guardian need a custodian in Canada. Minors between 17 and the provincial age of majority (18 or 19) "may need a custodian at the discretion of an immigration officer." The custodian must reside in Canada and be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident 19 or older. The parents or legal guardians in the country of origin and the custodian must complete a notarised Custodianship Declaration (IMM 5646); pages 1 and 2 are required with the application. Custodianship is not mandatory for a minor travelling with one parent or an authorised guardian, but signed consent from the non-accompanying parent "should be provided." The page gives no processing time specific to custodianship and says processing varies by visa office. **This resolves the open item in `canada-junior-factcheck.md`**, which listed the custodianship requirement as unconfirmed against IRCC. Search summaries showed other IRCC documents using "under 17" and "under 18"; the guide above is the one that was read. The study-permit rule for programmes over six months is still as recorded in the Canada file.
- **Czechia, SEARCH (embassy and consulate pages).** The legal processing time for a long-term residence permit for study is stated as 60 days on one post's page. A secondary-school student not on exchange applies for a long-term visa for study. Consent of both parents (typically), documents not older than 180 days, and a Czech-language enrolment confirmation are required. Posts differ.
- **Travel inside the EU, DIRECT (Your Europe, "Documents for minors travelling in the EU", last checked 14 September 2026).** "There are no EU rules on this matter"; each EU country decides whether it requires an official authorisation; a child travelling alone, with adults who are not legal guardians, or with only one parent "may need an extra (official) document signed by their parents"; transit countries may ask for it; airlines have their own forms and should be checked before departure; rules may change without notice.
- **Germany, RECORD.** BAMF cites §16f AufenthG (school attendance) for a non-EU minor in a club-linked boarding school; a notarised consent for a minor travelling without both parents is understood to be needed.
- **Austria, RECORD.** Pupil permit fee EUR 218; means threshold EUR 1,308.39 a month (2026); family reunification suspended for some categories until 30 September 2026 (re-check before publishing anything time-specific).
- **Finland.** Migri's processing time and its current requirements for an under-18 student were not confirmed (migri.fi blocks automated access); see `junior-schools-education-factcheck.md`.
- **USA.** F-1 and P-1A points in `usa-junior-factcheck.md` remain secondary-source summaries; not re-checked.

## 4. A planning order (EHA's sequence, not a regulation)

The sources fix some dependencies and leave others to the parties. A reasonable order, derived from them:

1. Written offer and responsible adult confirmed (not an IIHF step).
2. School admission confirmed in writing, where the permit depends on it (Sweden requires being finally admitted; Finland requires paying the fee before a study permit).
3. Immigration application, which for Sweden is first made from outside the country and takes about three months for 75% of cases; for Canada needs the custodian and notarised forms; for Czechia a consulate-specific document set.
4. IIHF transfer: requested by the new club's MNA; the former MNA has 7 days; no answer means approval; the player may not play for the new club before the approval date. For under-18s the 1 July to 15 February window does not apply, but a national rule (Sweden's under-18 conditions, Finland's guardian relocation, Austria's licensing window) may.
5. Travel authorisation from parents in the form the destination, transit countries and airline require.
6. Registration with the national association and the club before an official game.

Steps 2 to 4 can overlap in time, but a page must not say that one clears another: club interest, school admission, the permit, the IIHF transfer and national registration are decided by different bodies.
