Hockey in Czechia: leagues, contracts, visas and transfers

A practical guide for junior and senior players: how the market works, what clubs assess, and which sporting, contract and immigration questions must be resolved before a move.

01

How Czech hockey is organised

Czech hockey combines professional senior competitions, national and regional junior leagues, and a broad club-development system. For a foreign player, the league name is only one part of the decision: role, age eligibility, competition rules, registration and the lawful basis for residence and work all matter.

Český hokej publishes the official competition names and regulations. One club’s package is not a national standard.

02

Senior leagues: Extraliga, Maxa liga and 2. liga

Tipsport Extraligatop professional level
Maxa ligasecond professional level
2. ligathird senior level
  • Tipsport Extraliga is an elite market with intense competition for every role.
  • Maxa liga is the official second senior level. The published technical standard available when this guide was updated sets a maximum of six foreign players in a game.
  • 2. liga is the third level; sporting and living conditions vary materially between clubs.
Check the season: limits, age eligibility, formats and registration deadlines may change. The former commercial name is no longer used in the current EHA reference.
03

Junior hockey and the development route

For 2026/27, the federation’s official competition pages and documents show Extraliga juniorů, Liga juniorů and Regionální liga juniorů. Separate dorost and school-age competitions operate below the junior category.

A foreign junior must verify birth-year eligibility, expected ice time, education, housing, guardian or legal-representative requirements where applicable, medical arrangements and the real development plan. An academy name without a defined role is not evidence of a good route.

04

What clubs assess in a foreign player

  • the standard of the previous league, not points alone;
  • position, shot, size, pace, play without the puck and special-teams use;
  • age, development potential and readiness for a defined role;
  • full games or shifts as well as a short recent highlight video;
  • citizenship, contract status, availability and transfer history;
  • language, independence and readiness to live abroad.

An available import place does not oblige a club to use it. The player must improve a specific role.

05

Foreign-player limits and passport status

Import status under competition regulations and the legal right to live and work in Czechia are separate checks.

The published Maxa liga standard sets a maximum of six foreign players in a game. Do not apply that number automatically to Extraliga, 2. liga or junior competitions: the club must confirm the current rule for its competition and season.

EU citizens generally benefit from EU free-movement rules. A third-country national needs an appropriate residence basis and, where applicable, work authorisation.

06

Pay, benefits and contract terms

There is no single official salary table covering all Czech leagues and clubs, so EHA does not present unverified ranges as fact. Assess the whole package.

  • gross or net amount and payment schedule;
  • term, trial period and termination rights;
  • housing, meals, transport, equipment and medical insurance;
  • bonuses, fines, injury, treatment and rehabilitation;
  • contract registration, transfer costs and return travel;
  • who pays agency, visa, permit and banking costs.

A verbal promise of housing or employment is not a written contract term.

07

Housing and the player budget

Living costs depend on the city, accommodation type and whether players share. Before accepting, request the address or area, deposit, utilities, travel to the rink, food, mobile service and insurance details.

Build two budgets: recurring monthly costs and one-off relocation costs. If housing is supplied, confirm whether it is free, deducted from pay, and what happens after early termination.

08

EU and non-EU residence and work

For third-country nationals, the Czech Ministry of the Interior’s official portal provides long-term visa and residence routes for sports purposes in professional sports competitions. That route does not cover amateur competitions; amateur hockey and other roles require a separate assessment.

  1. obtain a written offer or contract and confirm the competition’s status;
  2. check the correct visa or permit on the official portal and with the competent consulate;
  3. verify processing time, documents, insurance and entry rules;
  4. do not begin paid activity before the status permits it.

This is general orientation, not legal advice. The club, Czech Ministry of the Interior or competent consulate must confirm the route for the player’s citizenship, stay and contract.

09

IIHF transfer, registration and insurance

If the player’s last registration is with another national association, the move normally uses the IIHF international transfer system. The receiving club works with Český hokej and tells the player which documents and fees are required.

  1. confirm the last federation and current contract status;
  2. agree the type of international transfer;
  3. obtain federation and competition eligibility confirmation;
  4. complete domestic club registration;
  5. confirm medical requirements and insurance before practice or competition.

An international transfer does not replace immigration status, work rights, a contract or insurance.

10

Complete document package

  • passport and any current residence document;
  • current hockey resume and Elite Prospects link;
  • official statistics, height, weight, position, shot and date of birth;
  • short highlights and full games or shifts;
  • last registration and contract-status information;
  • club offer or contract;
  • insurance, medical documents and emergency contact;
  • for a minor, guardian, education and accommodation documents where required.
11

Tryouts and approach timing

Recruitment for core roles often starts in spring, but exact schedules differ. Later needs can arise through injuries, budget changes or unsuccessful signings. Approach clubs with a complete package, not a promise to send video later.

Before travelling for a tryout, record the dates, number of practices and games, housing, meals, transport, insurance, cost responsibility, decision criteria and reply deadline in writing.

12

Resume, video and offer verification

The first message should state who the player is, the level played, the target role and why the profile fits that club. Use the hockey resume guide and the guide to video for clubs.

Before accepting, verify the club’s legal identity, authorised signatory, competition, role, pay and taxes, housing, insurance, termination, transfer and immigration route. Follow the full club-offer checklist.

What is Czechia’s second division called?

The current official name is Maxa liga. The former name has been removed from current EHA material.

How many foreign players may dress in Maxa liga?

The published technical standard available when this page was updated sets a maximum of six foreign players in a game. Re-check it before the season.

Is an international transfer required?

A move between national associations normally follows the IIHF process coordinated by the receiving club and federation.

Does the sports visa route cover amateur hockey?

No. The official sports-purpose route applies to professional competitions. An amateur case needs a separate residence assessment.

When should a player submit a profile?

Prepare the complete package before the main spring and early-summer roster-building phase; individual openings can arise later.
