From USPHL, EHL or NA3HL to senior hockey in Europe

Which competition you played in, the five factors European clubs check and realistic first targets — orientation, not a level formula.

Updated 18 November 2026.

Competitions · What decides · First targets · Next step

Short answer

A player from USPHL, EHL or NA3HL junior hockey can start a senior career in Europe, but usually at a third national level or in a defined role at a lower second level, not straight into a top league. What decides the first league is not the junior league's name but your age, role, games played, statistics in context and a full-game video. NCDC and EHL top-division players with a leading role have a stronger case than depth players in USPHL Premier, Elite, EHL Premier or NA3HL.

EHA has no confirmed transfer cases from these leagues to publish yet, so this guide gives orientation, not a promise: European clubs judge each player on video and role.

Frequently asked questions

Can I play pro hockey in Europe after USPHL, EHL or NA3HL?

Yes, usually starting at a third national level or in a defined role at a lower second level. Role, games and video decide.

Is NCDC the same as USPHL Premier?

No. NCDC is the USPHL's top division with tuition-free spots; Premier and Elite are lower divisions.

Does EHA have examples of these transfers?

Not confirmed ones we can publish yet; we only publish cases with the player's consent and verified details.

Will signing in Europe affect NCAA eligibility?

It can. Check NCAA rules before signing any senior contract.
