# Canada junior hockey fact-check (CHL: WHL / OHL / QMJHL)

Checked 10 August 2026 against official sources. This is an editorial record, not legal advice.

## Verified points for 2025/26

- Player eligibility runs from the year of a player's 16th birthday through the year of his 20th birthday. WHL and OHL each explicitly cap **overage (20-year-old) players at 3 per roster**; WHL requires teams to cut down to 3 by 16 October if they enter camp with more, with the roster locking on 10 February. QMJHL's own age-band and overage-count pages could not be found as a distinct primary document — CHL's QMJHL eligibility page confirms players may be rostered "until his 20th year, with Commissioner-approved exceptions" but does not state a specific overage cap; treat any QMJHL-specific overage number as unconfirmed.
- Foreign (import) players enter the CHL only through the annual **CHL Import Draft** — a single joint draft across all three leagues (not three separate league drafts), with selection order rotating between WHL/OHL/QMJHL and all 60 CHL teams participating. For the 2025/26 season the import cap was **raised from 2 to 3 players per team**, and the draft expanded from 2 to 3 rounds accordingly. The 2025 Import Draft ran 2 July 2025; 142 players were selected. Clubs are additionally limited to **one 16-year-old import per season**, non-tradeable during that season. Whether a team can sign an import outside the draft under any circumstance was not found in an official source — not confirmed.
- A major 2025 change to note explicitly: the NCAA Division I Council approved a rule change (7 November 2024, effective 1 August 2025) that lets CHL players **retain NCAA Division I eligibility**, provided they receive no more than "actual and necessary expenses" (meals, lodging, transport, equipment, coaching, ice time, medical/health insurance, entry fees) — no salary. This does **not** apply to NCAA Division III, where CHL players remain ineligible, and a player who signs an NHL contract and later returns to the CHL stays NCAA-ineligible. Per the OHL's own FAQ, a player who leaves before completing his Standard Player Agreement forfeits the league's education package; a player who completes his term can apply the package to NCAA schooling afterward. This materially changes the trade-off a foreign junior and family are weighing versus an NCAA-track route (e.g. NCDC/USHL) compared to seasons before 2025/26 — state it prominently and accurately.
- WHL's own material describes its education package as one year of tuition and books funded per year played, usable at Canadian institutions. Specific forfeiture conditions (e.g. withdrawing from classes, falling below a passing grade) appear in a Standard Player Agreement referenced in third-party legal documents, not confirmed directly on the WHL's own current scholarship page — state the tuition-per-year-played benefit as confirmed, and flag forfeiture mechanics as needing direct confirmation with the specific league/team.
- Housing is billet-family based. Hockey Canada's national "Player Billeting Resource Package" sets baseline safeguarding standards: criminal record and vulnerable-sector screening for all household members 19+, an in-home interview, reference checks, orientation, ongoing supervision, a private bedroom for the player (or shared only with a teammate, never other household members), a school-attendance obligation for the billet family to uphold, and an anti-hazing appendix. This is a national resource framework; leagues/teams run their own implementation on top of it (e.g. the OHL's own prospective-player page confirms billet screening via interview plus criminal background check, placement only in pre-approved homes, and a "Hazing-Zero Tolerance Policy").
- Safeguarding: the CHL runs the "Respect Hockey Culture Center" (launched October 2023), an independent third-party reporting platform (operated by REES Community, NHL-grant funded) covering bullying, abuse, harassment and discrimination reports league-wide, plus the "Talk Today" mental-health/suicide-prevention programme (since 2014, with the Canadian Mental Health Association). The OHL separately runs its own "Players First" initiative and an "Onside" respect-education programme. Hockey Canada became a full signatory to Abuse-Free Sport / the Office of the Sport Integrity Commissioner (OSIC) in October 2022 — a claim surfaced that OSIC may no longer be operating as of a December 2023 ministerial order, but this could not be verified against a primary source and may describe a restructuring rather than a shutdown; the page must not state OSIC's current status as settled fact.
- Immigration for a foreign minor is the least-confirmed area and needs the strongest caution on the page. Confirmed via IRCC's own published rule: a study permit is required for any programme longer than 6 months at a designated learning institution. A commonly cited requirement — that a minor under 17 travelling without an accompanying parent needs a Custodianship Declaration (IRCC Form IMM 5646) naming a Canadian citizen or permanent-resident custodian — could only be verified via third-party immigration-service sites, not IRCC.gc.ca directly (the direct guideline page returned an access error during this check). Whether a billet family can serve as that custodian, and whether a work permit is separately required given a player receives a stipend/education package, was not found in any official source. This section must not be presented as settled — verify directly with IRCC or immigration counsel before relying on it.

## Publication note

The page must state the 2025 NCAA Division I eligibility change prominently and accurately, since it materially changes how a foreign junior and family should weigh the CHL against an NCAA-track route like the NCDC — and must note it does not extend to Division III. The immigration/visa section needs the site's strongest standard caution language: the custodianship-declaration and work-permit questions could not be confirmed against IRCC's own site in this check, so the page must direct families to IRCC or immigration counsel rather than presenting a specific procedure as confirmed. Treat QMJHL-specific overage/import numbers as unconfirmed by a primary source even though the joint CHL-wide Import Draft mechanics (3 rounds, 3-import cap, one 16-year-old per team) are confirmed.
