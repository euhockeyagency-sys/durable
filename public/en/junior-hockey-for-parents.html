Junior hockey abroad: a parent guide

A practical checklist for evaluating a junior hockey route without confusing a tryout, an invitation and a guaranteed place.

1. How junior hockey is organised

Junior systems combine age-category teams, regional competitions, national leagues, school programmes and a possible senior pathway. The league name is only one part of the decision.

2. When can a foreign player join?

There is no universal European age. Ask the federation and club about the exact age band, registration dates, international transfer conditions and whether the player’s nationality changes the route.

3. Education comes first

Request the school name, language, timetable, accreditation, attendance responsibility, transport and what happens if hockey ends. A promise to “arrange school” is not a plan until it is written down.

4. Housing and daily care

Clarify whether the player lives in a boarding school, host family or with a parent; who is the responsible adult; bedroom and curfew arrangements; meals, transport, insurance and emergency contact.

5. What to check with the club

  • team, coach, training schedule and likely role;
  • registration and transfer responsibility;
  • education and accommodation plan;
  • all costs and benefits in writing, without relying on a verbal promise;
  • refund and cancellation conditions for camps or tryouts;
  • safeguarding contact and complaint route.

6. Documents and minor support

Depending on the country, the file may include passport, parental consent, medical and insurance documents, school records, transfer papers, representation details and proof of accommodation. The club and competent authorities must confirm the exact list.

7. Questions before travel

Who meets the player? Where does he or she sleep? Who authorises medical care? Who pays for travel and equipment? What is the decision date? Which adult can be contacted at any hour?

8. Red flags

Pressure to pay for a guaranteed roster place, refusal to name the team or adult responsible, no written education plan, unclear housing, requests to travel before documents are checked, or a contract that differs from the sales message.

9. Tryout versus place

Ask whether the invitation is an assessment, a camp, a practice opportunity or an offer conditional on selection. Confirm the number of ice sessions, evaluation criteria, total cost and whether any place is actually available.

10. Immigration and transfer

Sporting selection does not itself grant a right to enter, study, work or register. For a minor, check the current federation, immigration authority, consulate and IIHF requirements before booking travel.

11. Prepare the player profile

Use a short hockey resume, current statistics, citizenship, position, age, recent highlights and a full game. Include school, language and housing needs so the club can assess the whole route.
