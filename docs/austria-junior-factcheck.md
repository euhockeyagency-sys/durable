# Austria U18/U20 junior hockey — fact-check

Checked: 14 August 2026. Research method: direct retrieval and full-text
extraction of ÖEHV (Austrian Ice Hockey Federation) regulatory PDFs, the
2024 IIHF International Transfer Regulations, and official Austrian
government immigration/education pages. DIRECT = fetched and read in full
from the primary source. SEARCH = secondary source, not independently
verified against a primary document. Every figure below must be re-checked
against the current-season document before being republished as "current" —
league regulations are dated per-season (e.g. "2025/26") and change yearly.

## 1. League structure

- ÖEHV runs the junior pyramid directly, not via regional federations: the
  **ÖEHV U20 International League** and **ÖEHV U18 International League**,
  below which sit ÖEHV U17, U15, U13 and U11 championships. Source: DÖNAM
  2025/26 (Durchführungsbestimmungen Österreichische
  Nachwuchsmeisterschaften), §2–§3, EN:
  https://www.eishockey.at/media/doc/bbaf3421b4f5e1ba1b5a8d0b31540dd2/doenam-2025-26-v02-eng-.pdf
  (binding DE version:
  https://www.eishockey.at/media/doc/90e462778b2bc79fc2a1d594d9ac729a/doenam-2025-26-v02.pdf).
- **There is no U16 category** in ÖEHV's own structure — the step below U18
  is U17. Do not use "U16" for Austria.
- U20/U18 are explicitly **cross-border "International League"** competitions:
  2025/26 participants include Austrian club academies (Linz, Graz/Steiermark,
  Red Bull Salzburg, Vienna Capitals, Villacher SV, Innsbruck) alongside
  Hungarian and Slovenian clubs, plus foreign-branded academy entrants. DÖNAM
  §3(2)-(3), same source.
- The senior **Alps Hockey League** (Austria/Italy/Slovenia, already covered
  on this site as a senior league) is a **structurally separate competition**
  from the U20i/U18i junior leagues — confirmed via the Alps HL Austrian-
  championship regulations, which contain no youth provisions:
  https://www.eishockey.at/media/doc/fd8be3bcc613801fac8bdd1e63d09a09/dfbst-oem-alpshl-2025-26.pdf.
  One Alps HL entrant, "Red Bull Hockey Juniors," is itself a development-age
  team playing at senior level — a blurred edge case, not a formal junior
  division of Alps HL.
- **Not confirmed**: whether a purely domestic (non-cross-border) Austria-only
  U20/U18 championship exists as an alternative to the International League.
  DÖNAM only describes the International League at these two age levels.

## 2. Age bands (2025/26 season — re-verify every season)

Source: DÖNAM 2025/26, §3. Cutoffs are single birth years, not two-year bands:

| Category | Birth year (2025/26) |
|---|---|
| U20i | 2006 and younger |
| U18i | 2008 and younger |
| U17 | 2009 and younger |
| U15 | 2011 and younger |
| U13 | 2013 and younger |
| U11 | 2015 and younger |

- U20i allows up to 3 "overage" players per game (born 2005); U18i allows
  none. DÖNAM §13(5).
- Female players may play in mixed teams up to U17 and "may be one year
  older per age group" than the stated cutoff. DÖNAM §13(6).
- Separately, ÖEHV's Meldebestimmungen (registration regulations) define a
  "minor" for registration purposes as not yet 18 by 31 December of the
  current federation year (1 June–31 May) — a legal/registration definition
  distinct from, but consistent with, the sporting cutoffs above.

## 3. International transfer rules for minors

### IIHF baseline (confirmed)
Source: 2024 IIHF International Transfer Regulations, effective 9 October
2023:
https://blob.iihf.com/iihf-media/iihfmvc/media/downloads/regulations/2024/2024_iihf_international_transfer_regulations.pdf
- Art. I.5.1: no transfer fee for a player under 18.
- Art. I.5.2: the general 1 July–15 February transfer-deadline window does
  not apply to under-18 transfers.
- The full document was searched for a FIFA-style parental-relocation
  protection clause for minors — **none exists**. "Parent"/"guardian" do not
  appear in the IIHF regulations at all.

### ÖEHV's own domestic licensing window — applies regardless of age
Source: ÖEHV Meldebestimmungen ("Stand August 2023"), §7(1):
https://www.eishockey.at/media/doc/26c009e59e706d59dfddae3cd96ebb75/oeehv-meldebestimmungen.pdf
- Player licensing/loan agreements may only be concluded **1 June–15
  February**, and this **explicitly applies to players never previously
  registered in Austria, regardless of age**. No under-18 carve-out from
  this domestic window was found. For the U20i/U18i International League
  specifically, DÖNAM §13(4) sets an even tighter roster-addition cutoff for
  2025/26: **1 June 2025 – 31 January 2026**, after which no player may be
  added to any roster, "including players never previously registered in
  Austria."
- **Practical implication for the article**: IIHF exempts under-18
  transfers from its own window, but Austria's domestic licensing rule
  still effectively blocks a new registration after mid-February
  (mid-to-late-January for the U20i/U18i league specifically) regardless of
  the player's age. Don't imply a minor can be licensed and playing at any
  time of year.

### Austria's functional equivalent to a "guardian relocation" rule
Source: same Meldebestimmungen, §7(4), definition of "Eishockeyösterreicher"
(EHÖ):
- A non-Austrian player licensed with ÖEHV clubs for **5 consecutive
  seasons before turning 18** is reclassified as domestic ("EHÖ"), not a
  transfer player.
- A faster **EU-citizen-only** track ("EHÖ-EU") applies if a parent
  demonstrably relocated to Austria before the player's 17th birthday, the
  family has had its "centre of life" in Austria for ≥1 year, one parent is
  under Austrian social insurance, and the player has lived with that
  parent for ≥1 year.
- **This is a status/quota reclassification, not a transfer precondition.**
  A minor without a relocating parent can still get an ordinary
  international transfer and be licensed as a transfer player — EHÖ/EHÖ-EU
  status only affects import-quota bookkeeping (see below), not whether the
  move is possible at all. Do not present this as equivalent to Finland's
  hard guardian-relocation transfer requirement — it is a materially
  different mechanism (bonus/quota status vs. a gating condition).

### Import quota at youth level (soft, not hard)
DÖNAM §4(6)/§13(2): a club fielding a maximum of 3 non-domestic (transfer)
players per U20i/U18i game gets a 50% entry-fee discount. This is a
financial incentive, not a hard roster cap — clubs may field more imports
and simply forfeit the discount.

### Training-cost compensation (AKES) does not apply at youth-registration stage
Source: ÖEHV AKES22, October 2023:
https://www.eishockey.at/media/doc/fada2716e02613e1aef73bea06c7d754/akes-bestimmungen-2023-10.pdf
- AKES (training-cost compensation) is scoped to ICE Hockey League/Alps
  Hockey League (senior/pro) rosters only, §1. Youth-league roster fees use
  the ordinary fee schedule, §3(2). AKES does not create a domestic
  training-compensation charge for a foreign minor joining a U18/U20 team —
  it only becomes relevant later if that player is later rostered on a
  senior ICE HL/Alps HL squad.

### Not confirmed
No ÖEHV document was found imposing a hard requirement that a parent or
guardian must accompany or permanently relocate with a non-EU minor as a
precondition of the *sporting* transfer/license (unlike Finland's stated
rule). Do not assert Austria has no such requirement in absolute terms —
state that none was found in the documents reviewed, and that the
Aufenthaltsbewilligung–Schüler permit (§6 below) explicitly does not
require a relocating parent.

## 4. Education alongside hockey

- Austria's federally codified dual-career sport-school network
  ("Schulen mit sportlichem Schwerpunkt") is built around **skiing**
  (the "Skihauptschule"/"Skimittelschule" network), confirmed by direct
  fetch of the official page:
  https://www.schulsportinfo.at/infos/bewegung-und-sport/root/abteilung-schulsport/schulen-mit-sportlichem-schwerpunkt
  — "Eishockey" does not appear on it. **There is no equivalent nationally
  codified hockey-school system**, unlike Finland's urheilulukio network
  (already published on this site).
- Instead, hockey education support is organized club-by-club through named
  regional academies, each individually brokering school partnerships:
  - **Eishockey-Akademie Oberösterreich** (Linz), run jointly with EV Linz
    and the Upper Austria regional federation, with a partner-school
    arrangement and a student dormitory ("Internat"). Admission happens
    "after the semester break" (roughly early February) via application,
    video and motivation letter, not an on-ice tryout.
    https://www.ooeehv.at/akademie/die-akademie
  - **Eishockey-Akademie Steiermark** (Graz 99ers) has historically
    partnered with Sport-BORG Monsbergergasse (a dual-career school,
    4-year "HASCH" or 5-year "BORG" Matura track). The school's current
    (2026) partner-sport list, fetched directly
    (https://www.sportborg.at/index.php/akademiesport), does **not**
    currently name ice hockey among its partner sports — flag as not
    confirmed for the current year; the club itself remains active and
    competing per the DÖNAM roster.
  - **ENZO** (Eishockey Nachwuchszentrum Ost, Vienna/Lower Austria) targets
    kindergarten/primary-age development, not U18/U20 boarding academy —
    do not present it as equivalent to the Linz/Graz programs.
  - Red Bull Salzburg runs an internal age-graded academy (U20/U18/U17/U16
    squads) feeding the International Leagues, but no education-partnership
    detail was confirmed.

## 5. Housing / billeting

- All four core ÖEHV regulatory documents (DÖNAM, Meldebestimmungen, AKES,
  Alps HL rules) were searched for any housing/billeting provision — **none
  exists**. There is no ÖEHV federation-level housing program.
- The one partial exception: Eishockey-Akademie Oberösterreich (Linz)
  bundles a dormitory into its academy package. This was not independently
  confirmed for the Graz or Salzburg academies. Present housing as
  club/academy-specific and to be confirmed directly, consistent with every
  other market on this site.

## 6. Immigration (non-EU minor)

Austria is EU/Schengen; EU/EEA citizens do not need a visa. For a non-EU
minor, two general (non-hockey-specific) routes apply:

- **Family reunification** — requires a parent to already hold a
  qualifying Austrian residence status (Red-White-Red Card, EU Blue Card,
  long-term residence, settlement permit) or Austrian citizenship, plus
  proof of means (≥€1,308.39/month, 2026 figure) and adequate
  accommodation. A suspension is noted on the official page for some
  categories of new applications through **30 September 2026** — re-check
  the live page before publishing anything time-specific:
  https://www.migration.gv.at/en/types-of-immigration/permanent-immigration/family-reunification/
- **"Aufenthaltsbewilligung – Schüler" (pupil residence permit)** — the
  more directly relevant route for a minor joining a club academy, and does
  **not** require a parent to relocate. For third-country minors enrolling
  in Austrian schooling for more than 6 months: requires proof that an
  adult in Austria (which may be a host family, school director, or
  boarding-school/dormitory staff) has been entrusted with the player's
  care and education via written parental declaration, plus school
  enrollment proof, accommodation proof, health insurance, funds evidence,
  and (14+) a criminal record certificate. 12-month permit, renewable, filed
  at the embassy/consulate before travel. Fee €218 (2026).
  https://www.wien.gv.at/amtswege/aufenthaltsbewilligung-schueler
  (Vienna implementation; federal portal:
  https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/aufenthalt/3/2/1/1/Seite.120117)
- No sport-specific fast-track visa for minors was found (Red-White-Red
  Card categories are adult/employment-based).
- This is general immigration information, not a hockey-specific rule —
  state plainly it must be confirmed with an immigration professional or
  the embassy/consulate before travel.

## 7. Recruitment timing

- No ÖEHV document states a league-wide tryout/trial window. The only
  federally dated mechanism is the licensing window itself (1 June–15
  February generally; 31 January 2026 specifically for U20i/U18i roster
  additions this season).
- ÖEHV runs national-team talent-identification camps ("Sichtungscamps")
  for U14/U15 players feeding the national-team pool — these are
  **selection camps, not club recruitment tryouts**, and are U14/U15-only.
- Individual academies set their own admissions timing (Linz: after the
  winter semester break, roughly early February). Treat timing as
  club/academy-specific, as with every other market on this site.

## Publication note

- Do not use "U16" for Austria — the category below U18 is U17.
- Present the EHÖ/EHÖ-EU mechanism carefully as a status/quota
  reclassification, not a hard transfer precondition — this is a real
  nuance versus Finland's guardian-relocation rule and must not be
  flattened into "Austria also requires a guardian to relocate."
  Non-relocating families can still get an ordinary transfer; they simply
  don't gain EHÖ status.
- State the domestic 1 June–15 February (or 31 January for U20i/U18i this
  season) licensing window clearly, since it applies regardless of the
  IIHF under-18 exemption.
- Present immigration content as general routes, not hockey-specific, and
  flag the family-reunification suspension date as something to re-check
  live before publishing.
- Re-verify all season-specific dates/birth-years against the then-current
  DÖNAM document before reusing this content next season.
