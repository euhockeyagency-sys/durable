Hockey in Germany: leagues, U20, transfers and work

A practical guide for players and families, from choosing a realistic level to contracts, registration, international transfers and the lawful right to play and work.

01

How German hockey is structured

The senior system centres on the DEL, DEL2 and the North and South divisions of the Oberliga. Regionalliga and other competitions below them are organised regionally; they are not one nationwide fourth tier with identical rules.

A move should be assessed by actual role, pace, training load, club budget, contract status and the player’s eligibility, not only by the division name.

02

DEL, DEL2, Oberliga and Regionalliga

DELtop professional level
DEL2professional second level
Oberligathird level: North and South
  • DEL: the top of the market, requiring a proven professional standard and a defined role.
  • DEL2: the 2026/27 framework lists 14 clubs and 52 rounds, with the regular season starting on 18 September 2026.
  • Oberliga: DEB confirmed 26 teams for 2026/27: 12 in the North and 14 in the South.
  • Regionalliga: a regional level whose format and eligibility depend on the relevant association and competition.
03

Junior route: U20 DNL and U17

The national development system includes U20 DNL Division I, Division II and Division III North/South. U17 has Division I and II with northern and southern groups. DEB documents must be checked each season for divisional composition and age eligibility.

For an international junior, the club should confirm the team, expected ice time, education, a guardian where required, housing, insurance, transfer and registration. A tryout is not a roster guarantee.

04

Choosing a realistic level

Clubs compare the previous league, pace of decision-making, physical readiness, production in role context, play away from the puck, special teams and video quality. A country name or high point total in a weaker competition does not prove fit by itself.

DEL and DEL2 usually require a ready professional profile. Oberliga and regional competitions offer a wider range of needs, but role, budget and lawful registration are still case-specific.

05

Club requirements and import status

Prepare position and shot, date of birth, height and weight, citizenship, contract status, current league, statistics, role, video and exact availability. Goalies benefit from full games and core numbers with team context.

Important: there is no single verified import limit covering DEL, DEL2, Oberliga and every regional league. The club must check its current competition rules and explain whether the player occupies an import place.
06

Contract, salary and conditions

There is no official universal salary table for German hockey leagues. Pay depends on level, role, experience, budget and the total package. Before accepting, obtain a draft contract and confirm:

  • gross or net amount, payment schedule, bonuses and taxes;
  • housing, deposit, utilities, food and transport;
  • equipment, sticks, medical care and insurance;
  • trial period, injury, termination, leave and national-team release;
  • who pays transfer, registration, visa and related fees.

An oral promise does not replace a signed document and legal review of the agreement.

07

Housing and cost of living

Costs vary materially by city and housing type, so one universal figure would be misleading. Ask for the full address, housing type, roommates, monthly rent, deposit, utilities, furniture, distance to the rink and transport.

Compare the amount left after rent, tax, insurance, food and travel, not salary alone. If the club only “helps find housing,” establish who signs the lease and carries the deposit risk.

08

EU/EEA and non-EU players

EU/EEA citizens generally do not need a work visa for employment in Germany, although registration, tax and social-security formalities remain. A third-country national needs a lawful basis for entry, residence and the proposed sporting employment before starting the activity.

For the special professional-athlete route, the federal portal lists four key conditions for 2026: minimum age 16, work for a German club or sporting institution participating in competitive sport, at least €50,700 gross annual salary, and confirmation by the competent governing sports body in agreement with the DOSB.

Do not confuse routes: this threshold belongs to the special professional route and does not automatically turn an amateur offer into a valid basis. Check the individual route with the official authority before travel.
09

IIHF transfer, DEB registration and insurance

  1. International move: national associations process it through the IIHF transfer system.
  2. DEB: the federation explains that international clearance is required when moving from another association and in specific cases for players born abroad, regardless of citizenship.
  3. Term: a transfer can be requested as limited or unlimited; a multi-season request requires supporting documentation.
  4. Registration: the club checks the roster application, age, competition and deadlines before the first game.
  5. Insurance: confirm coverage for training, games, injuries, treatment and the tryout period in writing.
10

Player documents

  • valid passport and proof of citizenship;
  • contract or official offer identifying the club;
  • last registration and information required for the international transfer;
  • insurance and medical documents requested by the club;
  • for non-EU players, visa or residence documents for the employment purpose;
  • for minors, consents, guardian arrangements, education and housing confirmation.

The final list comes from the club together with DEB, the competition and immigration authority. Do not book travel before the team, purpose of entry and responsible contacts are confirmed.

11

Recruitment timing and tryouts

Most roster building takes place in spring and summer, while individual needs can emerge later because of injury, budget or roster changes. Contact clubs selectively and explain why the profile fits that team and role.

Before a tryout, confirm dates, number of sessions and games, travel and housing costs, insurance, responsible contact, decision criteria and response deadline. A tryout should not disguise a paid trip without a genuine sporting evaluation.

12

Resume and video for a German club

One compact package should include a hockey resume, Elite Prospects, verifiable statistics, three to five minutes of recent highlights and a full-game or full-shift link. Identify the jersey number and sequence clearly without using editing to hide the original game quality.

State the realistic target league, citizenship, contract status and availability. Also review the EHA video standard and guide to agent work in Germany.

13

Offer verification, sources and FAQ

Verify the club’s legal name and actual team, league, signatory, term, pay, housing, insurance, transfer, registration, immigration route and termination provisions. Use the EHA offer checklist for an independent review.

Editorial review: 29 July 2026. Re-check regulations and thresholds before applying.

What are the main senior leagues?

DEL, DEL2 and Oberliga North/South. Regional competitions below them have separate rules.

Is an international transfer required?

Yes when moving between national associations. The club and DEB confirm the exact procedure.

Does an EU passport guarantee a place?

No. It can simplify administration but does not replace the sporting evaluation.

Can a non-EU player use the special route?

Only when the official conditions, including professional income and federation confirmation, are met.

What should be verified in an offer?

The team, league, signatory, pay, housing, insurance, transfer, registration, immigration route and termination terms.
