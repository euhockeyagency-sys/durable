US junior hockey leagues: how to choose a player's route

Compare the league system, the player's role and the complete season cost before travelling. This guide explains the main differences between USHL, NAHL, NCDC, NA3HL, EHL and USPHL Premier.

How to read US junior hockey levels

USHL and NAHL belong to the USA Hockey system. NCDC's Tier II designation is used by USPHL and does not identify the same sanctioning system. NCAA is college hockey; NHL, AHL and ECHL are professional leagues, rather than additional junior divisions in this table.

The comparison below helps frame questions; it is not a ranking of every team by playing strength. Rosters, coaching and roles differ within a league. For cross-border USPHL organizations, also confirm the country where the player will live and compete.

Where to start the decision

EHA judgement: first compare the player's current level and role with full-game evidence from the target team. Then compare the proposed role, coaching, school arrangements and complete budget. A points total or league label cannot replace that review.

  • For a stronger league, ask where the player will actually compete if they do not make the lineup.
  • For a paid programme, ask what the fee buys and how development will be reviewed.
  • Check recent alumni from the specific team: season, college, level and whether they actually began competing there.

The complete family budget

Tuition-free describes hockey tuition within an offer, rather than automatically covering school, flights and the player's entire living costs. A junior hockey scholarship is not a university scholarship.

  • Hockey tuition, deposits, camps, equipment and replacement items.
  • Housing: monthly cost × actual months, meals and local transport.
  • School, learning support, insurance and personal flights.
  • Refunds if the player is cut, injured, released or moves teams.

Camp, tender, draft and roster are different stages

A camp invitation offers an evaluation opportunity. A tender or draft selection can establish a team's rights under that league's rules. Before travelling, clarify whether the offer concerns a camp, protected list or active roster, what selection remains and what happens to the deposit. Do not apply NAHL rules to NCDC.

USPHL uses separate NCDC and Premier agreements. Read the NCDC guide for the distinction.

Age, education and safeguarding

Before paying, obtain season-specific eligibility confirmation using the player's birth date, citizenship and registration/transfer history. Junior participation does not automatically establish NCAA eligibility or permission to stay in the country. Coordinate the education plan with the school and prospective college.

For a minor, identify the responsible adult, host-family rules, medical procedures and safeguarding reporting contact in advance. The parent guide helps prepare those questions.

Frequently asked questions

Which US junior hockey league is best for a player?

The decision depends on current level, role, full-game evidence, coaching, education and budget. The league name alone does not determine the best route.

Are NAHL and NCDC the same Tier II system?

No. NAHL is USA Hockey Tier II, while NCDC uses a Tier II designation within USPHL. Registration rules and agreements must be checked separately.

Does tuition-free hockey mean a free season?

No. Confirm housing, meals, school, insurance, flights and personal expenses separately, even when hockey tuition is covered.

Does a camp invitation guarantee a roster place?

No. Clarify the invitation status, remaining selection stages, payment conditions and refund terms.

Does a US junior route fit this player?

Send the birth year, position, current team and league, role, statistics, an Elite Prospects profile if available and a recent full game. Include the education objective, family budget and questions about any offer. Tell us if full-game video is not yet available.
