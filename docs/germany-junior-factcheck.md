# Germany junior hockey fact-check

Checked 9 August 2026 against official sources. This is an editorial record, not legal advice.

## Verified points for 2026/27

- Deutscher Eishockey-Bund's (DEB) Spielordnung (fetched live, current version dated 31 May 2025), Art. 50 "Altersgrenzen," fixes age by the calendar year in which the season begins: U20 (Juniorenspieler) = players turning 17, 18 or 19 in that year; U17 (Jugendspieler) = players turning 15 or 16. For season 2026/27 (begins 2026): **U20 = born 2007–2009; U17 = born 2010–2011.** Unlike Sweden's or Finland's two-year or single-year bands, Germany's U20 spans three birth years.
- Top junior competitions are named **U20 Division I/II/III** and **U17 Division I/II**. The older "DNL1/DNL2" branding was retired when the league restructured for 2018/19 and should not be used in new content.
- DEB Spielordnung Art. 52a Ziff. 3.3.1.3: for a player who has **not yet turned 18** in the relevant season, the formal international Transferkarte is **not required** — a simple written or electronic confirmation from the player's former national federation suffices instead, which is a simplification relative to an adult's transfer, not an extra burden. Art. 52a Ziff. 2 (a general rule applying to all minors, not transfer-specific) requires the permit application to be countersigned by the player's legal representative (gesetzlicher Vertreter).
- The IIHF's own baseline (International Transfer Regulations, effective 6 October 2025), Art. I.5, applies underneath: no transfer fee before age 18, and the general 1 July–15 February transfer-deadline window does not apply to under-18 transfers.
- **One point needing clarification before publication**: DEB's own domestic transfer-window rule (Art. 55: 1 June–15 September and 1 December–31 January for all youth categories) does not carry an explicit under-18 carve-out, even though the IIHF layer above it waives its own deadline for minors — the practical interaction between these two rules for a mid-window international arrival should be confirmed directly with DEB rather than assumed.
- DOSB's "Eliteschule des Sports" is a nationwide brand (43 sites) for schools combining education and elite sport; the confirmed ice-hockey site is the Schul- und Leistungssportzentrum Berlin (SLZB, Sportforum Lichtenberg/Hohenschönhausen), taking hockey players from grade 7. Separately, and distinctly, **Bavaria runs its own state programme** — "Partnerzentren des Wintersports" (PZW) / "Stützpunktschule," not the DOSB Eliteschule brand — with Realschule Füssen recognised as an ice-hockey Stützpunktschule partnered with EV Füssen, and neighbouring Gymnasium Hohenschwangau running a boarding house that feeds EV Füssen's youth teams. These two systems (DOSB nationwide vs. Bavaria state-specific) should not be conflated in copy.
- No DEB-level housing/billeting policy was found. Practice is club-level only, as with Sweden: Krefeld has run a boarding house ("Eishockey-Internat") via youth-welfare partner Marianum since 2006; Adler Mannheim's academy has a "Jungadler Wohnheim"; Kölner Haie and EHC Nürnberg publicly recruit host families ("Gastfamilie") for their U20-Division players.
- Germany is EU/Schengen; standard EU/EEA-vs-non-EU framework applies. For a non-EU minor attending a boarding school linked to a club (rather than a sport-specific visa, which does not appear to exist as a distinct category), BAMF cites §16f AufenthG — residence permit for school attendance, explicitly including long-term schooling such as boarding school — as the applicable basis. A minor travelling without both parents is understood to need a notarised consent declaration from all custody holders (valid up to 6 months) and, per §80 Abs. 4 AufenthG, a person resident in Germany designated to exercise parental responsibility during the stay; this second citation could not be independently re-verified against the primary Auswärtiges Amt page (unreachable at check time) and needs re-confirmation before publication.
- DEB maintains a documented Safe Sport toolkit rather than one single binding policy: a Handlungsleitfaden (December 2023), a legal-orientation guide (June 2024), a Merkblatt for clubs to build their own Kinderschutzkonzept, an Interventionsleitfaden, and model behaviour-code/consent templates, with a named contact. This is guidance for clubs to self-implement rather than one uniform top-down mandate, and should be described that way rather than implying every club already has an active programme.

## Publication note

Two items are flagged as needing a second verification pass before being stated as firm fact: the Art. 55 domestic-window vs. IIHF Art. I.5 minor-deadline-waiver interaction, and the §80 Abs. 4 AufenthG custodian-designation requirement. The public page should state both cautiously. Retain the general disclaimer to verify current rules with DEB, IIHF, BAMF and the club, and do not present a specific school or host-family arrangement as guaranteed or federation-vetted.
