Why good hockey statistics do not always bring club offers

A player produces points, sends a profile and receives no concrete response. That alone does not prove the player is too weak or needs a lower league. First identify which question the club still cannot answer.

Viachaslau Marshchonak · Hockey agent and scout · 25 October 2026

Where to start

Statistics describe production in a particular environment. An offer also requires a fit with a specific role, level, set of conditions and timeline. Before rewriting a resume, lowering expectations or paying for a trial, identify where the decision is getting stuck.

This is EHA's professional interpretation for preparing a player profile. It is not a study of rejection rates or an explanation of an individual coach's decision. Only feedback from that club can establish its reasons.

1. The points lack context

Matching production does not make two profiles equivalent. Assessment needs age relative to opponents, competition category, games played, role and power-play usage. If ice time is unknown, do not invent it or calculate efficiency with false precision.

Prepare: a verifiable statistics link, season, club, league and a short description of the role. If production increased, explain what changed: team, competition, linemates or usage. Better numbers invite a question; they do not by themselves prove readiness for a higher level.

2. The role is unclear

“Productive forward” does not tell a team whether it is looking at a centre, winger, power-play option or someone suited to another task. A defenceman's points do not describe exits, decisions under pressure or off-puck work. A goaltender needs a different assessment from a skater.

Prepare: two or three specific strengths and footage that shows them. Replace “all-round defenceman” with an observable action, such as receiving under pressure and finding a first pass. Do not claim a quality that the available footage cannot establish.

3. The video only shows successful finishes

A goals package can introduce a player quickly while leaving questions about shifts without points: support routes, recovery, turnovers and repeatable decisions. Camera angles also limit assessment. An action missing from the footage is not necessarily a missing skill.

Prepare: a recent full game, jersey number and colour, date, opponent and a few useful timestamps. For a goalie, retain the build-up before a shot and the actions after a save. See what video a club needs.

4. The club's need has not been checked

A club at a broadly suitable level may not be recruiting your position. A familiar name or a history of signing imports does not establish an opening now. Silence alone does not reveal the reason either.

Check: the current roster, position, proposed role and actual recruitment need. A country shortlist is a starting point for research. A targeted presentation should explain the fit with a specific task, as well as the player's wish to move. See how to find a hockey club in Europe.

5. Sporting interest does not match the family's conditions

Even where a player interests a club, costs, availability and contract status need separate agreement. A junior also needs an education, housing and responsible-adult plan. A sporting assessment does not establish registration or permission to work; an EU passport does not replace checking a particular league's rules.

Prepare: an affordable budget and what it includes, flexibility on countries, dates and essential family conditions. Check offers in writing. Families can use the parent pathway and season budget comparison.

6. The materials or timing leave the decision unresolved

A profile without a season, an outdated team, a private video link or an unclear release date creates more questions. A complete package still does not create a vacancy or oblige a club to reply.

Prepare: a short hockey resume, working links, current status and availability. Check the package and recipient's relevance first. Sending more identical messages will not correct a mismatch with the market.

Illustrative example: equal points, different questions

This is a fictional scenario, not an EHA client case. Two forwards each record 40 points in 40 games. One supplies a full game and a clear role description; the other provides only scoring clips, with team usage unknown.

That does not establish that the first is a better player. It establishes that less evidence is available for the second. Request context and footage next, rather than assigning a precise ranking or promising a particular division.

What a player or parent can do now

  • Check the current club, statistics, links and contract status.
  • Describe a proposed role and provide supporting footage.
  • Separate preferences from confirmed club needs.
  • Write down availability, budget and essential conditions.
  • If the reason for silence is unknown, say so rather than labelling it a rejection based on level.

For an EHA free initial profile check, send the profile, current statistics, available footage and goal. In the message, mention previous approaches and any feedback received. Do not send passport or medical documents.

The initial check establishes whether the materials are sufficient and what the next step could be.
