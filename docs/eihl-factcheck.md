# EIHL 2026/27: season dates fact-check

## 2026-11-15 review

Sources read (official Elite League site, eliteleague.co.uk):
- Article 5442, "50 days to go": 270 league games and 54 Challenge Cup group-stage fixtures; all ten teams return on Saturday 12 September 2026 with five Challenge Cup games; the first league games are on Sunday 20 September 2026; the final round of regular-season fixtures is on Sunday 4 April 2027.
- Article 5433, "Opening Weekend revealed for 2026/27": the season begins on Saturday 12 September.

Corrected on the EN and RU pages (`leagues/uk-eihl`, `ligi/velikobritaniya-eihl`): the pages said the regular season ran "from 12 September 2026"; the league fixtures actually start on 20 September, and 12 September is the opening of the season with Challenge Cup games. The end date, 4 April 2027, is unchanged. The statement that the top eight enter the playoffs was kept from the 2 October review and was not re-checked on this date (the playoff dates are not in the sources above).

Source links replaced: `eliteleague.co.uk/the-eihl` and `eliteleague.co.uk/key-dates` were fetched on 15 November 2026 and returned unrelated third-party betting content, not league information. Both links were removed from the pages and replaced with articles 5442 and 5433. Check these two URLs again before using them anywhere else.

Wording: the EN title, meta and lead now include "fixtures" and "British", which the page lacked for searches such as "eihl fixtures" and "british elite league".

## 2026-11-15 review: roster and import rules

Question: what is the EIHL rule on "imports" for 2026/27 (the pages previously said it was not verified).

Primary sources (eliteleague.co.uk), read on 15 November 2026:
- Article 3944, "Roster Regulations for 2024/25": season roster 25 (was 29); at least one British goaltender per team; two-way players excluded from the 25 and may play 20 league games before they count; a full-regulations PDF is linked (document 95) but the link returned a client error, so the PDF was not read.
- Article 4646, "Updated Roster Regulations for 2025/26": season cap 25, players under 20 on 1 August do not count; two-way means under 25 on 1 August; injured reserve 42 days for skaters, 14 for goaltenders. Document 135 (PDF) also returned a client error and was not read.
- Article 5504, "Opening Rosters 2026/27": non-homegrown players per team at the 12 September opening are 16, 15, 17, 17, 17, 18, 16, 16, 16, 15 (Belfast, Cardiff, Coventry, Dundee, Fife, Glasgow, Guildford, Manchester, Nottingham, Sheffield), so the range is 15 to 18. Article 5539 ("Roster Updates 2026/27") shows one change (Sheffield at 16 non-homegrown).
- The league's featured block shows an article titled "Roster Regulations updated for 2026/27", but its own URL could not be found and its text was not opened.

Secondary sources for the 2026/27 figures:
- Pro Hockey News, "EIHL Roster Regulations updated for 2026/27" (read from a search extract; the page itself is blocked to our fetcher by robots.txt, so it was not opened): maximum of 15 non-homegrown players on a gameday roster; gameday roster 18 skaters and two goalies; 25 players for the season; at most 20 non-homegrown players used in a season and at most 18 registered at one time; two-way players may play 20 games (Challenge Cup group games excluded) before they count; a homegrown player on injured reserve may be replaced only by another homegrown player; changes approved at the July EIHL Board Meeting.
- Yahoo Sports (Cardiff Devils signing report): same 20-player gameday roster, 15 non-homegrown, one British goaltender; two-way players must be homegrown, under 25, contracted mainly to a team in a lower English or Scottish league.

What the pages now say: the numbers above, labelled as "rules as reported after the July board meeting", plus an explicit statement that the definition of a homegrown player and the full regulations text were not confirmed from a primary source. Sources list on the EN and RU pages names the league articles and labels the Pro Hockey News item as a trade-press report.

Not verified, do not publish as fact: the league's definition of "homegrown" (the old British-trained definition from the 2021-era rules is not carried over); whether a 2026/27 PDF exists and matches the trade-press summary; the exact wording on goaltenders for 2026/27 (the 2024/25 league article and the Yahoo report agree, nothing newer was read).

Playoffs: a snippet of the league's `/the-eihl` page says seeds run from #1 to #8, which supports "the top eight enter the playoffs". That page carries injected third-party betting text (see the 15 November note above), so the claim stays on the page but is marked as supported by one weak source.
