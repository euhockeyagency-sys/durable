Net vs gross in European hockey: what €2,000 means in Germany and Poland

Two labelled 2026 examples — a German and a Polish employment contract — with every assumption stated, plus how housing and bonuses change the package.

Germany · Poland · Housing and bonuses · Next step

Short answer

“€2,000” means very different money in Germany and in Poland. On a German employment contract, €2,000 gross a month loses about €423–435 to the employee's social contributions in 2026 before wage tax. On a Polish employment contract (umowa o pracę), the same gross in złoty loses about 13.71% to social contributions, 9% of the rest to health insurance and then income tax — in our example about 72% is left. A “net €2,000” offer is something else again: the club pays the tax and contributions on top.

Always ask three things: is the figure gross or net, per month or per season, and on which type of contract. The examples below are illustrations for 2026 with stated assumptions, not tax advice.

Rates: 2026 social insurance values (Arbeitskammer des Saarlandes, January 2026). Your health insurer's own additional rate can differ from the 2.9% average. At exactly €2,000 a month the reduced “transition zone” for low earnings (up to €2,000) gives practically no relief. A non-EU player on the German athlete route needs a much higher salary — €50,700 gross a year in 2026 — so this example mainly concerns EU players.

Contract type changes everything. Many Polish clubs use civil-law or business-to-business contracts instead of an employment contract; contributions, health insurance and tax are then calculated differently, and some costs move to the player. Ask the club which contract you will sign and ask its accountant for a net calculation in writing.

Comparing offers from two countries? First check that both leagues fit your level. Take the EHA Level Calculator: it gives an indicative league range in two minutes. It does not check citizenship, age rules or registration, so treat the result as preliminary.

Housing and bonuses change the package

  • Housing, car, meals: a flat or car paid by the club can count as taxable income in kind in both countries, depending on how it is provided and reported. Ask whether the club reports it on the payslip.
  • Bonuses: points, game or playoff bonuses are usually paid through payroll and taxed like salary; compare them net.
  • Season vs year: a “monthly” salary paid only for 8 months is a different annual income from 12 payments. Divide the guaranteed season total by 12 before you compare.
  • “Tax-free”: treat a payment as tax-free only if the club names the legal basis for your specific case in writing.

Frequently asked questions

Is a hockey salary in Europe quoted gross or net?

Both happen. Always ask whether the figure is gross or net, per month or per season, and on which contract type.

How much of €2,000 gross is left in Germany?

Employee social contributions take about €423–435 in 2026; wage tax comes on top and depends on tax class — use the official BMF calculator.

How much of 8,500 PLN gross is left in Poland?

On an employment contract with PIT-2 filed, about 6,125 PLN in 2026. Civil-law or B2B contracts give a different result.

Is club housing tax-free?

Not automatically. It can count as taxable income in kind; ask how the club reports it.
