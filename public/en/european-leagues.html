How to compare leagues correctly

EHA assessment: import-openness labels are market guidance, not confirmed vacancies, quotas or registration permission. A level number describes a place within the relevant system; the same number in two countries does not mean equal playing strength. Cross-border leagues and North American competitions require separate comparisons.

  1. Sporting fit: compare your current league, role, statistics and recent full-game video.
  2. Eligibility: open the league guide and confirm current-season rules with the federation. Citizenship, local development and import status are different criteria.
  3. Terms: check cash pay, housing and costs using the offer comparison checklist.
  4. Readiness: establish the IIHF transfer, domestic registration and right to work before travel.

What to do with the filtered results

Choose a few relevant league guides and compare the actual sporting role, current rules and full costs. Treat an attractive league name as a question to investigate, not proof that the level fits.

  • Unsure about your level? Use the level calculator as a first orientation, then review full-game evidence.
  • Considering an import route? Read the import-market guide and confirm the current rule for the player.
  • Already have an offer? Compare net/gross pay, paid months, housing and expenses.
  • Choosing for a child? Start with the parent guide, including school, housing and the family budget.

For official international-transfer documents, use IIHF Rules & Regulations. Domestic eligibility and immigration need their own checks. See how EHA distinguishes official rules from assessments.

Questions about European hockey leagues

Which European hockey league is the strongest?

Specify what you mean by strongest: team results, roster quality or fit for a particular player. This guide compares domestic divisions and player routes; it does not present a universal ranking of all European leagues.

Does level 2 mean the same standard in every country?

No. A division number describes a position in its own competition system. Compare actual teams, player roles and game video rather than treating matching numbers as equal strength.

What does the import-openness filter mean?

It groups EHA market assessments. Open, limited or hard does not confirm a vacancy, import quota, registration permission or a right to work. Verify current-season rules and the individual player's eligibility.

Are all senior leagues professional and paid?

Do not assume a division number determines pay or employment status. Ask the actual club about the contract, paid period, housing, insurance and costs. Salary figures require a source and date.

Where should parents compare U18 and U20 options?

Use the separate junior league reference and parent guide. Age eligibility, school, supervision, housing and the full family budget need to be considered together.

When can a player join a European club?

Recruitment timing depends on the team, competition and season. A club's recruitment decision, a federation transfer deadline and immigration processing are separate issues. Confirm all relevant dates before travel or payment.
