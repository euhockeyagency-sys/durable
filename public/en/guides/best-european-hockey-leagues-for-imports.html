Top 10 European hockey leagues for imports in 2026/27: level, import rules and pay

Ten leagues with checked 2026/27 rules for foreign players, grouped by level — what each one allows, what is known about pay and who it suits.

Comparison table · Who each league suits · Pay · Next step

Short answer

For the 2026/27 season, the European leagues with the clearest room for import players are the ICE Hockey League, the EIHL, Ligue Magnus and Poland's TAURON Hokej Liga — each lets a club dress about ten or more players who do not count as local. Switzerland's National League pays at the top of the market but has only six import places per game. DEL2, Erste Liga, Mestis, the Oberliga and France's Division 1 are realistic first steps for players who need a defined role at a lower level.

This is not a quality ranking. We list ten leagues whose 2026/27 rules for foreign players EHA has checked against a regulation or a named report, grouped by level. Pay figures appear only where a source exists; for most leagues there is no reliable salary sample, and we say so instead of guessing.

How we chose the ten leagues

  1. Room for imports in 2026/27. A published game-sheet or registration rule, read in the league's own document or, where marked, in a press report.
  2. A professional or semi-professional season. Senior leagues with a full regular season, not tournaments or camps.
  3. A route EHA can describe. Each league has a page on this site with the rule, the season, the source and the check date.
  4. Different entry levels. From leagues for established professionals to first senior steps, so most players find their range.

Two leagues just missed the list because the door is narrower: the Italian Hockey League (at most three Category B players, goaltenders must be Category A) and Poland's 1 Liga (at least 55% Polish skaters). The full map of divisions is in the European league directory.

Which league suits which player

Established professionals: National League, ICEHL, DEL2

Switzerland's National League allows six foreign players per game and ten per club over the season, so clubs sign imports who change the top of the line-up. The ICEHL has more import places — up to ten on a 22-player sheet under the 2022 rule — and EHA estimates pay at roughly €35,000–150,000+ per season. DEL2 has four foreign places per game and counts EU citizens as foreign, so it suits players who can take a top-six, top-four or starting-goaltender role at once.

Many import places: EIHL, Ligue Magnus, TAURON Hokej Liga

The EIHL dresses up to 15 non-homegrown players per game; non-UK players need a UK sportsperson visa. Ligue Magnus counts locally trained (JFL) players instead of passports and allows up to ten non-JFL players per sheet. The TAURON Hokej Liga requires at least ten Polish or Karta Polaka players per roster, which leaves up to twelve other places on a full roster.

A first senior step or a defined role: Erste Liga, Mestis, Oberliga, Division 1

Erste Liga allows five foreign players per game and eleven registrations a season, so in-season openings are common. Mestis publishes no foreign quota; a club signs an import only when it has a need beyond its own players and Liiga loans. The Oberliga is reported at three imports per game and suits EU players best, because the German athlete visa needs €50,700 gross a year in 2026. France's Division 1 allows up to seven non-JFL players on a 20-player sheet.

What is known about pay

Most of these leagues publish no salary scale. The EHA estimate exists for the ICEHL (€35,000–150,000+ per season); for TAURON Hokej Liga, DEL2, the Oberliga and Erste Liga the only public figures are third-party ranges, shown on each league page as examples, not averages. For the National League, EIHL, Ligue Magnus, Mestis and Division 1 we have no reliable sample and quote no number.

Two numbers are hard requirements rather than salaries: a non-EU player needs at least €50,700 gross a year for the German athlete route (2026) and at least €1,149 gross a month for the Finnish athlete permit (2026). Compare offers on guaranteed income after tax and after every cost you pay yourself — housing, car, flights, insurance, equipment. The detailed guide is Hockey player salaries in Europe.

Passport, status and registration

“Import” means something different in each league: German citizenship in DEL2, JFL training status in France, Polish citizenship or a Karta Polaka in Poland, homegrown status in the UK, Swiss or “like a Swiss” status in Switzerland. An EU passport helps with the right to live and work in EU countries, but it does not always free an import place. See EU vs non-EU passport in hockey.

A rule in this table says a club may sign a player like you. It is not a guarantee of a contract, a registration or a roster place: the club, the federation and the immigration office each decide separately.

Frequently asked questions

Which European hockey league has the most import places?

Among the leagues on this list, the EIHL allows the most: up to 15 non-homegrown players per game in 2026/27, according to the league release reported by trade press. Ligue Magnus allows up to 10 non-JFL players, the ICEHL up to 10 imports under its 2022 rule, and the TAURON Hokej Liga up to 12 non-Polish players on a full roster.

Which European league pays imports the most?

Of these ten, the Swiss National League is generally the top of the market, but it publishes no scale and EHA has no reliable sample, so we quote no number. EHA's estimate for the ICEHL is about €35,000–150,000+ per season.

Is this a ranking of the best leagues?

No. It is a list of ten leagues with checked 2026/27 import rules, grouped by level. The right league depends on your level, passport, age and role.

Does an EU passport mean I am not an import?

Not always. DEL2 counts anyone without German citizenship as foreign; France counts JFL training status; Poland counts Polish citizenship or a Karta Polaka.
