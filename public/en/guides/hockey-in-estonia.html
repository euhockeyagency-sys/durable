Hockey in Estonia: leagues, visas, work and transfers

A practical guide for players and families: senior and youth levels, international players, contracts, registration, international transfer and lawful residence.

01

How hockey in Estonia is structured

The Estonian Ice Hockey Federation (Eesti Jäähokiliit) organises senior, youth and women’s competitions. The latest fully confirmed structure is for 2025/26; the 2026/27 participants and rules should be checked after official publication.

It is a compact market. A player’s role, passport, club budget and the team’s ability to complete registration and lawful residence or employment formalities matter alongside performance.

02

The federation’s official 2025/26 Unibet Hokiliiga page lists five clubs. That number and line-up should not be carried forward automatically to 2026/27.

Before negotiating, request the exact team, competition, role, registration deadline and financial conditions. Playing in a national championship does not by itself prove professional employment status.

03

Youth and women’s hockey

The federation publishes U19, U16 and U14 championships, plus younger U12, U10 and U8 competition. A separate women’s Hokiliiga is also organised.

For a minor international player, confirm age eligibility, school, housing, guardian, insurance and parental consent separately. A team place does not replace education or immigration arrangements.

04

Choosing a realistic level

Clubs assess the previous league, skating, decision speed, play away from the puck, physical readiness, special-teams role, current video and verifiable statistics. Numbers without level context are insufficient.

Compare the offer with the team’s actual status and game schedule. Clarify whether it is a contract, tryout, amateur registration or youth programme.

05

International players and import limits

A single public import limit across every Estonian competition cannot be confirmed. Rules can differ by league, age group and season.

Before accepting: ask the club for the current rules and written confirmation that your passport, age and previous federation are eligible.
06

Contract, salary and conditions

There is no single official salary table. Put the following in writing:

  • gross or net amount, currency, payment dates, bonuses and taxes;
  • housing, deposit, utilities, meals and transport;
  • equipment, sticks, medical care and insurance;
  • tryout, injury, termination and national-team release terms;
  • who pays for the visa, employment registration, IIHF transfer and federation fees.

Verify the legal entity and signatory authority. Verbal promises do not replace a signed agreement.

07

Housing and costs

Costs depend on city, housing type and club conditions, so one national figure would be unreliable. Ask for the address, rent, deposit, utilities, furniture, arena transport and move-in dates.

Calculate the amount left after taxes and compulsory expenses. If housing is promised, the contract should state whether it is free, deducted from salary and who pays the deposit.

08

EU/EEA and non-EU players

EU/EEA citizens benefit from free movement, but longer residence remains subject to applicable Estonian registration requirements. Hockey registration is a separate process.

For a non-EU player, the official route may involve short-term employment registration or a temporary residence permit for employment. The correct option depends on citizenship, duration, contract and the nature of the work.

09

Long-stay D visa and specific restrictions

Estonia’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs states that a long-stay D visa may be issued for up to 365 days within twelve consecutive months. A visa does not replace employment registration or a residence permit where either is required.

Specific restrictions apply to Russian and Belarusian citizens regarding visa applications and entry. No universal player route can be confirmed; the club and consulate must check the individual legal basis, place of application and any applicable exception before contract signature.

Important: a club invitation, tryout or hockey registration does not itself guarantee a visa, entry or the right to work.
10

IIHF transfer, federation registration and insurance

  1. International transfer: a move from another national federation requires an IIHF transfer.
  2. Registration: the club confirms deadlines and eligibility for the exact competition.
  3. Employment and residence: sporting eligibility does not replace immigration formalities.
  4. Insurance: confirm cover for tryouts, training, games, injury and treatment.
11

Player documents

  • valid passport;
  • signed agreement or official club letter;
  • visa, employment-registration or residence-permit documents;
  • last hockey registration and information for the IIHF transfer;
  • medical records and insurance;
  • for a minor: consent, guardian, school and housing arrangements.

The final list is set by the club, federation, IIHF and immigration authorities. Do not buy a non-refundable ticket before entry and documentation are confirmed.

12

Recruitment timing and tryouts

Rosters are generally built before the season; late openings depend on injuries, budgets and team changes. In a compact market, early contact and an exact role fit matter.

Before a tryout, confirm dates, format, costs, insurance, housing, contact person, decision criteria and response deadline. Use the EHA tryout checklist.

13

Resume, video, sources and FAQ

Send one package with a hockey resume, Elite Prospects, verifiable statistics, current highlights and a recent full game or shifts. State citizenship, contract status, languages and availability.

Verify the team, signatory, pay, housing, insurance, visa, employment, transfer and termination with the EHA offer checklist.

What are the main levels in Estonian hockey?

The federation runs Unibet Hokiliiga, youth championships and the women’s Hokiliiga. Teams and rules must be checked in official materials for the exact season.

Can an international player compete in Estonia?

Eligibility depends on the competition rules, passport, federation registration and lawful residence or employment basis. The club should confirm eligibility in writing.

How can a non-EU player be employed?

The route commonly involves short-term employment registration or a temporary residence permit for employment. The exact process depends on duration, contract and citizenship.

Can a Russian or Belarusian citizen rely on the ordinary work-visa route?

No universal route can be confirmed because Estonia applies specific restrictions to visa applications and entry. The club and consulate must check the individual basis and any exception before contract signature.

Is an IIHF international transfer required?

Yes when a player moves from a club or federation in another country. A transfer does not replace a visa, employment registration or residence rights.
