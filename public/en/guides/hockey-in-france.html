Hockey in France: leagues, imports and work rules

A practical orientation for players. Re-check league, registration and immigration requirements for the current season.

01

League structure

France has a deep national senior pyramid under the FFHG:

  • Synerglace Ligue Magnus — top national professional division;
  • Division 1 — second national senior level;
  • Division 2 — third national senior level;
  • Division 3 — fourth national senior level.

The federation also runs U20 and U18 national structures, U15 competition, women’s elite hockey and the Coupe de France. Use the current 2026/27 FFHG documents for format and player eligibility.

03

JFL rules: more important than passport alone

French senior roster rules are built around JFL — joueurs formés localement (locally trained players), not a simple foreign-passport quota. FFHG defines JFL independently of nationality, mainly through four full seasons of hockey activity in affiliated French clubs by age 20, subject to the detailed transitional rules.

For a 20-player game sheet in 2026/27, the minimum JFL numbers are 10 in Ligue Magnus, 13 in Division 1 and 15 in Division 2, each including at least one goaltender. Division 3 permits a maximum of three non-JFL players on a 22-player sheet and requires at least one JFL goaltender. U20 also permits a maximum of three non-JFL players on a 22-player sheet.

Practical effect: “foreign player” and “non-JFL” are not the same thing. Before any offer, the club must confirm the player’s JFL/non-JFL status, available roster place and current qualification with FFHG.
04

Junior route and age

FFHG runs national U20 and U18 structures and U15 competition. For 2026/27, Ligue Espoir U20 and U18 calendars are published and active.

Junior players should be assessed on development potential, birth-year eligibility, education, housing, expected ice time and JFL consequences. A non-JFL U20 or U21 player with a professional player contract is not eligible for the U20 championships under the 2026/27 activity rules; a non-professional non-JFL U20/U21 player can play U20 but loses that eligibility after appearing in five senior games.

05

EU/EEA/Swiss and non-EU players

EU/EEA and Swiss nationals have a different employment route from third-country nationals. For a worker who is not an EU, EEA or Swiss national, official Service-Public guidance states that the employer generally needs the appropriate work authorisation, unless the player already holds a visa or residence document that gives employment rights.

Important: JFL status, citizenship, FFHG qualification and the legal right to work are separate checks.
06

Contract, salary and club conditions

There is no official universal salary table for French hockey. Before accepting, put in writing:

  • gross or net salary, payment dates, bonuses and tax handling;
  • housing, deposit, utilities, meals and local transport;
  • equipment, sticks, medical care and insurance;
  • trial period, injury, release and termination clauses;
  • who pays transfer, licence, immigration and travel costs.

Participation in Magnus, D1 or D2 does not by itself prove a particular salary or housing package.

07

Housing and cost of living

There is no single official monthly “hockey player budget” for France. Rent, utilities, transport, insurance and food vary sharply between hockey cities and by what the club provides. Ask for the exact housing address, rent, deposit, utilities, transport to the rink and every player-paid cost before comparing offers.

Compare the amount left after tax and compulsory costs rather than headline salary alone.

08

Transfer, FFHG qualification, visa and work

  1. International transfer: a move between national associations normally follows the current IIHF process.
  2. FFHG licence and qualification: the receiving club must complete the federation requirements before the player is eligible. FFHG publishes and updates the Magnus qualified-player list after new validations.
  3. Residence: the player needs the correct basis for the intended stay.
  4. Work: the employer and player must have the required employment authorisation or a residence/visa status that already grants work rights.

General orientation only, not legal advice. Re-check the current season with the club and FFHG, and immigration requirements with the official authority or competent consulate.

09

Recruitment timing and tryouts

The main roster-building window is generally spring and summer. Later needs can appear because of injuries, budgets or roster adjustments, but JFL balance can strongly affect whether a non-JFL player can actually be added.

Before a tryout, confirm dates, number of sessions and games, insurance, housing, costs, responsible contact, decision criteria and reply deadline.

10

Player package and documents

  • current Elite Prospects and verifiable statistics;
  • recent highlights plus a full game or full shifts;
  • position, shot, height/weight, citizenship and contract status;
  • passport and documents needed for international transfer;
  • signed offer or contract and immigration/work documents where applicable;
  • for juniors: school, housing, guardian and parental-consent documents where required.

A club should be able to understand the player’s level, role, availability and JFL implications quickly.

11

EHA assessment

EHA assessment — not an FFHG rule: France offers unusual depth from Magnus through D3 and a strong U18/U20 pathway. For a player developed outside France, the central roster question is often not nationality itself but whether the player counts as non-JFL and whether the club has room within its JFL balance.

What are the main senior levels?

Synerglace Ligue Magnus, Division 1, Division 2 and Division 3.

What is JFL?

JFL means locally trained player. It is based on the FFHG training-history definition, not simply nationality.

Is an international transfer normally required?

Yes for a move between national associations; the club and federation confirm the exact steps.

How can a non-EU player work in France?

The employer generally needs the appropriate work authorisation unless the player already holds a visa or residence status that grants work rights.

When should a player apply?

Usually during spring and summer, with later openings possible during the season.
