Hockey academies in Europe: cost, conditions and how to get in

Czechia, Sweden, Finland and Austria compared: admission, school, housing, what a tariff includes and which payments come on top.

Four countries · What's included · How to apply · Questions · Next step

Short answer

In Czechia, Sweden, Finland and Austria a “hockey academy” is usually a club's youth programme combined with a school, not a boarding product with a public price list. Admission means two separate decisions — the club's selection and the school's enrolment — plus the federation's rules for a foreign minor and, outside the EU, a residence permit. We found published club fees (for example CZK 17,900 a season in Czechia and SEK 17,000 in Sweden) but no published season tariff for a foreign boarder in any of the four countries, so ask each academy for a written, itemised offer.

What a tariff may include — and what often comes on top

When an academy quotes a price, ask it to tick each line as included, extra or not provided:

  • ice time, coaching, off-ice training, video;
  • registration and the international transfer;
  • housing (host family, dormitory, flat) and how many meals a day;
  • school fees, books, uniform, language lessons;
  • health and accident insurance;
  • team kit and personal equipment, sticks;
  • away games, tournaments, camps;
  • transport between home, school and rink;
  • flights home, visa or permit fees;
  • deposit, refund and exit terms if the player is cut, injured or leaves.

How to apply

  1. Send the academy a short profile: birth year, position, current league, statistics and a recent full game.
  2. Ask which team the player would be registered with and the expected role.
  3. Ask the school, separately, about enrolment, language and timetable.
  4. Check the federation's under-18 rule for the country (table above) and the IIHF transfer.
  5. Outside the EU, check the residence route with the consulate before paying anything.
  6. Get the full offer in writing and compare it in the estimator.

Questions to ask the academy

  • Which legal entity receives the money and is it the club itself?
  • How many foreign players are on the team this season, and in which age group?
  • Who is the responsible adult for the player outside training?
  • What happens to school and housing if the player is moved to another team?
  • Can we speak to a family whose child spent last season in the academy?

Frequently asked questions

How much does a hockey academy in Europe cost?

There is no public market price. We found club fees (CZK 17,900 a season at VHK Vsetín, SEK 17,000 for Tyresö Hanviken's junior team) but no published season tariff for a foreign boarder in Czechia, Sweden, Finland or Austria. Ask for an itemised written offer.

Can a foreign player under 18 join a European academy?

It depends on the country's federation rule and on residence. Sweden asks for home-federation evidence, a representative and education; Finland requires the guardian to move for a permanent transfer; Austria licenses players only from 1 June to 15 February.

Is admission to the school automatic once the club selects the player?

No. The club's selection and the school's enrolment are separate decisions, and outside the EU the residence permit is a third.

Does an academy guarantee ice time?

No academy can promise minutes. Ask which team the player is registered with and how the role will be reviewed.
