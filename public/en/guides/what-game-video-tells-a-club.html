For clubs

What game video actually tells a club

Highlights show peaks. A recruiter uses game video to understand how a player behaves across shifts, roles and changing situations.

Start with the role you need

Watch the footage against the position and role in your roster. A centre, defenceman, winger and goalie leave different evidence in the game. Before pressing play, write down the two or three behaviours that matter most for the vacancy.

Watch decisions before outcomes

Goals and assists are easy to notice, but the decision before the result is often more useful. Look at scanning, support routes, puck protection, first passes, gap control, rebound decisions and recovery after a turnover. A missed chance can still show a good read; a successful play can be difficult to repeat.

Compare shifts, not just clips

  • How often does the player get into the right position without the puck?
  • Does the player keep pace when the play moves in the opposite direction?
  • Can the player make the same role-specific decision repeatedly?
  • What changes against stronger pressure or later in the game?
  • Does the player respond to coaching and teammates?

A full game gives context that a selected highlight cannot. It should be considered alongside level, role, statistics and the club’s own live evaluation.

Check the video’s limits

Camera angle, ice time, broadcast quality and opposition can distort what a recruiter sees. Ask whether the footage is recent, whether the player is clearly identified and whether the competition represents the level under discussion. Video supports evaluation; it does not establish citizenship, registration approval, medical clearance or employment authorization.

Turn observations into a trial brief

Use the video to define what should be checked in person: skating pace, defensive detail, physical readiness, communication or execution under your system’s pressure. That makes the trial a test of specific questions rather than a general impression.
