Documents and the IIHF transfer for a minor hockey player: steps and dates

A move abroad involves five separate approvals: the club offer, the school, the immigration authority, the IIHF transfer and national registration. Each is decided by a different body. Here are the IIHF steps with their dates and the national documents that have confirmed rules.

Five approvals, five bodies

A club invitation is not a completed move. Five things are decided separately, by different parties:

  1. the club's written offer and the responsible adult;
  2. the school's admission, where a permit depends on it;
  3. the immigration authority's decision on a residence permit or visa;
  4. the IIHF transfer between national associations;
  5. registration with the national association and club before an official game.

Clearance in one area does not replace another. For the offer itself, see the 15-point checklist.

The IIHF transfer, step by step

The IIHF International Transfer Regulations, in effect from 6 October 2025, set these steps. Article numbers are in brackets.

  1. Request. The national association of the club that wants to register the player submits an online request to the player's current association through the IIHF Online Transfer System (art. I.3.1). Any player who wants to compete under another association needs a transfer (I.2.1).
  2. Seven days to answer. The deadline is seven days after the system issues the request notice to the current association, or any extension (I.1.1, I.3.2). The IIHF may extend it on request if there are reasonable grounds.
  3. Silence means approval. If the current association does not approve or refuse before the deadline, the transfer is approved automatically (I.3.3).
  4. The approval date matters. The transfer is approved on the date the new association receives the system's confirmation. From that date the player is under the new association's jurisdiction, may start to play for the new club, and must stop playing for the former club (I.3.4).
  5. Refusal is limited. The current association may refuse only if the player has a professional contract with a club in that association, has not met contractual or financial obligations to the former club (including unreturned equipment), or a material reason exists between the clubs other than compensation, such as a confirmed suspension (I.3.5). It must state its reasons and upload evidence.
  6. Appeal. The new association may appeal a refusal for the player within seven days, with documents translated into English; the current association then has seven days to respond; the IIHF decides on written submissions only (Part II, art. 1.2 to 1.6). An IIHF decision can be appealed to the IIHF Disciplinary Board within seven days, with a fee of CHF 1,000 (II.1.7 to 1.9).

What is different for a player under 18

  • No transfer window. The general window, from 1 July to 15 February for Northern Hemisphere members, does not apply to players under 18 (I.5.2, I.4.8). National rules can still impose a window; see the calendar by month.
  • No transfer fee. For a transfer completed before the player's 18th birthday, no transfer fee applies (I.5.1). Whether a national registration or licensing fee can still be charged to a minor is not confirmed.
  • Contracts. The IIHF views 18 as the legal age of majority for signing a contract unless national law shows otherwise. A contract for a player under 18 is permitted only if consistent with national law and may not run longer than three years; a longer clause is not recognised (I.8.7, I.8.8). Education and hockey-school agreements count as contracts.
  • Tryouts. The player's current association may give written permission for exhibition games during a tryout of up to 15 days from the first game; a longer tryout needs new written permission (I.10).
  • Limited or unlimited. A limited transfer lasts for the new contract's duration and needs a new transfer to extend. A permanent unlimited transfer needs an executed request form and makes the player a full member of the new association (I.4).

What the IIHF regulations do not cover

The regulations contain no rule on parents, guardians, relocation or school for a minor. Those come from national federations and from immigration authorities. They also do not say which documents the club's association uploads or what the IIHF's own fixed fee is: both are in the system guidelines, which are not public. The following national points have been recorded by EHA from federation rules; confirm the current rule with the federation of the destination country.

  • Sweden. The Swedish federation's under-18 rule requires documentation from the home federation that the player is at the highest level or nationally relevant, written documentation of a guardian or a representative appointed by the parents, and access to civil education or vocational training. An unlimited transfer is not allowed in the normal case.
  • Finland. A permanent transfer of a player under 18 requires the guardian to relocate to Finland permanently with the player, with proof attached.
  • Germany. For a player not yet 18 the formal transfer card is not required; a written or electronic confirmation from the former federation suffices.
  • Slovakia. The legal guardian's consent is required as co-signatory to a minor's transfer.
  • Czechia. No additional restriction beyond the IIHF baseline was found, which is not confirmation that none exists.
  • Austria. The domestic licensing window of 1 June to 15 February applies regardless of age.

Immigration and travel documents with confirmed dates

Sweden

Migrationsverket lists a processing time of three months for a first application for other studies and exchange studies at upper secondary school, and seven months for an extension. This is the time within which 75 percent of recently decided cases were completed, based on the last 12 months; it is not a guarantee. The first application must be made from outside Sweden, and the other requirements are in the school and education guide.

Canada

IRCC's guide for applying for a study permit outside Canada (page dated 6 October 2026) says children aged 17 or less who come to study without an accompanying parent or legal guardian need a custodian in Canada. Minors between 17 and the provincial age of majority may need one at an immigration officer's discretion. The custodian must live in Canada and be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident aged 19 or over. The parents or guardians abroad and the custodian complete a notarised Custodianship Declaration (form IMM 5646), pages 1 and 2 of which go with the application. A custodian is not mandatory for a minor travelling with one parent or an authorised guardian, but signed consent from the other parent should be provided. The guide gives no processing time for custodianship and says processing varies by visa office.

Czechia

Czech consular pages say the legal processing time for a long-term residence permit for study is 60 days on at least one post's page. A secondary-school student not on exchange applies for a long-term visa for study, and consent of both parents, documents not older than 180 days and a Czech-language enrolment confirmation are asked for. Posts differ, so confirm with the consulate responsible for your country of residence.

Germany

For a non-EU minor in a club-linked boarding school, the federal migration office BAMF cites the school-attendance residence permit under §16f of the Residence Act. A notarised consent for a minor travelling without both parents is understood to be needed, and the custodian-designation rule is flagged for a second check.

Travelling inside the EU

Your Europe (last checked 14 September 2026) states that there are no EU rules on this: each EU country decides whether it requires an official authorisation. A child travelling alone, with adults who are not legal guardians, or with only one parent may need an extra official document signed by the parents. Transit countries may ask for it, airlines have their own forms and should be asked before departure, and rules can change without notice.

A planning order

This sequence is EHA's suggestion, derived from the rules above. It is not an official procedure.

  1. Get the written offer and the responsible adult confirmed.
  2. Get school admission in writing where the permit depends on it. Sweden needs the applicant finally admitted, and Finland ties the study permit to paying the school's fees.
  3. Apply for the residence permit or visa. In Sweden the first application is made from abroad; in Canada the custodian and notarised forms are needed first.
  4. The club's association requests the IIHF transfer. The player may not play for the new club before the approval date. A national rule such as Sweden's under-18 conditions or Finland's guardian relocation may add conditions.
  5. Obtain the parents' travel authorisation in the form the destination, any transit country and the airline require.
  6. Complete registration with the national association and club before an official game.

Steps 2 to 4 can overlap in time, but one does not clear another.

What EHA cannot confirm

  • The IIHF's fixed transfer fee and the exact documents the club's association uploads.
  • Whether a national registration or licensing fee applies to a minor.
  • Finland's processing time and current requirements for an under-18 student, and visa timing for Germany, Switzerland and Austria.
  • Czech Ministry of Interior guidance, and US visa rules from primary pages.

FAQ

Does IIHF transfer approval give my son the right to work or study in the country?

No. The IIHF transfer decides which national association a player may play under. Immigration status, school admission and national registration are separate approvals.

Who files the IIHF transfer for a minor?

The national association of the club that wants to register the player submits the request in the IIHF Online Transfer System. The player or parent does not file it themselves.

How long does the transfer take?

The player's current federation has seven days from the request notice to approve or refuse. If it does not answer, the transfer is approved automatically. The player may only start for the new club from the approval date.

Do the IIHF regulations require parental consent for a minor?

The regulations contain no rule on parents, guardians, relocation or school. Those requirements come from national federations and immigration authorities and differ by country.
