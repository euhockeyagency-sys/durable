# Slovakia U18/U20 junior hockey — fact-check

Checked: 14 August 2026. Research method: direct retrieval and self-extraction
of SZĽH (Slovak Ice Hockey Federation) regulatory PDFs, the current IIHF
International Transfer Regulations, Slovak sports-school sites, and the
Slovak Act on the Residence of Foreigners (Slov-Lex legal portal). DIRECT =
fetched and read from a primary source. SEARCH = secondary source, not
independently verified against a primary document. Two genuine
inconsistencies were found in the primary sources themselves (below) — do
not silently resolve them; state the uncertainty on the page or confirm
directly with SZĽH before publishing an exact figure.

## 1. League structure

- SZĽH runs the pyramid directly. Senior: Tipsport liga (Extraliga SR, top
  tier) → TIPOS SHL (1. liga) → 2. SHL (2. liga). Junior/youth: **Extraliga
  juniorov** (U20, top tier) → 1. liga juniorov (2nd tier); **Kaufland
  Extraliga dorastu** (U18-equivalent, called "dorast" domestically, top
  tier) → K-Classic 1. liga dorastu (2nd tier); below that, Kadeti, then
  Žiaci (various regional tiers), then Prípravky (youngest).
  Source: https://www.hockeyslovakia.sk/sk/article/sutaze
- Confirms this site's existing content: Tipsport liga and TIPOS SHL are
  correctly the top two senior tiers.
- **Domestic naming ≠ national-team age labels.** Slovakia's national teams
  use IIHF-standard single-year bands (SR 20, SR 18, SR 17, SR 16, SR 15 are
  separate national squads), but the **domestic club competitions use
  Dorast/Juniori/Kadeti**, not a clean single-birth-year "U18"/"U16" split —
  see §2. Do not describe the domestic Dorast league as "U18" without
  qualification.

## 2. Age bands — genuinely unresolved between two SZĽH documents

**Do not publish a specific birth-year cutoff without flagging this
uncertainty, or without confirming directly with SZĽH first.**

- General rule (Súťažný poriadok SZĽH, Art. 3.4.3): Dorastenci = a
  two-birth-year band topping out at age 16 in the relevant season; Juniori
  = a two-birth-year band topping out at age 18; Seniori = 20 and older.
  Source:
  https://www.hockeyslovakia.sk/userfiles/file/S%C3%BA%C5%A5a%C5%BEn%C3%BD%20poriadok(1).pdf
  (Art. 3.4; the document's own amendment date is partially corrupted in
  the PDF text extraction and could not be fully confirmed — likely
  24 March 2021, not verified).
- Concrete 2025/26-season figures (Smernica SZĽH pre 2. ligu seniorov
  2025/2026): U20 = born 2006 or later. For "aged-up" eligibility: a
  **Dorast** player is born 2009 or 2010 (ages 15–16, consistent with the
  general rule); a **Junior** player is born 2006, 2007, or 2008 (ages
  17–19) — a **three-birth-year band topping out at 19**, not 18 as the
  general rule states.
  Source:
  https://www.hockeyslovakia.sk/userfiles/file/Smernica%20pre%202SHL_2025-26%20final%2016%209%2025.pdf
- These two documents do not agree on where "Juniori" tops out (18 vs. 19).
  Confirm the exact current-season Extraliga juniorov/Extraliga dorastu
  birth-year table with SZĽH directly (or the season's Rozpis súťaží
  document) before stating a number on the page.

## 3. International transfer rules for minors

- **No Slovakia-specific "guardian must relocate" rule found.** SZĽH's
  Prestupový poriadok (Transfer Regulation) Art. 15.3 requires only the
  **legal guardian's consent** to a minor's transfer — not relocation or
  residency. Art. 20.7 explicitly states the conditions for a player under
  18 to play abroad are set by **IIHF regulations**, i.e. SZĽH defers to
  the IIHF baseline rather than adding its own condition.
  Source:
  https://www.hockeyslovakia.sk/userfiles/file/Prestupov%C3%BD%20poriadok%20%C3%BA%C4%8Dinn%C3%BD%20od%2001_08_2019.pdf
  (Art. 5.1.1, 9.1.3, 10.1.3, 10a.1.2, 15.3, 20.7)
- This is a genuine, well-sourced contrast to Finland's rule (guardian must
  permanently relocate with the player) — state it plainly: Slovakia
  requires guardian consent as co-signatory, not relocation.
- **IIHF baseline** (2025/26 IIHF International Transfer Regulations,
  effective 6 October 2025): no transfer fee for a player under 18 (Art.
  5.1); the general 1 July–15 February transfer-deadline window does not
  apply to under-18 transfers (Art. 5.2).
  Source:
  https://blob.iihf.com/iihf-media/iihfmvc/media/downloads/regulations/2026/2026_iihf_international_transfer_regulations.pdf
- A foreign player (any age) who has held continuous legal residence in
  Slovakia for 2 years and played two full consecutive seasons for the same
  SZĽH club stops counting as a "foreign player" for roster-limit purposes
  (Art. 23.3) — a quota mechanism, not a transfer precondition.
- **Not confirmed / dated source flag**: the Prestupový poriadok found is
  dated effective 1 August 2019 (an older 2016 version is also still linked
  from SZĽH's own documents page). Given this governs minors, confirm with
  SZĽH's international department or IS SZĽH that no newer version exists
  before publishing.

## 4. Education alongside hockey

SZĽH's own "Smernica k starostlivosti o športovo talentovanú mládež"
(directive on care for sports-talented youth, based on a 2002 Ministry of
Education guideline and the School Act) confirms a formal structure: **CTM**
(Centrá talentovanej mládeže / talent centers) established jointly by a
club, SZĽH and the Ministry of Education at locations with an existing sport
school or sport class; and **ÚTM/športová trieda** (a specialized
school track, 4–8 hours/week of graded sports training), set up by the
school director jointly with the club and federation.
Source:
https://www.hockeyslovakia.sk/userfiles/file/Z%C3%A1pisnice%202016/Smernica%20SZ%C4%BDH%20k%20starostlivosti%20o%20%C5%A1portovo%20talentovan%C3%BA%20ml%C3%A1de%C5%BE.pdf

Confirmed city-specific programs (via each school's own official site):
- **Košice**: Stredná športová škola, Tr. SNP 104 — runs a "Krajská
  hokejová akadémia" (regional hockey academy) with its own admission
  testing, has a school dormitory. https://sportgymke.edupage.org/
- **Trenčín**: Stredná športová škola Trenčín — dedicated ice-hockey track
  in cooperation with HK Dukla Trenčín ("Regionálna hokejová akadémia"),
  ~700-bed dormitory.
  https://www.sportovaskolatn.sk/sportova-priprava/ladovy-hokej ,
  https://www.sportovaskolatn.sk/skolsky-internat.html
- **Nitra**: Stredná športová škola Nitra — most hockey students train with
  MMHK Nitra (HK Nitra's youth arm), 300+-bed dormitory.
  https://www.spojskolanr.sk/hokej , https://www.spojskolanr.sk/internat
- **Poprad**: Stredná športová škola, Kukučínova 4239/1 — on-site training
  ice-hockey hall; a dormitory addition from September 2024 was reported in
  secondary sources but not independently confirmed on the school's own
  site. https://sportpp.edupage.org/
- **Bratislava — not confirmed.** The city's state sports gymnasium
  (Ostredková 10) lists athletics, judo, handball, gymnastics, volleyball
  and wrestling in available material; ice hockey was not confirmed as one
  of its tracks. HC Slovan Bratislava runs a large youth program (reported
  ~400 players, "športové hokejové triedy") but no named partner secondary
  school equivalent to the Trenčín/Nitra/Košice model was found. Confirm
  directly with HC Slovan/ŠK Slovan before stating a Bratislava
  sport-school hockey track exists.

## 5. Housing / billeting

No federation-level housing or billeting program was found in SZĽH's
Súťažný poriadok, Prestupový poriadok, the 2. liga seniorov directive, or
the sports-talented-youth care directive. The only accommodation
infrastructure confirmed is the **school dormitories** attached to the
sport schools in §4 (Košice, Trenčín, Nitra, Poprad) — a schooling
arrangement for enrolled students, not a hockey-federation billeting
program. This is an absence-of-evidence finding, not a confirmed negative —
individual clubs may run informal host-family arrangements not published
anywhere searchable. Present housing as club/academy-specific and to be
confirmed directly, as with every other market on this site.

## 6. Immigration (non-EU minor)

Source: Act No. 404/2011 Coll. on the Residence of Foreigners, consolidated
text as of 15 December 2024 (Slov-Lex):
https://static.slov-lex.sk/static/SK/ZZ/2011/404/20241215.html — cross-checked
against the government PDF mirror at
https://www.minv.sk/swift_data/source/policia/hranicna_a_cudzinecka_policia/pravne_predpisy/zakony/zakon_404_2011_o_pobyte_cudzincov.pdf

- **§25 "special activity" route includes sport (§25(1)(c))**, but it is
  explicitly tied to a **professional sport performance contract** (per the
  Act on Sport, No. 440/2015, §35) and can run up to 5 years matched to the
  contract term — the general cap for other special-activity categories is
  2 years. Because this route requires a pro contract, **it is unlikely to
  be the practical route for a junior/minor player** without one — do not
  present it as the default youth-athlete visa.
- **§27 "family reunification"** is the more likely general route for a
  minor: an unmarried child under 18 of a third-country national who
  **already holds temporary or permanent residence in Slovakia**. This
  requires the sponsoring parent/guardian to already have Slovak residence
  status — structurally similar in spirit to a "guardian must be resident"
  requirement, but this is general Slovak immigration law, not a hockey
  rule.
- A study-purpose residence category likely also exists for a minor
  enrolled at one of the §4 sport schools, but the specific statute article
  was **not directly verified** in this research — confirm before
  publishing.
- No Slovakia-specific youth-athlete visa route was found.

## 7. Recruitment timing

No SZĽH document states a tryout/trial window (checked both the Súťažný
poriadok and Prestupový poriadok for "skúšobná," "nábor," "trial,"
"tryout" — no relevant match found). As with every other market on this
site, timing is club-by-club.
