After the MHL and NMHL: European options for young players from Russia and Belarus

What citizenship changes for a move to Europe: the transfer procedure, entry and work rules by country, and routes that still work.

MHL or NMHL · Country by country · Routes · Checklist · Next step

Short answer

For a young player from the MHL or NMHL the main obstacle to Europe in 2026/27 is usually not the hockey level but citizenship. Finland, the Baltic states, Poland and Czechia have closed or sharply limited work entry for Russian citizens; Belarusian citizens face a mixed picture that differs country by country. Inside the Eurasian Economic Union (for example Kazakhstan) no work permit is needed. Elsewhere in Europe we found no published blanket ban for hockey players, but that does not mean a visa will be issued: each case goes through the consulate and the club.

Rules have changed several times in November 2026 alone. Treat every line below as a starting point and confirm it with the club and the consulate before signing.

What changed in November 2026

  • IIHF: in January 2026 the IIHF Council kept Russia and Belarus out of IIHF events in 2026/27 (IIHF). At the end of October the Court of Arbitration for Sport ordered Russian teams reinstated; several national federations and governments said they would not play or would not issue visas (report). This concerns national-team events, not club contracts, but it hardened visa policy.
  • Finland: from 16 November 2026 Russian athletes, including those competing under a neutral flag, are refused visas for short-term work unless it is essential to Finland's national interest; the rule also applies to transit through Finland (Yle).

MHL or NMHL: what the transfer looks like

The NMHL is run by the Russian Ice Hockey Federation (FHR), which remains an IIHF member: a move abroad goes through the IIHF international transfer procedure (ITC), see IIHF international transfer. The MHL is the KHL's junior league; the KHL describes itself as a non-IIHF organisation, while the 2025 IIHF Transfer Regulations have a separate section on transfers with non-member organisations (IIHF regulations, section 9; KHL statement). Which procedure applies to a particular MHL player depends on his registration and contract. Ask the receiving federation in writing before the player travels.

  • A valid contract in Russia must be respected: IIHF members must honour contracts with non-member organisations too (section 8.1 as quoted by the KHL).
  • For players under 18 add the minor rules: documents and IIHF transfer for a minor.

Realistic routes and typical profiles

  • EAEU (Kazakhstan, Belarus for Russian citizens): no work permit, clubs know the paperwork; check the league's foreign-player rules (Kazakhstan).
  • A second citizenship or an existing EU residence permit changes the whole picture: tell the club at the start, not at registration.
  • Schengen countries without a blanket ban: possible only with a signed club offer, a national D visa and a work or sport permit; plan months, not weeks (work visa for a hockey player).
  • Junior route (under 18): education-based residence is a separate case and depends on the country; a tryout invitation is not a residence basis.

EHA has no verified case set for this route yet, so we name no players and quote no success rates. Past-season transfers are examples only: the rules for 2026/27 must be checked again.

Before you sign: checklist

  1. Citizenship(s) and passport type (biometric or not).
  2. Current contract: end date, release terms, MHL or NMHL registration.
  3. Written confirmation from the club that it can register a player with this citizenship.
  4. Visa type, who applies, where and how long it takes.
  5. Transfer route: ITC through FHR or non-member procedure; who pays fees.
  6. Dates: transfer calendar 2026/27.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Russian MHL player sign with a Finnish club in 2026/27?

From 16 November 2026 Finland refuses visas to Russian athletes for short-term work unless it is essential to its national interest; a Finnish route is practically closed for most players. Check with Migri and the club.

Is the NMHL transfer different from the MHL?

The NMHL is run by the FHR, an IIHF member, so the ITC procedure applies. The MHL is the KHL's junior league; check with the receiving federation whether the non-member procedure applies.

Are Belarusian players treated like Russian players?

Not everywhere. Latvia accepts long-term visa applications from Belarusians, Finland's new rule targets Russian citizens, Czechia and Lithuania restrict both.

Does EHA guarantee a visa or a club?

No. EHA can assess the level and the route, but visas are decided by states and registration by clubs and federations.
