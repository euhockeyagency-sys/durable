Asia League Ice Hockey: player route and rules

A cautious player guide to Japan's professional route: club field, visa status, COE, transfer and registration checks.

01

League position and verified status

Asia League Ice Hockey 2026/27 is active and remains Japan's main professional club route, separate from JIHF national, university and school competitions. The official 2026/27 season outline confirms six teams: Red Eagles Hokkaido, Tohoku Free Blades, H.C. Tochigi Nikko Icebucks, Yokohama GRITS, Stars Kobe and HL Anyang from South Korea.

02

2026/27 format and player level

The regular season runs from 12 September 2026 to 14 March 2027. Six teams play an eight-round round robin for 40 games per team and 120 games in total. The league states that the playoff format will be updated once finalised.

Clubs assess previous league, skating, decision speed, physical readiness, play away from the puck, role, special teams, current video and verifiable statistics — and clarify whether the offer is a professional contract, an education-based route or a tryout.

03

International players and registration

The official 2026/27 season outline allows each team to register up to three foreign-national players. Players holding Japanese or Korean nationality are outside that foreign-player quota. The total season roster is capped at 40 registered players, and the deadline for additional player registrations is 31 January 2027. International players must also complete the required international transfer procedure.

04

Entertainer visa and Certificate of Eligibility

Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists professional sportspersons under the possible working status Entertainer. The standard document list includes a passport, application, photograph and Certificate of Eligibility (COE); additional documents may be required. An application without a COE is possible but can take longer and need more evidence — a COE makes processing smoother but does not guarantee visa issuance. The receiving club, Immigration Services Agency or Japanese consulate must confirm the correct status.

05

Contract, pay and housing

There is no single official salary table. Put pay, housing, equipment, insurance, termination, and who pays for the visa, COE and flights in writing, with a bilingual interpretation of the agreement confirmed. See the Japan country guide for the university/high-school Student-status route if education is part of the plan.

06

IIHF transfer and registration

A move from another national federation requires an IIHF international transfer. The receiving team must confirm registration deadlines and eligibility for the exact competition; sporting eligibility does not replace lawful residence or work status.

07

Player application package

Prepare a valid passport and consulate-standard photographs, birth year, position, shot, height and weight, current team and verified statistics, Elite Prospects or federation profile, role-based highlights and a recent full game, plus contract status and citizenship.
