How to play pro hockey in Germany: DEL2, Oberliga, import rules and visas

A practical guide for players and families, from choosing a realistic level to contracts, registration, international transfers and the lawful right to play and work.

Who Germany suits: EU/EEA players looking for a deep senior pyramid from the DEL through DEL2 and the Oberliga, and juniors who fit the U20 DNL or U17 levels. For non-EU players the special professional-athlete route requires a gross annual salary of at least €50,700 in 2026, which many Oberliga and regional offers do not reach; that, not the sporting level, is often the deciding filter. Imports in short: DEL2 — at most 4 foreign players per game; Oberliga — 3 per game according to press reports. An EU passport does not count as German in DEL2.

DEL2, Oberliga or Regionalliga: which level to aim for

Short answer: DEL2 is a professional league with four foreign places per game, so it suits a player who is already a top-six forward, top-four defender or starting goaltender at a comparable level. The Oberliga has more teams (26) and more varied needs, but it is reported at three imports per game, and many offers do not reach the €50,700 salary that the German athlete visa requires for non-EU players. Regionalliga and the regional leagues below are organised by the regional associations; they can give an EU player or a player with German status ice time, but EHA has not checked their 2026/27 import rules.

Routes by player type

  • EU/EEA import with a recent senior season: Oberliga first; DEL2 if you already play a top role in a comparable second division. In DEL2 your EU passport still counts as foreign.
  • Non-EU player: start from the salary filter. If the realistic offer is below €50,700 gross a year, the Oberliga route is usually closed whatever the sporting level; look at DEL2 or at other countries in the top 10 leagues for imports.
  • Player with German citizenship or German training: you do not use an import place; compare ice time in DEL2, Oberliga and the U20 DNL route.
  • Junior leaving the U20 DNL: Oberliga clubs often use young players with a development licence; ask which team you would actually dress for.

01

How German hockey is structured

The German hockey championship is the DEL, the top professional level. Below it are the DEL2 (14 clubs in 2026/27, 52 games each) and the Oberliga (26 teams: 12 in the North and 14 in the South).

The senior system centres on the DEL, DEL2 and the North and South divisions of the Oberliga. Regionalliga and other competitions below them are organised regionally; they are not one nationwide fourth tier with identical rules.

A move should be assessed by actual role, pace, training load, club budget, contract status and the player’s eligibility, not only by the division name.

03

Junior route: U20 DNL and U17

The national development system includes U20 DNL Division I, Division II and Division III North/South. U17 has Division I and II with northern and southern groups. DEB published the 2026/27 Division I schedules on 29 July 2026, with the new junior season beginning at the end of August. Divisional composition and age eligibility remain season-specific.

For an international junior, the club should confirm the team, expected ice time, education, a guardian where required, housing, insurance, transfer and registration. A tryout is not a roster guarantee.

04

Choosing a realistic level

Clubs compare the previous league, pace of decision-making, physical readiness, production in role context, play away from the puck, special teams and video quality. A country name or high point total in a weaker competition does not prove fit by itself.

DEL and DEL2 usually require a ready professional profile. Oberliga and regional competitions offer a wider range of needs, but role, budget and lawful registration are still case-specific.

05

Club requirements and import status

Prepare position and shot, date of birth, height and weight, citizenship, contract status, current league, statistics, role, video and exact availability. Goalies benefit from full games and core numbers with team context.

Important: each league has its own import rule. DEL2 allows at most four foreign players per game and six per club and season, and counts anyone without German citizenship as foreign (Spielordnung, September 2026). 06

German hockey salaries: DEL2, Oberliga and Regionalliga

There is no official universal salary table for German hockey leagues. DEL2 is professional, while Oberliga and Regionalliga packages can vary widely by club, role, age, experience and budget. EHA therefore does not present unverified ranges as guaranteed earnings.

Separate every component of the written offer:

  • gross salary, estimated net salary, currency, payment schedule, bonuses and taxes;
  • housing: exact property, monthly value, deposit, utilities and roommates;
  • food, local transport, flights, equipment and sticks;
  • medical care, health and accident insurance;
  • whether outside employment is expected and legally permitted;
  • trial period, injury pay, termination, leave and national-team release;
  • who pays transfer, registration, visa, translation and banking fees.

Compare the amount left after tax, rent, insurance and essential costs. A housing-inclusive package can be more valuable than a larger cash figure, but only when the benefit and responsibility for it are written into the contract.

Salary and immigration are separate checks: a club offer can be realistic at its league level but still fail to meet the special professional-athlete visa threshold.
07

Housing and cost of living

Costs vary materially by city and housing type, so one universal figure would be misleading. Ask for the full address, housing type, roommates, monthly rent, deposit, utilities, furniture, distance to the rink and transport.

Compare the amount left after rent, tax, insurance, food and travel, not salary alone. If the club only “helps find housing,” establish who signs the lease and carries the deposit risk.

08

EU/EEA and non-EU players

EU/EEA citizens generally do not need a work visa for employment in Germany, although registration, tax and social-security formalities remain. A third-country national needs a lawful basis for entry, residence and the proposed sporting employment before starting the activity.

For the special professional-athlete route, the federal portal lists four conditions for 2026: minimum age 16; work for a German club or sporting institution participating in competitive sport; at least €50,700 gross annual salary; and confirmation by the competent national governing body in agreement with the DOSB.

€50,700 per year equals €4,225 gross per month. This is an immigration threshold for the special route, not a published salary standard for DEL2, Oberliga or Regionalliga. A semi-professional offer may therefore be commercially genuine but still not qualify under this route.

Do not confuse routes: never assume that an amateur agreement, housing benefit or job outside hockey solves the immigration requirement. The club and competent authority must confirm the lawful route before travel or work begins.

This is general information, not legal advice. Requirements and thresholds should be checked again immediately before filing.

09

IIHF transfer, DEB registration and insurance

  1. International move: national associations process it through the IIHF transfer system.
  2. DEB: the federation explains that international clearance is required when moving from another association and in specific cases for players born abroad, regardless of citizenship.
  3. Term: a transfer can be requested as limited or unlimited; a multi-season request requires supporting documentation.
  4. Registration: the club checks the roster application, age, competition and deadlines before the first game.
  5. Insurance: confirm coverage for training, games, injuries, treatment and the tryout period in writing.
10

Player documents

  • valid passport and proof of citizenship;
  • contract or official offer identifying the club;
  • last registration and information required for the international transfer;
  • insurance and medical documents requested by the club;
  • for non-EU players, visa or residence documents for the employment purpose;
  • for minors, consents, guardian arrangements, education and housing confirmation.

The final list comes from the club together with DEB, the competition and immigration authority. Do not book travel before the team, purpose of entry and responsible contacts are confirmed.

11

Recruitment timing and tryouts

Most roster building takes place in spring and summer, while individual needs can emerge later because of injury, budget or roster changes. Contact clubs selectively and explain why the profile fits that team and role.

Before a tryout, confirm dates, number of sessions and games, travel and housing costs, insurance, responsible contact, decision criteria and response deadline. A tryout should not disguise a paid trip without a genuine sporting evaluation.

12

Resume and video for a German club

One compact package should include a hockey resume, Elite Prospects, verifiable statistics, three to five minutes of recent highlights and a full-game or full-shift link. Identify the jersey number and sequence clearly without using editing to hide the original game quality.

State the realistic target league, citizenship, contract status and availability. Also review the EHA video standard and guide to agent work in Germany.

What are the main senior leagues?

DEL, DEL2 and Oberliga North/South. Regional competitions below them have separate rules.

What is a typical German Oberliga hockey salary?

There is no official league-wide salary table. Compare the written cash salary, tax, housing, insurance, equipment, transport and any expected outside employment in the individual offer.

Is an international transfer required?

Yes when moving between national associations. The club and DEB confirm the exact procedure.

Does an EU passport guarantee a place?

No. It can simplify administration but does not replace the sporting evaluation.

Can a non-EU player use the special route?

Only when the official conditions, including professional income and federation confirmation, are met.

What should be verified in an offer?

The team, league, signatory, pay, housing, insurance, transfer, registration, immigration route and termination terms.
