Alps Hockey League vs Oberliga: which to choose in 2026/27

Two leagues often offered to the same player: the cross-border Alps Hockey League and Germany's third-level Oberliga, compared point by point.

Side by side · Which one for whom · Offer checklist · Next step

Quick answer

Both are realistic first senior steps in Central Europe, but they reward different players. The Alps Hockey League counts imports in points, and young non-domestic players are cheaper: 1 point if born 2007 or later, 2 if born 2005–2006, 4 for a senior import, maximum 16 per game sheet in 2026/27. The Oberliga has 26 German clubs and, by press reports, three import places per game. For a non-EU player the German athlete route requires at least €50,700 gross a year in 2026, which many Oberliga offers do not reach.

Which one for which player

  • U20/U22 import without an EU passport: Alps Hockey League — the age points help and the German salary threshold does not apply.
  • EU senior player with a defined role: both; compare ice time and the full package.
  • Non-EU senior player: Alps Hockey League, unless the Oberliga offer reaches €50,700 gross a year.
  • Player with German status: Oberliga — you do not take an import place.

How age, current league, role and goal change the choice

  • Age: the younger the non-domestic player, the cheaper he is under the Alps Hockey League points system; in the Oberliga the over-23 cap works the other way for older players.
  • Current league: a player coming from a top European U20 league or North American junior hockey usually needs a first senior season with ice time; a player already in a senior league compares the role and level he would have in each.
  • Role: both leagues sign imports for a clear job — scoring, defending, special teams. A depth role is harder to justify with a limited import count.
  • Goal: development and ice time point to the place with the larger role; a German work permit points to an Oberliga offer only if it meets the €50,700 threshold.

What an offer usually consists of — checklist

In both leagues the money is often only part of the deal. Ask the club to put each line in writing:

  • salary: gross or net, monthly or per season, how many months;
  • housing: flat or shared, who pays rent and utilities;
  • car or transport, fuel;
  • meals;
  • health and accident insurance;
  • flights at the start and the end of the season;
  • bonuses and what happens if the season ends early;
  • for non-EU players: who applies for the permit and who pays the fees.

Frequently asked questions

Does age matter in the Oberliga?

Yes. Press reports describe a cap on over-23 players per game: 13 in 2025/26 and 12 from 2027/28; the 2026/27 figure was not confirmed in the DEB regulation, so ask the club.

Which league has more room for young imports?

The Alps Hockey League: under its 2026/27 rules a non-domestic player born in 2007 or later counts 1 point of the 16 allowed per game sheet.

Can a non-EU player join an Oberliga club?

Only if the club can use the German professional-athlete route: at least €50,700 gross a year in 2026, age 16+ and confirmation by the governing body, among other conditions.

Which league pays more?

There is no reliable published sample for the Oberliga. EHA's market estimate for the Alps Hockey League is about €15,000–45,000 per season; compare the full package, not only the salary.

When must a transfer be completed?

In the Alps Hockey League non-domestic players can change clubs inside the league until 30 December; international transfers for adults close on 15 February 2027 under IIHF rules. A separate Oberliga deadline was not confirmed — ask the club.
