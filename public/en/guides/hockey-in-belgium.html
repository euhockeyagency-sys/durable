Hockey in Belgium: leagues, work permits and transfers

A cautious guide for players and families: a top league currently being restructured, domestic divisions, regionalised work permits, IIHF transfer and tryouts.

01

How hockey in Belgium is structured

For 2026/27, the Royal Belgian Ice Hockey Federation (RBIHF) confirms senior competitions from Division 1 through Division 5, youth competitions at U8, U10, U12, U14, U16 and U18, and a new U23 competition intended to bridge youth and senior hockey. Division 1 and Division 2 are the two senior contact competitions; Divisions 3 and 4 remain recreational non-contact leagues, while Division 5 is used as a flexible friendly-games competition.

Regional top-level context: the former BeNe League is now branded as the Central European Hockey League (CEHL). The official CEHL site confirms that EHC Neuwied left for the German Oberliga before 2026/27 and states that a revised competition setup would follow. Because no single official public notice reviewed here gives a final 2026/27 Belgian-club format, verify the exact CEHL participation and format directly before relying on an older structure.

The federation's live league page now shows active 2026/27 fixtures across Belgian competitions, but exact team lists should still be taken from the current RBIHF league view rather than a historic season article.

03

Youth hockey

RBIHF confirms U8, U10 and U12 development competitions plus U14, U16 and U18 championships for 2026/27, together with the new U23 bridge competition. For an international junior, ask the club to confirm the exact team, role, education, housing, guardian arrangements where required, insurance and international transfer in writing.

04

Choosing a realistic level

Clubs assess previous league strength, skating, decision speed, physical readiness, play away from the puck, role, special teams, recent video and verifiable statistics. Given the current restructuring, also ask which specific competition — CEHL or a domestic division — the role is for, since a "Belgian club" offer can mean very different levels right now.

05

Import limits

No current numeric foreign-player limit was confirmed from an official RBIHF or CEHL regulation in the sources reviewed. Do not rely on historical import numbers or secondary reporting for 2026/27.

Before accepting: ask the club to show the written 2026/27 roster and registration rule for the exact competition it is offering you.
06

Contract, salary and conditions

No official hockey salary table was found for Belgium. Put the following in writing before signing:

  • gross or net amount, currency, payment dates, bonuses and taxes;
  • housing, deposit, utilities, meals and transport;
  • equipment, sticks, medical care and insurance;
  • tryout, injury, termination and national-team release terms;
  • who pays for the work permit, IIHF transfer and federation fees.

Living costs: there is no single official monthly “hockey player budget” for Belgium. Housing, utilities, transport, insurance and food vary by city and by the club package. Get every benefit and player-paid cost in writing before comparing an offer.

07

EU/EEA/Swiss and non-EU players: a regionalised system

EU, EEA and Swiss nationals are exempt from work authorisation under the official Brussels guidance. For third-country nationals, Belgium uses a regionalised employment system: the competent region depends on the principal place of work, while residence and work are combined through the Single Permit for employment longer than 90 days.

  • Flanders: a dedicated professional-athlete route; the 2026 minimum salary threshold is €52,083.50/year.
  • Brussels: non-European workers generally need work authorisation; up to 90 days uses a regional work-authorisation route, while work over 90 days uses the Single Permit. Since 4 May 2026, applications are submitted through the federal One-Stop counter.
  • Wallonia: uses its own regional work-permit service; the correct category and documents must be confirmed with the Walloon authority for the player's contract and duration.

For third-country nationals working more than 90 days, the federal Immigration Office confirms the Single Permit: the employer applies through the competent region, and the region and Immigration Office jointly decide the work and residence parts. Confirm the correct region and current salary threshold before signing.

08

IIHF transfer, federation registration and insurance

  1. International transfer: a move from another national federation requires an IIHF international transfer.
  2. Registration: RBIHF set 1 August 2026 as the club participation confirmation deadline for 2026/27; player registration deadlines must be confirmed for the exact competition.
  3. Work authorisation: sporting eligibility does not replace the regional work-permit process for a non-EU player.
  4. Insurance: confirm cover for tryouts, training, games, injury and treatment.
09

Player documents

  • valid passport;
  • signed agreement or official club letter;
  • EU residence registration, or Single Permit / regional work-permit documents for a non-EU player;
  • last hockey registration and information for the IIHF transfer;
  • medical records and insurance;
  • for a minor: consent, guardian, school and housing arrangements.
10

Recruitment timing and tryouts

RBIHF required clubs to confirm 2026/27 competition participation by 1 August 2026, but this is not a universal player-recruitment deadline. Before a tryout, confirm dates, format, costs, insurance, housing, contact person, decision criteria and response deadline. Use the EHA tryout checklist.

Is the BeNe League still called that?

No. It was renamed the Central European Hockey League (CEHL) for the 2024/25 season after German clubs joined as full members. As of mid-2026 several clubs have since left CEHL, and Belgium's top-flight structure for 2026/27 was still being finalised — verify the current name and format directly with the Belgian federation before acting.

How many import players can a Belgian club use?

Not officially confirmed. Secondary reporting describes the cap being cut from four imports per squad to one for 2026/27, with some previously exempt Dutch players reclassified as imports — but this must be verified directly with the league before relying on it.

Do EU citizens need a work permit in Belgium?

No, EU/EEA/Swiss citizens have standard free-movement rights. Non-EU players need a Single Permit, applied for through the region where the employer is based — Flanders, Brussels or Wallonia each run their own process.

Is an IIHF international transfer required?

Yes, when a player moves from a club or federation in another country.
