How a hockey agent works

Hockey agents are surrounded by myths: either the agent is expected to do everything for the player, or agents are described as people who only collect fees. The reality is more practical. A professional agent provides market access, preparation, communication and honest assessment — not a guaranteed contract.

What an agent actually does

  • Assesses the player’s level. Statistics, current league, video, age, passport and position are evaluated together. The result should be a realistic range of leagues, not “we will try everywhere.” See our guide to choosing the right level.
  • Prepares the presentation. This includes a concise hockey resume, relevant video and positioning for a specific market.
  • Tracks club needs. Clubs recruit by role, timing, budget and registration status. The representative’s job is to understand where the player genuinely fits.
  • Presents the player selectively. A credible introduction to a suitable coach or sporting director is more useful than an untargeted mass email.
  • Supports discussions. Contract terms, housing, insurance, bonuses, termination provisions and documentation must be clarified before the player commits.

What an agent cannot do

  • Guarantee a contract. The club always makes the final decision.
  • Place a player above the demonstrated level. Clubs review statistics and video independently, and an unrealistic submission damages everyone’s credibility.
  • Replace performance. Representation can help select a sensible restart market, but it cannot erase a weak season or missing game footage.

How fees may be structured

Representation terms vary by country, league, contract and the scope of work. Some agreements use a percentage of the player’s contract; individual services such as profile assessment, CV preparation, video review or market consultation may use a fixed fee. The exact amount, payment trigger and included work should be written down before the service begins.

A serious warning sign is a large advance payment for “finding a club” before the representative has reviewed the player and explained a credible plan. Players and parents should ask what will be delivered, when the work starts and what happens if no club is interested.

Warning signs when choosing an agent

  • A contract is “guaranteed” before the agent has watched the player.
  • A top league is promised despite a profile from a substantially lower level.
  • A paid tryout is presented as selective when anyone can join by paying.
  • The representative cannot explain the markets, process or recent work in a verifiable way.
  • There is no critical feedback — only praise followed by a payment request.

A professional assessment is sometimes uncomfortable. It should identify the current level, suitable leagues, weak points and the next measurable step. Sometimes the honest answer is that there is no realistic offer today and the player needs a development plan for the next season.

How EHA starts the process

EHA begins with an initial profile assessment. The player submits Elite Prospects, recent statistics and video; we explain which markets appear realistic and what should be improved. If the profile is suitable, the next stage is presentation preparation and targeted work with relevant clubs. We do not promise a contract.
