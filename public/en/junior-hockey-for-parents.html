1. Choose the route

Start with the player's birth year, position, current league, role, recent video, citizenship and objective. Then decide what you are comparing: a regular club, academy, school programme or a tryout route. League names alone do not show whether the environment fits the player.

Use the junior leagues reference and country guides to understand age categories and the structure of each market before contacting programmes. If the United States and an NCAA pathway are under consideration, review the NCDC player and parent guide for current level, age, registration, costs and eligibility checks.

2. Evaluate the conditions

For a minor, the hockey level is only one part of the decision. Ask for the school name and timetable, accommodation model, responsible adult, transport, insurance arrangements and the complete family budget. Confirm exactly what the programme provides and what the family pays separately.

EHA boundary: EHA can assess the sporting route and help identify questions to verify. School admission, accommodation terms, insurance, visa, residence and registration conditions must be confirmed with the relevant organisation or authority.

3. Verify an offer before committing

  • confirm the exact team, age group, coach and expected role;
  • identify whether the document is a tryout invitation, conditional offer or confirmed place;
  • get school, housing, costs, benefits and refund terms in writing;
  • confirm who handles federation registration and the IIHF transfer;
  • verify the legal entity receiving any payment and the responsible adult for the player;
  • do not treat verbal promises as confirmed conditions.

For a broader checklist, read How to verify a hockey club offer.

4. Prepare the move

The exact document list depends on country, age and programme. Common items can include passport, parental consent, school records, insurance information, accommodation confirmation and transfer documents. Use the documents guide and IIHF transfer guide, then confirm current requirements with official sources.

Red flags

Pressure to pay for a guaranteed roster place, refusal to name the team or responsible adult, unclear housing, no written education plan, travel requested before documents are checked, or terms that change after payment all require additional verification.

5. Get an EHA assessment

The free first review is an initial sporting assessment. Send the player's age, position, citizenship, current team or league, Elite Prospects when available, recent video and the family's objective. EHA can identify a realistic market direction and the next step to investigate.

It is not a guarantee of selection, school admission, housing, visa, registration, transfer approval or a contract. If a deeper video review is useful, the separate full player analysis remains an optional next step rather than a required part of the process.

Frequently asked by parents

Can my child play junior hockey in Europe?

Possibly, but the realistic route depends on birth year, current level and role, recent full-game evidence, citizenship, school requirements and the rules of the target country and competition.

What can EHA assess for a junior player?

EHA can review the player's sporting profile, likely market direction and the questions a family should verify. School, accommodation, visa, registration and other official conditions must be confirmed with the relevant club, school, federation or authority.

Does an invitation mean the player has a roster place?

No. A tryout, camp invitation, conditional offer and confirmed roster place are different stages and should be verified separately.

What should parents verify before paying?

Verify the team, responsible adults, sporting role, school and housing plan, all costs and benefits, refund terms, registration and transfer responsibilities, and the legal entity receiving payment.

How much does junior hockey in Europe cost?

There is no single price. Ask for a written total covering programme fees, school, housing, meals, transport, insurance, equipment, travel, registration and transfer costs, plus refund terms.

Is this legal or immigration advice?

No. Requirements change by country and season. Official conditions must be verified with the relevant authorities and organisations.
