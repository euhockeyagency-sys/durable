# Belarus hockey fact-check

Checked 10 August 2026 against available sources. This is an editorial record, not legal advice. iihf.com blocked automated fetching directly during this review (HTTP 403 on all direct attempts) — findings below rely on IIHF's own headlines/URLs as indexed via search, plus secondary reporting that quotes IIHF statements. A human should open the IIHF URLs directly in a browser to confirm exact wording before treating anything below as a direct quote.

## Verified points

- The Belarusian Ice Hockey Association (Федерация хоккея Беларуси) has been an IIHF member federation since 1992 (secondary source: Wikipedia summary, not independently confirmed on iihf.com directly).
- On 28 February 2022, the IIHF Council suspended Russia's and Belarus's national and club teams "from IIHF competitions until further notice" and stripped hosting rights (the 2023 World Junior Championship was moved). Source: iihf.com/en/news/32301, corroborated by SI.com and TheIHLC.
- In February 2025, the IIHF Council reaffirmed that reincorporation was "not yet safe" for the 2025/26 season. Source: iihf.com/en/news/65675.
- On 23 January 2026, the Belarusian Ice Hockey Federation publicly called the extended ban "unjustified, unfair, illegal" via a Belarusian state-adjacent press release (belarus.by) — this release itself describes the ban as covering "national and club teams... participating in international competitions," with no mention of the transfer mechanism specifically.
- At its Congress on 25 May 2026, the IIHF reinstated Belarus for three specific lower-tier events only: Men's U18 top division, Women's Division IV, and Women's U18 Division IIIB. Source: iihf.com/en/news/73721 and /75410, corroborated by Pro Hockey Rumors and Daily Faceoff.
- On 30–31 July 2026, the IIHF confirmed the senior Men's World Championship and World Junior Championship remain closed to Belarus (and Russia) for 2026/27. Source: The Star/Reuters, RMNB (secondary reporting, IIHF's own release not directly fetchable).
- **The precise scope of the suspension with respect to the international transfer (ITC) mechanism specifically is not confirmed.** No primary source found uses the words "international transfer" in connection with the ban — every description found frames it as covering "competitions" and "hosting rights."
- Circumstantial evidence the standard transfer pathway is not fully functioning for Belarus: the Russian club Slavutich (Smolensk) has competed directly in Belarus's own domestic Extraliga since 2024/25, reaching the 2025/26 final. Source: belarushockey.com, 24 April 2026. Both Russia and Belarus are IIHF-suspended, so this cross-border participation is very likely arranged outside the standard IIHF-sanctioned framework — but this is an inference from circumstantial evidence, not a confirmed mechanism.
- No evidence was found of any Western (non-CIS) import player joining a Belarusian club during the 2022–2026 suspension period. Absence of evidence is not proof of absence, but no case could be confirmed either way.
- Top domestic league: currently sponsor-branded the "Betera-Extraliga." 14 clubs completed the 2025/26 season; champion Metallurg Zhlobin, runner-up Slavutich (Smolensk, Russia), third Yunost Minsk. Source: belarushockey.com, 24 April 2026 — a Belarusian hockey news site, not confirmed as the federation's own official site. The federation's presumed official site, hockey.by, returned a connection refused error on every attempt during this review and could not be verified directly.
- General immigration route for a foreign national in Belarus (non-government sources only — official mfa.gov.by was unreachable, connection refused, during this review): a long-term Type D visa combined with an employer-sponsored work permit; processing reported at roughly 3–6 weeks; permits typically valid up to one year, renewable. A visa category covering sport/cultural/scientific/educational/internship purposes is reportedly available. Sources: Rivermate, residencepermit.by — both private immigration-consulting sites, not government sources.
- EU sanctions against Belarus were expanded on 23 April 2026 in parallel with a new Russia sanctions package (trade/export/transit restrictions, not sport-specific). Sources: Mayer Brown, Consilium (the Consilium page itself returned 403 to direct fetch and should be re-verified manually).
- Dmitry Lukashenko (the Belarusian president's son) chairs the "Presidential Sports Club," which holds significant influence over Belarusian sport funding, and is himself a hockey player. Both Dmitry Lukashenko and the Presidential Sports Club are separately sanctioned individually/as an entity by the EU (since 6 November 2020), US OFAC (SDN list, 2 December 2021), and Canada. No EU/US/UK sanction was found specifically naming the Belarusian Ice Hockey Association or Betera-Extraliga clubs themselves — this is a business due-diligence flag for EHA given how closely state sports funding and this sanctioned entity appear to be connected, not a claim that the federation or league itself is sanctioned.

## Publication note

This is the most editorially sensitive country guide published on this site to date. The single most important fact for a family reading this page is that the practical mechanism for a legitimate international transfer into Belarusian domestic hockey is not confirmed by any primary source found — the page must not imply this is a normal, routine market like the site's other country guides. Do not state or imply a specific import quota, salary range, or transfer timeline as settled fact anywhere on the page.
