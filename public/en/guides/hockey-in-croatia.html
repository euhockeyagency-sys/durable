Hockey in Croatia: leagues, EU work rules and transfers

A cautious guide for players and families: the domestic championship and the two regional leagues Croatian clubs actually play in, EU/non-EU work rules, IIHF transfer and tryouts.

01

How hockey in Croatia is structured

The Croatian Ice Hockey Federation (Hrvatski savez hokeja na ledu), an IIHF member since 1992, runs a domestic senior championship, junior and women's competitions. Croatia's four clubs for 2025/26 — KHL Sisak, Medveščak Admiral Zagreb, KHL Zagreb and Mladost Zagreb — are not confined to one league: several also play in cross-border regional competitions, which for a foreign player is often the more relevant route than the purely domestic table.

This is a market defined by its regional connections rather than an isolated domestic pyramid. Always confirm which specific competition a club is registered for this season before assessing the level.

02

Domestic championship and two regional leagues

Alps Hockey LeagueAustria, Italy, Slovenia, Croatia — KHL Sisak plays here
International Hockey League (IHL)regional league with Slovenian and Serbian clubs — Medveščak, Mladost and Zagreb play here
Prvenstvo HrvatskeCroatia's own domestic championship

No Croatian club currently plays in Austria's multinational ICE Hockey League: Medveščak Zagreb competed there for several seasons but withdrew after 2018/19 citing financial difficulties, and the 2025/26 ICE Hockey League field has no Croatian entrant. Do not assume a Croatian club plays at that level without checking the current season.

03

Youth hockey

The federation runs junior and age-group categories alongside the senior game; recent federation news names youth and women's champions by club, but a detailed breakdown of every age group's current activity was not independently confirmed. Check with the federation before treating any specific youth category as an active, regularly competing group.

For an international junior, ask the club to confirm the team, role, education, housing, guardian arrangements where required, insurance and international transfer in writing.

04

Choosing a realistic level

Clubs assess previous league strength, skating, decision speed, physical readiness, play away from the puck, role, special teams, recent video and verifiable statistics. Given the split structure, ask specifically which competition — Alps Hockey League, IHL, or the purely domestic championship — the role is actually for, since the three differ meaningfully in level.

05

Import limits

Secondary reporting describes the Alps Hockey League using a roster points system (a total points budget per squad, with imports costing more points than domestically trained or younger players), but this could not be confirmed against the league's own published regulations. No import limit could be confirmed at all for the purely domestic Croatian championship or the regional IHL.

Before accepting: ask the club for the current roster rule in writing for the specific competition it plays in — the three leagues Croatian clubs use are not interchangeable on this point.
06

Contract, salary and conditions

No official salary information is published for Croatian hockey. Medveščak's own financial history — a KHL spell followed by an EBEL withdrawal citing budget difficulties — is a signal that budgets vary sharply by club and era rather than evidence of a current pay level. Put the following in writing before signing:

  • gross or net amount, currency, payment dates, bonuses and taxes;
  • housing, deposit, utilities, meals and transport;
  • equipment, sticks, medical care and insurance;
  • tryout, injury, termination and national-team release terms;
  • who pays for registration, the IIHF transfer and federation fees.
07

EU/EEA and non-EU players

Croatia has been an EU member since 1 July 2013 and joined the Schengen Area on 1 January 2023, so EU/EEA citizens benefit from full free movement without a separate work permit, subject to the usual residence registration. This is one of the few markets on this site where an EU passport gives an unambiguous, current advantage.

A non-EU player needs Croatia's combined stay-and-work permit, issued by the Ministry of the Interior via local police, normally preceded by a labour-market test through the Croatian Employment Service unless an exemption applies (for example an EU Blue Card route). Confirm the current process and timeline directly, since the source consulted did not show a page-level update date.

08

IIHF transfer, federation registration and insurance

  1. International transfer: a move from another national federation requires an IIHF international transfer.
  2. Registration: the club must confirm deadlines and eligibility for the exact competition — domestic championship, IHL or Alps Hockey League.
  3. Work authorisation: sporting eligibility does not replace Croatia's residence and work-permit process for a non-EU player.
  4. Insurance: confirm cover for tryouts, training, games, injury and treatment.
09

Player documents

  • valid passport;
  • signed agreement or official club letter;
  • EU residence registration, or work-permit documents for a non-EU player;
  • last hockey registration and information for the IIHF transfer;
  • medical records and insurance;
  • for a minor: consent, guardian, school and housing arrangements.

The final list is set by the club, federation, IIHF and Croatian state authorities. Do not buy a non-refundable ticket before entry and documentation are confirmed.

10

Recruitment timing and tryouts

No official recruitment calendar specific to Croatian clubs was found. Given the small number of clubs and their regional-league commitments, early contact and clarity about which competition a role belongs to likely matter more than timing a message to a specific window — but this is orientation, not a confirmed rule.

Before a tryout, confirm dates, format, costs, insurance, housing, contact person, decision criteria and response deadline. Use the EHA tryout checklist.

Do Croatian clubs play in a purely domestic league?

Croatia has a small domestic championship, but its clubs are also active in two regional competitions: the International Hockey League (IHL) with Slovenian and Serbian clubs, and the Alps Hockey League with Austrian, Italian and Slovenian clubs. Confirm with the club which competition it is actually registered for.

Does a Croatian club currently play in Austria's ICE Hockey League?

No. Medveščak Zagreb played there for several seasons but withdrew after 2018/19 citing financial difficulties. The 2025/26 ICE Hockey League field has no Croatian club.

Is there a published import-player limit?

Not confirmed from an official source. Secondary reporting describes a cap-and-points system in the Alps Hockey League, but the exact current rule should be verified with the league before relying on it.

Does EU citizenship help a player working in Croatia?

Yes. Croatia is an EU member and, since 2023, a full Schengen member. EU/EEA citizens benefit from free movement; non-EU players need the standard combined stay-and-work permit.
