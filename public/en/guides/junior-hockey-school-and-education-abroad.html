School and education for a young hockey player abroad

A place in a hockey school and permission to live and study in the country are two separate things. Here is what has been confirmed for a foreign minor, what has not, and which school records to keep from the start.

The principle: a school place is not a permit

When a hockey school or club offers a place, that is only one check. The country's migration authority separately decides whether a minor may be granted a residence permit or visa for school, and under what conditions. These are different parties with different rules, so confirm both in writing before paying or travelling. This page covers the countries where official sources could be read; for the rest it says what is unconfirmed.

Hockey-school structures, briefly

EHA's country records describe how each system works: Sweden's NIU and LIP upper-secondary programmes, Finland's urheilulukio network of about 15 schools, Czechia's ad hoc club partnerships (it has no systemic scheme), Slovakia's CTM and ÚTM sport tracks with school dormitories, Germany's Eliteschule des Sports and Bavaria's own programme, and Switzerland's club-school partnerships such as EVZ Academy. The route comparison tabulates them. Whether a foreign minor may attend one is a separate question, covered below.

Can a foreign minor attend? What is confirmed

Sweden

Migrationsverket's page on upper secondary studies says a residence permit for upper secondary school can be granted only for an International Baccalaureate (IB) programme or for an international exchange arranged through an organisation that promotes student exchange. It states that a permit cannot be granted for distance learning and that studies must be full time and the applicant finally admitted. For an applicant under 18 it requires the consent of parents or guardians, a birth record naming them, an ID copy for each guardian and a letter describing the accommodation. The listed minimum means were SEK 10,656 a month for applications made in 2026, and an under-18 applicant may rely on the parents' or guardians' accounts. The first application must be made from outside Sweden, and the permit is granted for at most one year.

The page does not mention hockey programmes such as NIU or LIP, so EHA cannot say whether or how a non-EU minor is admitted to one. Ask the school and Migrationsverket, in writing, which route applies. EU and EEA citizens are outside this page and their position has not been researched here.

Finland

Parliament adopted a law on 10 December 2025 under which a third-country national who applies for, or holds, a study-based residence permit for upper secondary school (lukio) must pay an annual tuition fee. The fee is at least the average unit price of upper secondary education, and the same law charges students who study at a distance. Paying is a condition of attending teaching, and paying the provider's fees is also a condition of the study residence permit. The law also provides that third-country nationals staying in Finland to study are not subject to compulsory education. The fee provisions apply to students who start on 1 August 2026 or later.

The government bill's explanation says that under-18s who come to Finland on a student residence permit remain liable, while children living in Finland with family on a family-based permit are not the target. That is the explanation to the bill, not a section of the law. The law's text does not mention sport schools. The amount varies by provider, and published figures differ, so this page gives none. How the migration authority Migri treats an application for a lukio place could not be confirmed. Ask the school and Migri directly.

Czechia

Czech consular information says a secondary-school student who is not on an exchange programme applies for a long-term visa for study. For a minor it asks for written consent of both parents, translated into Czech and notarised if not signed at the consulate, a Czech-language confirmation of enrolment, proof of accommodation, travel medical insurance, and documents not older than 180 days. These are embassy pages, requirements vary by post and some are dated, so confirm with the consulate responsible for your country of residence. No sport-school-specific visa was found.

Germany, Switzerland and other countries

For a non-EU minor attending a boarding school linked to a club in Germany, the federal migration office BAMF cites the school-attendance residence permit under §16f of the Residence Act, and a notarised consent for a minor travelling without both parents is understood to be needed; EHA's record flags the custodian-designation rule for a second check. For Switzerland, no rules for a minor attending a sports school could be confirmed. Do not rely on a general statement for any country not listed here.

Online school is not a workaround

  • Sweden states that a residence permit cannot be granted for distance learning.
  • Finland's new law charges non-EU students for distance study as well.
  • For the United States, EHA's earlier record notes that an F-1 student visa is tied to a certified school and that fully online study does not qualify. That comes from secondary summaries of US government pages, so confirm it with the school or an immigration adviser.
  • Whether a given online course counts toward NCAA academic requirements was not confirmed; the Eligibility Center decides.

Treat an online school as a possible supplement to a school place that already carries a permit, not as a replacement for it.

Diplomas and NCAA academic eligibility

A player aiming at the NCAA needs school records that the NCAA Eligibility Center will accept. From its international page:

  • Division I requires 16 NCAA-approved core-course credits and a minimum 2.3 core-course GPA. Division II requires 16 credits and a 2.2 GPA. Division III schools set their own academic standards, though the Eligibility Center certifies athletics eligibility.
  • A student with only international credentials does not have to meet the 10/7 progression rule. A student who completed any secondary education at a US school does.
  • The school should submit official transcripts from year nine onward in the native language, with a complete, word-for-word certified English translation, and proof of graduation as defined for the country. The NCAA may ask for a year-eight transcript, a diploma, a grading scale and course dates. Records a student emails personally are not accepted.
  • The requirements are those of the education system the student is completing, not the country where the school is. For example, a French diploma earned abroad uses the French requirements.

How the NCAA credits the upper secondary or sport-school track of a particular European country could not be confirmed for this page: the country-specific profiles were not read. Ask the Eligibility Center before choosing a school for that purpose. For the wider age and eligibility picture see the age pathway, NCAA and CHL guide.

Keep these from the start

  • Official transcripts and certificates, with certified translations ready, and a grading scale.
  • A birth certificate naming the parents, and written parental consent in the form the authority asks for.
  • Written confirmation from the school of admission, the programme and its full-time status.
  • Accommodation documents, since several authorities ask for them for a minor. See the child-safety guide.
  • Bank records or guardians' account statements, dated within the window the authority sets.

The general documents list is in the documents guide.

Questions to ask the school and the club

  1. Which residence permit or visa do you expect for this student, and who applies and when?
  2. Does the programme count as full-time study for that permit, and is a fee a condition of it?
  3. What is the total annual cost, including any tuition fee for foreign students, materials and meals? Add it to the season cost calculator.
  4. What happens to money paid if the permit is refused or the player leaves?
  5. Who is the responsible adult for school attendance and homework?
  6. Will the school provide the records an NCAA path would need?

FAQ

Does admission to a hockey school give my child the right to live in the country?

No. Admission and immigration permission are separate checks. Each country decides who may get a residence permit or visa for school, and the conditions differ. Confirm both with the school and the migration authority before paying or travelling.

Can my child do online school while playing abroad?

Do not assume so. Sweden states that a residence permit cannot be granted for distance learning, and Finland's new law charges non-EU students for distance study too. Online study is not a confirmed basis for a permit in any country researched.

Does the Finnish tuition fee apply to hockey players?

The adopted law applies to third-country nationals who apply for or hold a study-based residence permit for upper secondary school. It does not mention sport schools. The government bill's explanation says under-18s who come on a student permit are liable. Ask the school and Migri how your case is treated.

What school records should we keep for a possible NCAA path?

Official transcripts from year nine onward, proof of graduation and a grading scale. Non-English records need a complete, word-for-word certified English translation, and the school must send them to the NCAA.
