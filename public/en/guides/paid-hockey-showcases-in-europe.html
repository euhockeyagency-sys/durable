Paid tryouts and summer camps in Europe: how to evaluate an offer before paying

What a paid showcase can and cannot do, the questions to ask about the organiser, attendees, cost and refunds, and how to tell a transparent event from a warning sign.

Types of events · Questions before paying · Warning signs · Next step

Short answer

A paid showcase or tryout camp can be worth the money when the organiser is named, the price list is clear, the attending clubs or scouts are confirmed in writing and you know what happens after the event. It is a poor deal — or a warning sign — when the offer promises a contract, a roster place or “scouts from top leagues” without naming them, asks for payment to a private account, or has no refund terms.

Before paying, compare the camp with what the same money buys elsewhere: a full-game video, a season in a league with real ice time, or a direct application to clubs. A club's own tryout for a specific roster place is a different thing from a commercial showcase, and you should know which one you are buying.

Three kinds of paid events

EventWho runs itWhat you can realistically getWhat it is not
Club tryout or camp for its own rosterThe clubA look from the coaches who pick the team; sometimes a contract decisionA place on the roster before the club decides
Commercial showcase or scouting campA private company or agencyGames or practices in front of named attendees; video; feedbackA route that replaces your season and your video

Questions to ask before you pay

About the organiser

  • What is the legal name of the company, where is it registered and who signs the agreement?
  • How long has it run this event, and can it name past participants who agree to be contacted?
  • Is the organiser also an agent who will ask for a representation fee later?

About who attends

  • Which clubs or scouts are confirmed — by name, with their role — and did the club confirm it, not only the organiser?
  • Do attendees have roster needs for your position and level next season?
  • How many players attend, by position and birth year, and how much game time does each get?

About cost and services

  • What exactly the fee includes: ice, games, video, housing, meals, insurance, transport.
  • What costs come on top: flights, equipment, visa, extra nights.
  • Do you receive the full-game video and written feedback, and when?

About refunds

  • What is refunded if you are injured, the event is cancelled or attendees do not come?
  • Is payment made to a company account against an invoice?

Before paying for exposure: is your level right for the clubs that will be watching? Take the EHA Level Calculator: it gives an indicative league range in two minutes. It does not check citizenship, age rules or registration, so treat the result as preliminary.

Compare the cost with the alternatives

Write down the full cost: fee, flights, housing, meals, insurance and days off school or work. Then ask what the same money would buy: a camera and a season of full games, a season in a league where you play a real role, or a structured application to clubs at your level. A showcase helps most when your video already shows a level that the attending clubs need and you simply need to be seen live.

What a refund policy can look like

One published example: a Swedish summer hockey school keeps the SEK 1,500 registration fee for cancellations from 1 March up to a week before the start, keeps SEK 3,000 for cancellations within seven days, and gives no refund once the school has started (organiser ↗). Ask for terms this precise before paying anywhere.

Five questions most families forget

  • What are the selection criteria, and who exactly applies them?
  • Who else attends — numbers by position and birth year?
  • Will we get individual written feedback, and from whom?
  • What is the full cost including flights, nights and equipment?
  • What is refunded if the player is injured on day one?

For players under 18

Ask who supervises the players day and night, where they sleep, which adult is responsible, and what medical consent and insurance apply abroad. A camp abroad is not an international transfer: if a club wants to register a minor later, separate rules apply (documents and IIHF transfer for a minor).

Frequently asked questions

Are paid hockey showcases worth it?

They can be when the organiser, attending clubs and refund terms are clear and your level matches what those clubs need. They rarely help when your video and statistics are not yet at that level.

Is a paid tryout a scam?

Not necessarily. A club's own tryout or a transparent commercial event can be legitimate. Promises of a guaranteed contract, unnamed scouts and payment to private accounts are warning signs.

What should a showcase fee include?

Ask for a price list: ice, games, video, housing, meals, insurance and transport, and what is refunded if the event changes or you are injured.

Does attending a camp guarantee a roster place?

No. Only a signed contract and the club's registration create a roster place.
