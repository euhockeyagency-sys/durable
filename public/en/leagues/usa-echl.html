ECHL: player route and visa rules

A cautious player guide to North America's AA-level professional league: club footprint, competition for roster spots, the P-1 visa requirement and IIHF transfer.

01

League position

The ECHL is a professional league at AA level, sitting below the NHL and AHL in North America's development pyramid, with 30 clubs including one in Canada. Verify the current season's exact club field before acting.

02

Import openness and competition

No formal import quota is published, which is not the same as an open door: competition for roster spots is high, and a league-wide absence of a quota does not confirm any specific club has a realistic opening.

03

What clubs assess

Clubs assess previous league and level, skating, decision speed, physical readiness, role, special-teams value, recent video and verifiable statistics, plus contract status and visa readiness — a player who cannot start on time is a harder sell regardless of level.

04

P-1 visa requirement

A P-1 visa is required for a non-US/Canadian player to play professionally in the ECHL. This is a petition-based process tied to the specific club and contract, not a general work visa — confirm the timeline and documentation with the club and qualified immigration counsel well before a season starts.

Before travelling: a tryout invitation is not visa authorisation to work. Confirm the petition is filed and approved before relying on a start date.
05

Contract and pay

There is no single public salary table; pay, housing, equipment, insurance and termination terms should be confirmed in writing with the specific club. See the United States country guide for how the ECHL compares with NHL/AHL, NCAA and USA Hockey junior routes.

06

IIHF transfer and registration

A move from another national federation requires an IIHF international transfer. The club must confirm registration deadlines and eligibility; sporting eligibility does not itself authorise work or residence.

07

Player application package

Prepare Elite Prospects and verifiable statistics, current team, league, role and availability, recent video and full shifts, citizenship, contract status and visa readiness.

How many clubs play in the ECHL?

30 clubs, including one in Canada. Verify the current season's exact field before acting, since AA-level leagues can add or relocate franchises.

Is there a formal import quota?

No formal import quota is published, but competition for roster spots is high and a P-1 visa is required for non-US/Canadian players.

Is an IIHF transfer required?

Yes, when the player moves from a club or federation in another country.
