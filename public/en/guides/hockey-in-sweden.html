Hockey in Sweden: leagues, pay, permits and tryouts

A practical guide for senior and junior players covering league levels, club requirements, contracts, costs, international transfers and lawful routes for EU/EEA and non-EU players.

01

How Swedish hockey is organised

Sweden has a connected senior, junior and regional competition structure. The top of the men's pyramid consists of the SHL, HockeyAllsvenskan and Hockeyettan. Regional levels, including HockeyTvåan and lower divisions, sit below them. The principal development route runs through U18 and U20 Nationell, regional junior competitions and then senior hockey.

A league name alone does not determine a player's opportunity. Clubs assess previous competition level, pace, role, age, physical readiness, verified statistics, full shifts and legal eligibility to register.

Season-specific verification is essential: participating clubs, formats, age eligibility, transfer dates and lineup rules can change. Use the current SIF regulations for the exact league and season.
02

Senior leagues: SHL, HockeyAllsvenskan and Hockeyettan

SHLtop professional level
HockeyAllsvenskanprofessional second level
Hockeyettannational third level
  • SHL: an elite market with exceptional competition and little tolerance for an unproven level.
  • HockeyAllsvenskan: a strong professional league where a club recruits for a defined role rather than a generic profile.
  • HockeyEttan: a broader market, but pace, physical level, budget and status remain demanding.
  • Regional divisions: they may provide a sporting entry point, but contract, pay and immigration eligibility must be checked separately. The professional-athlete permit route does not automatically cover every lower division.

The Swedish Ice Hockey Association publishes competition regulations and official statistics. Never carry team counts or formats from a previous season without checking.

03

The U18 and U20 junior route

The upper junior levels are U20 Nationell and U18 Nationell, with regional competitions below. Movement between levels depends on selection, age and current regulations. A player should establish which team will register him, where he will train, how many games are realistically available and whether a senior pathway exists.

For context, the official U20 Nationell page for 2025/26 listed 20 teams in two groups and 36 games per team. This demonstrates the scale of the top junior level but does not replace the 2026/27 regulations.

Foreign minors: SIF's 2026/27 rules impose additional conditions on international transfers. A player under 18 needs documented representation in Sweden and access to general education or vocational training; special grounds and transfer limits also apply. A club invitation alone is insufficient.
04

What Swedish clubs check in a foreign player

  • the standard of the previous league and opposition;
  • role: power play, penalty kill, faceoffs, top pair or depth usage;
  • recent full shifts and a full game, not highlights alone;
  • age, development potential and readiness to fill the required role now;
  • citizenship, last registration, contract status and availability;
  • whether the budget, housing and immigration route are workable;
  • for juniors, education, housing, representation and safeguarding.

Saying that a player has “played in Europe” is not evidence of level. The profile should let a coach understand the potential fit within minutes.

05

Salary, housing and contract terms

There is no single official salary table covering Swedish hockey leagues. Pay depends on level, role, age, track record, club budget and the benefits package. EHA therefore does not present unverified ranges as guaranteed earnings.

Separate cash salary from benefits in kind and verify:

  • whether salary is gross or net, the currency, payment dates and payment method;
  • housing: who signs the lease and pays rent, deposit and utilities;
  • meals, local transport, flights, equipment and sticks;
  • health and accident insurance;
  • terms governing injury, early termination, transfer or relegation;
  • whether the agreement is conditional on registration, transfer or a work permit.
Do not confuse two figures: a player's market salary and the immigration minimum are different. Meeting one does not prove that the other is satisfied.
06

Living costs and the player budget

Housing is a major cost and varies considerably by city, apartment size and tenure. Statistics Sweden reported that the average monthly rent for a three-room apartment in 2025 was SEK 9,118. This is a national statistical benchmark, not a quote for a player's housing and not proof that a suitable apartment is available.

Before accepting, build a monthly budget covering net income, rent, deposit, utilities, food, local transport, phone, insurance and trips home. If the club says it will “help with housing,” request the address, photographs, full cost, roommates, contract period and who is responsible for the deposit.

07

EU/EEA and non-EU residence and work

Citizenship determines the immigration route, not sporting eligibility. EU/EEA citizens generally do not require a Swedish work permit, although right-of-residence and administrative requirements still matter for a longer stay. Passport, contract and the actual purpose of stay must be consistent.

A third-country professional athlete contracted by a Swedish club normally needs a permit before entering Sweden and must await the decision outside Sweden. The Swedish Migration Agency identifies a written contract, valid passport, required salary, health and accident insurance, the club's place within the sports system and certification of sporting need among the conditions.

For men's ice hockey, the official professional-athlete route described by the Agency applies to the SHL, HockeyAllsvenskan and Hockeyettan. An offer from another division does not automatically qualify for this permit route.

From 1 September 2025, the Agency stated a minimum salary of SEK 19,600 per month for professional athletes and coaches. Because the figure is linked to a reviewed benchmark, open the current Agency page before filing and reconfirm both the amount and every condition.

This is general information, not legal advice. Do not start work or plan a move solely on the basis of a club message.

08

IIHF transfer, registration and home-grown status

  1. Identify the last association: the club checks where the player's registration is held.
  2. Complete the international move: a change of national association follows IIHF procedure and SIF requirements.
  3. Confirm eligibility: a transfer does not replace league registration, age eligibility or immigration permission.
  4. Check the dates: SIF rules contain season windows and separate provisions for different competitions.

SIF's 2026/27 rules also use a Swedish home-grown concept: broadly, a player registered with a Swedish club for at least three seasons before becoming a senior; for junior players, the status begins in the fourth registered season. The permitted number of non-home-grown players is set by the applicable series rules. Citizenship and home-grown status are not the same.

09

Complete document package

  • a valid passport and any second passport;
  • a written contract or complete club offer;
  • proof of last registration and information needed for the IIHF transfer;
  • insurance documents covering the intended sporting activity;
  • immigration documents where required;
  • for a minor, parental consents, representation, housing and education plan;
  • hockey resume, Elite Prospects profile, statistics and video links;
  • the club contact responsible for registration and permits.

Before buying tickets, ask the club to confirm in writing which steps it completes, which belong to the player and what timeline is realistic.

10

Tryouts and approach timing

There is no single official tryout window for every league. In practice, rosters begin to form well in advance and timing varies by club and level. April to August is often an important recruitment period, but this is professional observation rather than a federation deadline. In-season openings may follow injuries, budget changes or a poor start.

Before travelling, document the dates, training group, number of ice sessions and games, responsible coach, housing, meals, transport, insurance and decision date. Establish whether “tryout” means an assessment for a real opening or merely a paid camp.

11

Resume, video and first message

The first message should be short and targeted: name, year of birth, position, shot, height/weight, citizenship, current team and league, status, Elite Prospects link, recent highlights and a full game. Add one sentence explaining the role the player can offer that specific team.

Use EHA's separate guides to a hockey resume, club-ready video and finding a club in Europe. Untargeted mass outreach reduces the likelihood of a meaningful reply.

12

How to verify a club offer

  1. Verify the official team name, league and contact person on the club website.
  2. Obtain all financial and living conditions in writing.
  3. Check the sporting role, roster, competition at the position and realistic ice time.
  4. Ask the club to explain transfer, registration and immigration steps.
  5. Do not send money to a private person without a contract, payment purpose and verified recipient.
  6. For a junior, separately verify education, representation, housing safety and adult responsibility.

Use EHA's detailed club-offer checklist. If a club will not answer basic questions, a polished presentation does not remove the risk.

Is an international transfer required?

If the last registration is with another national association, the move normally follows IIHF and SIF procedure. The receiving club confirms the exact steps.

Does every league qualify for a non-EU athlete permit?

No. For men's hockey, the Migration Agency's specialised route identifies SHL, HockeyAllsvenskan and Hockeyettan. Another level must be assessed separately.

What does SEK 19,600 mean?

It is the Agency's published minimum monthly salary from 1 September 2025 for the relevant permit, not an average hockey salary. Recheck the current figure before filing.

Can a player under 18 transfer?

Only if the additional SIF and IIHF requirements are met. Grounds, written representation, education and correct documentation must be verified.

Does an EU passport guarantee a place?

No. It may simplify the immigration route but does not replace selection, transfer and registration.
